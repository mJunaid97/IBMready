# Shoulder Anatomy: record of the checks (3 November 2026)

The shoulder-girdle article was extended to the shoulder joint, rotator cuff, deltoid and upper humerus (batch `shoulder`: `content/drafts/batches/shoulder/`,
with its first-pass ledger, second-pass report and batch report) and retitled *Shoulder Anatomy*.

* First pass (author session): 45 ledger entries, 33 searches in all.
* Second pass (separate subagent, differently worded searches): supported 29, textbook 5, unsupported 11, wrong 0, unsafe 0; all eleven reworded to what the retrieved text says.
* Third check, added before merging because the second pass used only 16 searches for 44 sentences (`third-check.json`: an independent adversarial subagent, 45 searches, all 50 new or changed
  sentences): supported 42, textbook 4, unsupported 1, unsafe 3, wrong 0. Applied before merging:
  * "the main static stabilisers of the joint" (glenohumeral ligaments) became "among the static stabilisers of the joint, along with the labrum, the capsule and the shape of the bones" (StatPearls lists six;
    OpenStax calls ligament support additional but weaker);
  * three urgent-care sentences that named no route (nerve injury after a dislocation, a suspected fracture with numbness or a cold, pale or blue hand, sudden arm weakness after an injury) now say
    A&E, 999, an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 as the NHS pages do, and the arm-weakness sentence adds 999 for a drooping face or slurred speech;
  * two clauses the checker could not tie to a cited page were removed: the OpenStax remark about ligament support and the "painful, stiff and recovering stages" of frozen shoulder (the cited NHS 111 Wales page gives no stages).
* Atlas: the page now highlights the humeri and the four rotator cuff muscles on each side as well as the clavicles and scapulae; the preview image was re-rendered.

For a clinician: the route-to-care wording for fractures and dislocation is the NHS pages' own, summarised by the search tool; the deltoid's nerve supply is stated as the axillary nerve (StatPearls), though one dissection
study reports a lateral pectoral nerve supply to the clavicular part; the long head of biceps is described as a debated stabiliser. The inherited sentences from the October article were not re-checked.
