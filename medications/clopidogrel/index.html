    Clopidogrel

    An antiplatelet medicine that stops platelets clumping by blocking their P2Y12 receptor, taken to prevent clots after a heart attack, stent, stroke or in peripheral artery disease, often alongside aspirin for a defined period.

    Tablets 75 mg once daily; a larger loading dose is given in hospital at the start (summary of the cited reference, not a prescription)

    Antiplatelet effect within hours of a loading dose, or over several days at the usual daily dose; lasts 5-7 days after stopping as new platelets replace the blocked ones.

    What it is used for

    • Preventing further events after a heart attack or acute coronary syndrome, usually with aspirin for up to 12 months
    • After a coronary stent, to stop the stent clotting
    • After an ischaemic stroke or transient ischaemic attack, as long-term single antiplatelet treatment
    • Peripheral arterial disease

    How it works in the body

    Clopidogrel is a prodrug: the liver converts it, largely through the enzyme CYP2C19, into a molecule that binds irreversibly to the P2Y12 receptor on platelets. Without that receptor's signal, platelets cannot be activated by ADP and stick together far less readily. Because the block is permanent for the life of each platelet, the effect lasts about a week after stopping.

    Common side effects

    • Bleeding more easily: nosebleeds, bruising, bleeding gums, longer bleeding from cuts
    • Diarrhoea, stomach pain, indigestion

    Serious: seek help

    • Serious bleeding: blood in urine or stools, black stools, vomiting blood, a bleed after a head injury
    • Signs of an allergic reaction
    • Very rarely, a blood disorder causing fever, bruising and confusion (thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura)

    Cautions and interactions

    • Tell any doctor, dentist or pharmacist that you take it; it is usually paused before surgery only on the advice of the prescriber
    • Omeprazole and esomeprazole reduce its activation; other acid-suppressing medicines are preferred if one is needed
    • Combined with aspirin, warfarin, a DOAC or an NSAID the bleeding risk rises and the combination is decided by the prescriber
    • Some people convert it poorly (CYP2C19 variants) and get less protection

    Other medicines

    Clopidogrel + Omeprazole

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Omeprazole reduces the activation of clopidogrel, which may make it less effective at preventing clots after a stent or heart attack.

    Why it can occur
    CYP inhibition · CYP2C19 inhibition blocks conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite
    What official information says
    UK information lists clopidogrel among medicines that omeprazole can affect; the BNF advises avoiding the combination and using an alternative PPI.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK guidance: avoid; lansoprazole or pantoprazole are preferred alternatives when acid suppression is needed with clopidogrel.
    Basis of the status
    BNF: Omeprazole interactions (reduced antiplatelet effect of clopidogrel; avoid)

    Conditions

    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Peripheral arterial disease A long-term condition in which the arteries that supply blood to the legs become narrowed or blocked.

    Symptoms

    • Chest pain Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen.

    Medical tests

    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.

    Procedures

    • Coronary angioplasty and stenting A balloon and stent passed through a catheter from the wrist or groin reopen a narrowed or blocked coronary artery.

    Medications

    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Apixaban and DOACs A direct oral anticoagulant that blocks clotting factor Xa, preventing strokes in atrial fibrillation and treating deep vein thrombosis and…
    • Warfarin The long-established oral anticoagulant that blocks vitamin K-dependent clotting factors.
    • Fluconazole Fluconazole is an antifungal medicine used for thrush and other fungal infections.

    Drug classes

    • Antiplatelet drugs Medicines that stop platelets clumping into the clots that block arteries.
