    Omeprazole

    Also known as: proton pump inhibitor, PPI, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole, Losec

    A proton pump inhibitor that switches off acid production in the stomach, healing ulcers and reflux oesophagitis and protecting the stomach from anti-inflammatory drugs.

    Open in the 3D explorerStomach anatomy
    Generic name
    Omeprazole
    Drug class
    Proton pump inhibitors
    Also known as
    proton pump inhibitor, PPI, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole, Losec
    Forms
    Capsules and tablets 10, 20, 40 mg once or twice daily; oral suspension; intravenous
    Onset and duration
    Acid suppression within hours; maximal after 3-5 days. Symptom relief in reflux within days.
    Monitoring
    Magnesium and B12 if used for more than a year; kidney function if symptoms of nephritis.

    What it is used for

    • Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease and its complications (oesophagitis, Barrett oesophagus)
    • Healing stomach and duodenal ulcers; part of H. pylori eradication triple therapy
    • Protecting the stomach in people on NSAIDs, aspirin or anticoagulants at ulcer risk
    • Bleeding ulcers (intravenous), Zollinger-Ellison syndrome

    How it works in the body

    Irreversibly blocks the hydrogen-potassium ATPase, the 'proton pump' in the parietal cells of the stomach lining that secretes acid as the final step of every stimulus. Because the pump is disabled rather than the signals, acid falls by over 90%, and new pumps must be made before secretion recovers, so the effect outlasts the drug's short half-life. Given 30-60 minutes before breakfast, when pumps are most active.

    Common side effects

    • Headache, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain
    • Wind, dizziness

    Serious: seek help

    • Low magnesium with long-term use (cramps, arrhythmia)
    • Clostridioides difficile and other gut infections, and pneumonia (acid normally kills swallowed microbes)
    • Reduced absorption of B12, iron and calcium; a modest increase in fractures with prolonged high-dose use
    • Acute interstitial nephritis and, rarely, subacute cutaneous lupus

    Cautions and interactions

    • Use the lowest effective dose and review long-term use; rebound acid hypersecretion for a few weeks after stopping
    • May mask stomach cancer symptoms: alarm features (weight loss, swallowing difficulty, bleeding, age over 55 with new symptoms) need endoscopy first
    • Omeprazole reduces the activation of clopidogrel (pantoprazole preferred); stop two weeks before H. pylori testing
    • Interactions with methotrexate, some HIV drugs
