    Amoxicillin and antibiotics

    Also known as: penicillin, co-amoxiclav, Augmentin, antibiotic, flucloxacillin, doxycycline, nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim

    A widely used penicillin antibiotic that kills bacteria by breaking their cell walls, prescribed for chest, ear, sinus, dental and urinary infections.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy
    Generic name
    Amoxicillin and antibiotics
    Drug class
    Penicillins
    Also known as
    penicillin, co-amoxiclav, Augmentin, antibiotic, flucloxacillin, doxycycline, nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim
    Forms
    Capsules 250 and 500 mg, oral suspension, sachets; intravenous injection; usually three times daily for 5 days
    Onset and duration
    Improvement within 48-72 hours; the full course is decided by the infection, not by symptoms.
    Monitoring
    Clinical response at 48-72 hours; cultures before starting where possible so therapy can be narrowed.

    What it is used for

    • Chest infections and community-acquired pneumonia
    • Ear, sinus and throat infections caused by bacteria
    • Dental infections; H. pylori eradication (with a PPI and clarithromycin)
    • Urinary infections use nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim; skin infections flucloxacillin; atypical pneumonia doxycycline or clarithromycin

    How it works in the body

    Binds the penicillin-binding proteins that bacteria use to cross-link the peptidoglycan of their cell wall. Growing bacteria with a weakened wall burst under their own osmotic pressure. Human cells have no cell wall, which is why penicillins are so selective. Bacteria fight back with beta-lactamase enzymes that destroy the drug; clavulanic acid (in co-amoxiclav) blocks that enzyme. Antibiotics do nothing against viruses, the cause of most sore throats, colds and coughs.

    Common side effects

    • Nausea, diarrhoea, thrush
    • Non-allergic rash (very common in glandular fever, where amoxicillin should be avoided)

    Serious: seek help

    • Anaphylaxis in true penicillin allergy (about 1 in 10,000 courses); most reported 'penicillin allergies' are not true allergy and can be safely delabelled
    • Clostridioides difficile colitis, especially with broad-spectrum agents in hospital
    • Liver injury with co-amoxiclav (cholestatic hepatitis), seizures at very high doses in kidney failure
    • Antimicrobial resistance across the population with every unnecessary course

    Cautions and interactions

    • Take the course as prescribed; do not save leftovers or share
    • Report any previous reaction so an alternative (clarithromycin, doxycycline) can be chosen
    • Doxycycline: sun sensitivity, not for pregnancy or young children; nitrofurantoin: avoid with poor kidney function
    • Warfarin levels may rise with many antibiotics
