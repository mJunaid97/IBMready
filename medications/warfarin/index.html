    Warfarin

    Also known as: coumarin anticoagulant, Coumadin, vitamin K antagonist

    The long-established oral anticoagulant that blocks vitamin K-dependent clotting factors; effective for mechanical heart valves and clots but needs regular INR blood tests and careful dosing.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy
    Generic name
    Warfarin
    Drug class
    Anticoagulants
    Also known as
    coumarin anticoagulant, Coumadin, vitamin K antagonist
    Forms
    Tablets 0.5, 1, 3 and 5 mg (colour-coded), taken once daily at the same time
    Onset and duration
    Therapeutic after 3-5 days; effect persists 3-5 days after stopping. Reversed with vitamin K (slow) or prothrombin complex concentrate (immediate).
    Monitoring
    INR every few days at first, then every 4-12 weeks once stable, and after any new drug or illness; self-testing meters are available.

    What it is used for

    • Mechanical heart valves (the only oral anticoagulant licensed)
    • Atrial fibrillation with moderate or severe mitral stenosis, and antiphospholipid syndrome
    • Venous thromboembolism where DOACs are unsuitable (severe kidney failure, cost)
    • Historically the standard anticoagulant, now largely replaced by DOACs for other indications

    How it works in the body

    Blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase in the liver, so vitamin K cannot be recycled to activate clotting factors II, VII, IX and X. Existing factors take days to be cleared, so the effect starts after 2-3 days and heparin cover is needed for immediate protection. The dose needed varies enormously between people and with diet, illness and other drugs, so the INR blood test tunes it.

    Common side effects

    • Bruising, bleeding gums, nosebleeds
    • Hair thinning, rash

    Serious: seek help

    • Major bleeding, including intracranial: risk rises steeply when the INR is above 4
    • Skin necrosis in the first days (protein C deficiency), purple toe syndrome
    • Fetal harm: teratogenic in the first trimester and bleeding later

    Cautions and interactions

    • Hundreds of interactions: antibiotics, antifungals, amiodarone, NSAIDs, alcohol binges raise the INR; rifampicin, carbamazepine, large amounts of green vegetables lower it
    • Keep vitamin K intake steady rather than avoiding it
    • Carry an anticoagulant alert card; tell dentists and pharmacists
    • Target INR 2-3 for most indications, 2.5-3.5 for some mechanical valves
