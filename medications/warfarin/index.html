    Warfarin

    The long-established oral anticoagulant that blocks vitamin K-dependent clotting factors; effective for mechanical heart valves and clots but needs regular INR blood tests and careful dosing.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomyCheck interactions
    Generic name
    Warfarin
    Active ingredient
    Warfarin (variants: warfarin sodium)
    Product type
    Small-molecule medicine
    Drug class
    Anticoagulants
    Brand names
    UK Marevan · US Coumadin, Jantoven
    Routes
    Oral
    Dosage forms
    Tablet, Suspension
    Forms
    Tablets 0.5, 1, 3 and 5 mg (colour-coded), taken once daily at the same time
    Onset and duration
    Therapeutic after 3-5 days; effect persists 3-5 days after stopping. Reversed with vitamin K (slow) or prothrombin complex concentrate (immediate).
    Terminology
    RxNorm RxCUI 11289 · ATC B01AA03 · FDA EPC Vitamin K Antagonist [EPC]
    Monitoring
    INR every few days at first, then every 4-12 weeks once stable, and after any new drug or illness; self-testing meters are available.

    What it is used for

    • Mechanical heart valves (the only oral anticoagulant licensed)
    • Atrial fibrillation with moderate or severe mitral stenosis, and antiphospholipid syndrome
    • Venous thromboembolism where DOACs are unsuitable (severe kidney failure, cost)
    • Historically the standard anticoagulant, now largely replaced by DOACs for other indications

    How it works in the body

    Blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase in the liver, so vitamin K cannot be recycled to activate clotting factors II, VII, IX and X. Existing factors take days to be cleared, so the effect starts after 2-3 days and heparin cover is needed for immediate protection. The dose needed varies enormously between people and with diet, illness and other drugs, so the INR blood test tunes it.

    Common side effects

    • Bruising, bleeding gums, nosebleeds
    • Hair thinning, rash

    Serious: seek help

    • Major bleeding, including intracranial: risk rises steeply when the INR is above 4
    • Skin necrosis in the first days (protein C deficiency), purple toe syndrome
    • Fetal harm: teratogenic in the first trimester and bleeding later

    Cautions and interactions

    • Hundreds of interactions: antibiotics, antifungals, amiodarone, NSAIDs, alcohol binges raise the INR; rifampicin, carbamazepine, large amounts of green vegetables lower it
    • Keep vitamin K intake steady rather than avoiding it
    • Carry an anticoagulant alert card; tell dentists and pharmacists
    • Target INR 2-3 for most indications, 2.5-3.5 for some mechanical valves

    Other medicines

    Warfarin + Ibuprofen

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Ibuprofen with warfarin increases the risk of serious bleeding, particularly from the stomach, and can raise the INR.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk · Gastric mucosal injury and reversible antiplatelet effect on top of anticoagulation
    What official information says
    UK information for warfarin advises not taking ibuprofen or aspirin unless a doctor has said it is safe; the BNF advises avoiding the combination.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK guidance: avoid; paracetamol is the usual alternative. If unavoidable, INR and bleeding are monitored closely.
    What may be monitored
    INR · Signs of bleeding

    Warfarin + Aspirin

    MajorSpecialist supervision or close monitoring

    Aspirin with warfarin substantially increases bleeding, including gastrointestinal and intracranial bleeding.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information lists blood thinners such as warfarin and apixaban among medicines to tell a doctor about; the BNF advises avoiding the combination unless specifically indicated.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official guidance: combined only for specific indications (for example mechanical valves) under specialist direction, usually with gastroprotection.
    What may be monitored
    INR · Signs of bleeding · Haemoglobin

    Warfarin + Paracetamol

    ModerateMonitoring or dose adjustment advised

    Regular paracetamol (several doses a day for more than a few days) can raise the INR and bleeding risk in people on warfarin; occasional doses do not.

    Why it can occur
    other · Possible interference with vitamin K-dependent clotting factor synthesis
    What official information says
    UK information states occasional paracetamol is fine with warfarin, but regular use can affect the INR and a doctor should be told.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official information: occasional doses are acceptable; the INR is checked more often with regular use.
