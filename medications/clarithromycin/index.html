    Clarithromycin

    A macrolide antibiotic for chest, skin, ear and throat infections and part of the combination that eradicates Helicobacter pylori, notable for the number of medicines it interacts with through CYP3A4.

    What it is used for

    • Chest infections including pneumonia, and exacerbations of COPD
    • Skin and soft-tissue infections, ear, sinus and throat infections, particularly in people allergic to penicillin
    • Helicobacter pylori eradication, with a proton-pump inhibitor and a second antibiotic
    • Some mycobacterial infections under specialist care

    How it works in the body

    Clarithromycin binds the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome and stops the growing protein chain from moving along, so the bacteria cannot make the proteins they need; it slows their growth and, at higher concentrations, kills some species. It concentrates inside cells and lung tissue, which suits it to respiratory infections. In the liver it strongly inhibits CYP3A4, the enzyme that clears many other medicines.

    Common side effects

    • Feeling sick, diarrhoea, stomach pain, bloating
    • A changed sense of taste
    • Headache, difficulty sleeping

    Serious: seek help

    • Severe or persistent diarrhoea (possible C. difficile infection)
    • Palpitations or fainting from a prolonged QT interval
    • Yellowing of the skin or eyes, pale stools, dark urine
    • Severe skin reactions or an allergic reaction
    • Hearing loss or ringing in the ears, usually reversible

    Cautions and interactions

    • Interacts with many medicines through CYP3A4: statins (simvastatin and atorvastatin), warfarin, some calcium-channel blockers, colchicine, some antipsychotics and anticoagulants, among others
    • Not combined with other QT-prolonging medicines where an alternative exists
    • Dose reduced in significant kidney impairment
    • Liver disease and a history of jaundice with a macrolide
    • Myasthenia gravis can worsen

    Other medicines

    Clarithromycin + Atorvastatin

    ModerateMonitoring or dose adjustment advised

    Clarithromycin and erythromycin raise atorvastatin levels several-fold, increasing the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.

    Why it can occur
    CYP inhibition · Strong CYP3A4 inhibition; clarithromycin roughly quadruples atorvastatin exposure
    What official information says
    UK information lists these antibiotics among medicines to tell a doctor about; the BNF advises a reduced atorvastatin dose or avoiding the combination.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK guidance: the atorvastatin dose is limited (or the statin paused) during the course; report muscle pain.
    What may be monitored
    Muscle symptoms · Creatine kinase if symptoms

    Clarithromycin + Amlodipine

    Severity not gradedInteraction documented

    Clarithromycin raises amlodipine levels, increasing dizziness, low blood pressure and ankle swelling.

    Why it can occur
    CYP inhibition · Strong CYP3A4 inhibition (clarithromycin is named in the NHS list of medicines that interact with amlodipine)
    What official information says
    UK information lists these among medicines that can interfere with the way amlodipine works and advises telling a doctor.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Documented in official information; the prescriber may adjust the amlodipine dose or monitor blood pressure during the course.
    What may be monitored
    Blood pressure · Dizziness and swelling
    Basis of the status

    Azithromycin + Macrolides (class)

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combinationtherapeutic duplication

    Two macrolide antibiotics together (azithromycin with clarithromycin or erythromycin) add QT prolongation and gut side effects without benefit.

    Why it can occur
    QT prolongation
    What official information says
    Macrolides share cautions on QT prolongation; guidance never combines them.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    One macrolide at a time.
