    Prednisolone and steroids

    Also known as: steroid tablets

    A corticosteroid tablet that suppresses inflammation and the immune system, used in asthma flare-ups, arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease and many autoimmune conditions.

    What it is used for

    • Asthma attacks and COPD flares (5-day courses)
    • Flares of rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, gout, polymyalgia rheumatica, temporal arteritis
    • Autoimmune disease, severe allergic reactions, some cancers and transplant rejection
    • Replacing cortisol in adrenal insufficiency (hydrocortisone); dexamethasone in brain swelling and severe COVID-19

    How it works in the body

    A synthetic version of cortisol, the adrenal stress hormone, at doses far above what the body makes. It enters cells and binds glucocorticoid receptors that switch off genes for inflammatory cytokines and switch on anti-inflammatory ones, shrinking the whole inflammatory response, whatever its trigger: it does not treat the cause. The same receptors in liver, muscle, bone, skin and brain produce the side effects, and prolonged dosing suppresses the pituitary-adrenal axis so the body's own cortisol production shuts down.

    Common side effects

    • Increased appetite and weight gain, fluid retention, mood change, insomnia
    • Raised blood glucose (new or worsened diabetes), raised blood pressure
    • Indigestion; thin skin, bruising, acne

    Serious: seek help

    • Adrenal suppression: after more than 3 weeks the dose must be tapered, and extra cover is needed for illness and surgery; abrupt stopping can cause an adrenal crisis
    • Osteoporosis and fractures (bone protection with bisphosphonates for long courses), avascular necrosis of the hip
    • Infections (masked and more severe: chickenpox, shingles, TB reactivation), peptic ulcer with NSAIDs, cataract and glaucoma
    • Psychosis, severe mood disturbance; growth suppression in children; muscle wasting

    Cautions and interactions

    • Carry a steroid emergency card on courses longer than 3-4 weeks
    • Never stop long-term steroids suddenly; sick-day doubling rules
    • Check for TB, hepatitis, diabetes and blood pressure before long courses; consider PPI and bone protection
    • Live vaccines avoided on high doses; report exposure to chickenpox if not immune

    Other medicines

    Prednisolone and steroids + Metformin

    Severity not gradedInteraction documented

    Corticosteroids such as prednisolone raise blood glucose and can undo the control metformin provides.

    Why it can occur
    pharmacodynamic antagonism
    What official information says
    UK information lists steroid tablets among medicines that can affect blood sugar control and advises telling a doctor.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Documented; blood glucose is checked more often during a steroid course and diabetes treatment may be adjusted temporarily.
    What may be monitored
    Blood glucose · Blood glucose · HbA1c after prolonged courses

    Prednisolone and steroids + Aspirin

    Severity not gradedInteraction documented

    Corticosteroids with aspirin increase the risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding, and steroids can lower salicylate levels.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information lists steroid medicines among those to tell a doctor about when taking low-dose aspirin.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Documented; gastroprotection is often prescribed when the two are combined.
    What may be monitored
    Signs of gastrointestinal bleeding
