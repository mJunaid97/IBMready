    Ramipril

    An ACE inhibitor that relaxes blood vessels and eases the heart's workload, used for high blood pressure, heart failure, after heart attacks and to protect the kidneys in diabetes.

    What it is used for

    • High blood pressure (first choice under 55 and in diabetes)
    • Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (improves survival)
    • After a heart attack
    • Protecting the kidneys in diabetes and chronic kidney disease with protein in the urine
    • Angiotensin receptor blockers (losartan, candesartan) do the same job for people who cough on ACE inhibitors

    How it works in the body

    Blocks the enzyme that converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II, the hormone that constricts arteries, drives the adrenal glands to release aldosterone (salt and water retention) and thickens heart muscle and vessel walls. Less angiotensin II means relaxed vessels, lower pressure, less strain on a failing heart and lower pressure inside the kidney's filters. ACE also breaks down bradykinin, which accumulates and causes the characteristic dry cough.

    Common side effects

    • Dry tickly cough (10-15%, from bradykinin)
    • Dizziness with the first doses, tiredness, headache
    • A small rise in creatinine and potassium

    Serious: seek help

    • Angio-oedema: swelling of the lips, tongue and throat, can block the airway; stop immediately
    • Acute kidney injury when dehydrated, on NSAIDs, or with narrowed renal arteries
    • High potassium, especially with spironolactone, kidney disease or potassium supplements
    • Harm to the fetus: stop before or as soon as pregnancy is known

    Cautions and interactions

    • Pause on 'sick days' with vomiting, diarrhoea or fever to protect the kidneys
    • Avoid with NSAIDs and potassium supplements; caution with diuretics (first-dose hypotension)
    • Bilateral renal artery stenosis, previous angio-oedema, pregnancy: do not use

    Losartan + ACE inhibitors (class)

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Losartan with any ACE inhibitor (ramipril, lisinopril, enalapril, perindopril) duplicates renin-angiotensin blockade: more hypotension, kidney injury and hyperkalaemia without extra benefit.

    Why it can occur
    pharmacodynamic antagonism
    What official information says
    NICE: do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB to treat hypertension.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK guidance: not recommended.
    What may be monitored
    Blood pressure · Potassium · Kidney function
    Basis of the status
    NICE NG136 1.4: do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB
    Sources
    NICE NG136: Hypertension in adults: diagnosis and management (1.4 Choosing antihypertensive drug treatment) UK
