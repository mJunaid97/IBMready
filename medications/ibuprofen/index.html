    Ibuprofen

    Also known as: NSAID, Nurofen, Advil, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory

    A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) that eases pain, inflammation and fever by blocking prostaglandin production; effective but hard on the stomach and kidneys if overused.

    Generic name
    Ibuprofen
    Drug class
    NSAIDs
    Also known as
    NSAID, Nurofen, Advil, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory
    Forms
    Tablets 200-400 mg, liquid for children, topical gel for local joint and muscle pain (fewer side effects)
    Onset and duration
    30-60 minutes; lasts 4-6 hours.
    Monitoring
    Kidney function and blood pressure for longer-term use.

    What it is used for

    • Pain with an inflammatory component: sprains, back pain, period pain, dental pain, headache
    • Arthritis flares and gout attacks (short courses)
    • Fever
    • Relatives: naproxen (longer acting), diclofenac, and the COX-2-selective celecoxib and etoricoxib

    How it works in the body

    Blocks the cyclo-oxygenase enzymes COX-1 and COX-2, cutting production of prostaglandins, the local messengers that sensitise pain nerves, widen vessels in inflamed tissue and reset the brain's thermostat in fever. The same prostaglandins protect the stomach lining and keep blood flowing through the kidneys, which explains the main harms.

    Common side effects

    • Indigestion, nausea
    • Fluid retention, raised blood pressure

    Serious: seek help

    • Stomach and duodenal ulcers with bleeding or perforation, especially over 65, with steroids, aspirin or anticoagulants
    • Acute kidney injury, particularly when dehydrated or with ACE inhibitors and diuretics ('triple whammy')
    • Heart attack and stroke risk rises with high doses and prolonged use, most with diclofenac and COX-2 inhibitors
    • Worsening asthma in sensitive people; allergic reactions

    Cautions and interactions

    • Avoid in active ulcer disease, severe kidney disease, heart failure, third trimester of pregnancy
    • Use the lowest dose for the shortest time; take with food
    • Do not combine with other NSAIDs or aspirin; ask before use with blood thinners, lithium, methotrexate
    • Older adults often need a proton pump inhibitor alongside
