    Glyceryl trinitrate

    Also known as: GTN, nitroglycerin, GTN spray

    A fast-acting nitrate spray or tablet under the tongue that widens veins and coronary arteries to relieve an angina attack within minutes.

    What it is used for

    • Relieving an angina attack within minutes (spray or tablet under the tongue)
    • Preventing angina before exertion
    • Intravenous infusion for unstable angina, heart attack with ongoing pain and acute heart failure
    • Long-acting isosorbide mononitrate for regular angina prevention; GTN ointment for anal fissures

    How it works in the body

    Releases nitric oxide in vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylate cyclase and relaxing the vessel. Veins dilate most, so less blood returns to the heart and it has less to pump against (lower preload and wall tension, lower oxygen demand); coronary arteries and collaterals widen, improving supply to ischaemic muscle. Relief within minutes is typical of angina, but GTN also eases other pains and relief does not rule out a heart attack; pain that does not settle needs emergency help.

    Common side effects

    • Throbbing headache (from dilated scalp vessels), flushing
    • Dizziness and faintness from a drop in blood pressure: sit down before using

    Serious: seek help

    • Severe hypotension and collapse if combined with sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil or similar drugs: nitrates and PDE-5 inhibitors are contraindicated together, and emergency staff need to know about any recent dose
    • Fast heart rate; rarely worsening of angina; methaemoglobinaemia with prolonged infusion

    Cautions and interactions

    • Tolerance develops with continuous exposure: long-acting nitrates need a nitrate-free interval each day
    • If pain persists 5 minutes after a second dose, call emergency services: it may be a heart attack
    • Severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, very low blood pressure, raised intracranial pressure
    • Tablets lose potency after 8 weeks once opened; sprays last longer

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease Reflux of stomach acid into the oesophagus often enough to cause symptoms or damage the lining.
    • Angina Angina is chest pain or discomfort that happens when the heart muscle does not get enough blood.

    Symptoms

    • Chest pain Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen.
    • Headache Pain felt anywhere in the head.
    • Dizziness An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint.

    Medical tests

    • Electrocardiogram Ten sticky electrodes on the chest and limbs record the heart's electrical activity from twelve angles over ten seconds.
    • Troponin test A blood test for cardiac troponin, a protein released when heart muscle cells are injured.

    Procedures

    • Coronary angioplasty and stenting A balloon and stent passed through a catheter from the wrist or groin reopen a narrowed or blocked coronary artery.

    Medications

    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Metoprolol and beta blockers A beta blocker that slows the heart and reduces its oxygen demand, used for angina, after heart attacks.
    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.
    • Sildenafil Sildenafil is a medicine used to treat erection problems and, sometimes, pulmonary hypertension.
    • Tadalafil Tadalafil is a medicine mainly used to treat erection problems.

    Drug classes

    • Nitrates Vasodilators that relieve angina within minutes by relaxing veins and coronary arteries.
