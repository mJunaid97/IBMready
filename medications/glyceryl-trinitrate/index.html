    Glyceryl trinitrate

    Also known as: GTN, nitroglycerin, GTN spray, nitrates, isosorbide mononitrate

    A fast-acting nitrate spray or tablet under the tongue that widens veins and coronary arteries to relieve an angina attack within minutes.

    Open in the 3D explorerCoronary vessels anatomy
    Generic name
    Glyceryl trinitrate
    Drug class
    Nitrates
    Also known as
    GTN, nitroglycerin, GTN spray, nitrates, isosorbide mononitrate
    Forms
    Sublingual spray 400 micrograms per dose or tablets; patches; intravenous infusion; modified-release tablets (isosorbide)
    Onset and duration
    Sublingual within 1-3 minutes, lasting 20-30 minutes.
    Monitoring
    Blood pressure during infusions; frequency of spray use is a marker of angina control.

    What it is used for

    • Relieving an angina attack within minutes (spray or tablet under the tongue)
    • Preventing angina before exertion
    • Intravenous infusion for unstable angina, heart attack with ongoing pain and acute heart failure
    • Long-acting isosorbide mononitrate for regular angina prevention; GTN ointment for anal fissures

    How it works in the body

    Releases nitric oxide in vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylate cyclase and relaxing the vessel. Veins dilate most, so less blood returns to the heart and it has less to pump against (lower preload and wall tension, lower oxygen demand); coronary arteries and collaterals widen, improving supply to ischaemic muscle. Relief within one to three minutes helps confirm that chest pain was angina, though oesophageal spasm responds too.

    Common side effects

    • Throbbing headache (from dilated scalp vessels), flushing
    • Dizziness and faintness from a drop in blood pressure: sit down before using

    Serious: seek help

    • Severe hypotension and collapse if combined with sildenafil or similar drugs (contraindicated within 24-48 hours)
    • Fast heart rate; rarely worsening of angina; methaemoglobinaemia with prolonged infusion

    Cautions and interactions

    • Tolerance develops with continuous exposure: long-acting nitrates need a nitrate-free interval each day
    • If pain persists 5 minutes after a second dose, call emergency services: it may be a heart attack
    • Severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, very low blood pressure, raised intracranial pressure
    • Tablets lose potency after 8 weeks once opened; sprays last longer
