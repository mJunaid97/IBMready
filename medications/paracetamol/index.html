    Paracetamol

    Also known as: acetaminophen, Tylenol, Panadol, Calpol

    The first-line painkiller and fever reducer for most people (acetaminophen in North America), gentle on the stomach at normal doses but toxic to the liver in overdose.

    Open in the 3D explorerBrain anatomy
    Generic name
    Paracetamol
    Drug class
    Non-opioid analgesics
    Also known as
    acetaminophen, Tylenol, Panadol, Calpol
    Forms
    Tablets and capsules 500 mg, soluble tablets, liquid, suppositories, intravenous infusion
    Onset and duration
    30-60 minutes orally; lasts 4-6 hours. Maximum 4 g in 24 hours for adults (less if under 50 kg or with liver disease).
    Monitoring
    None routinely; liver tests after overdose.

    What it is used for

    • Mild to moderate pain of any cause: headache, muscle aches, back pain, osteoarthritis, after surgery
    • Fever
    • First-line pain relief in pregnancy and for children (by weight)
    • Combined with opioids (co-codamol) or given intravenously in hospital

    How it works in the body

    Still not fully understood. It weakly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase in the brain and spinal cord rather than in inflamed tissue, and its breakdown product AM404 acts on cannabinoid and TRPV1 pathways in the central nervous system, raising the pain threshold and lowering the fever set-point. It has almost no anti-inflammatory effect and does not irritate the stomach.

    Common side effects

    • Very few at normal doses; occasional rash

    Serious: seek help

    • Liver failure in overdose: even 8-10 g (16-20 tablets) can be fatal without treatment, and the danger rises with alcohol, malnutrition and enzyme-inducing drugs
    • Rare severe skin reactions and blood disorders

    Cautions and interactions

    • Count all sources: many cold remedies and combination painkillers contain paracetamol
    • Reduce dose in low body weight, chronic alcohol use and liver disease
    • Overdose is treated with acetylcysteine, most effective within 8 hours: any suspected overdose is an emergency even if the person feels well
