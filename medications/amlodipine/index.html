    Amlodipine

    Also known as: calcium channel blocker, Norvasc, felodipine, nifedipine, diltiazem, verapamil

    A long-acting calcium channel blocker that relaxes artery walls to lower blood pressure and prevent angina, taken once daily and among the most prescribed medicines worldwide.

    Generic name
    Amlodipine
    Drug class
    Calcium channel blockers
    Also known as
    calcium channel blocker, Norvasc, felodipine, nifedipine, diltiazem, verapamil
    Forms
    Tablets 5 or 10 mg once daily
    Onset and duration
    Gradual, over days; steady state in about a week.
    Monitoring
    Blood pressure; no blood tests needed for amlodipine.

    What it is used for

    • High blood pressure (first choice over 55 and in people of Black African or Caribbean origin, or when an ACE inhibitor is not suitable)
    • Angina prevention
    • Non-dihydropyridine relatives (diltiazem, verapamil) also slow the heart and treat some fast rhythms

    How it works in the body

    Blocks L-type calcium channels in the smooth muscle of artery walls. Muscle cells need calcium to contract, so with fewer channels open the arteries relax and widen, lowering resistance and blood pressure and letting more blood reach heart muscle. Amlodipine selects vessels over heart muscle, so it does not slow the heart; its long half-life gives smooth 24-hour control.

    Common side effects

    • Ankle swelling (fluid leaking from widened capillaries, not heart failure; not helped by diuretics)
    • Flushing, headache, palpitations in the first weeks
    • Gum overgrowth, tiredness, dizziness

    Serious: seek help

    • Marked low blood pressure, particularly with other agents
    • Worsening angina rarely at the start (reflex fast heart rate)
    • Verapamil and diltiazem: heart block and heart failure, especially with beta blockers

    Cautions and interactions

    • Grapefruit juice increases levels of some calcium channel blockers
    • Severe aortic stenosis, cardiogenic shock, unstable angina
    • Simvastatin dose is limited with amlodipine
