    Apixaban and DOACs

    A direct oral anticoagulant that blocks clotting factor Xa, preventing strokes in atrial fibrillation and treating deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism without routine blood monitoring.

    What it is used for

    • Preventing stroke in atrial fibrillation (first choice over warfarin for most)
    • Treating and preventing deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
    • Preventing clots after hip and knee replacement
    • Not used for mechanical heart valves or antiphospholipid syndrome (warfarin remains)

    How it works in the body

    Binds directly to activated factor X, the enzyme at the junction of the clotting cascade that converts prothrombin to thrombin. Less thrombin means less fibrin, so clots that form in the slow-flowing left atrial appendage or leg veins are prevented or stopped from growing. Unlike warfarin the effect is predictable, starts within hours, needs no blood-level monitoring and is little affected by diet.

    Common side effects

    • Bruising, nosebleeds, bleeding gums, heavier periods
    • Nausea, anaemia

    Serious: seek help

    • Major bleeding: gastrointestinal, intracranial (about half the rate of warfarin), retroperitoneal
    • Bleeding after trauma or surgery; reversal agents (andexanet, idarucizumab for dabigatran) exist but are limited
    • Spinal haematoma with epidural anaesthesia

    Cautions and interactions

    • Dose reduced with kidney impairment, low weight, age over 80 (apixaban), and some interacting drugs
    • Avoid NSAIDs and aspirin unless specifically advised; alcohol in moderation
    • Stop 24-72 hours before surgery depending on bleeding risk and kidney function
    • Missed doses matter because the effect wears off quickly; do not double up

    Apixaban and DOACs + Ibuprofen

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Ibuprofen with apixaban or another direct oral anticoagulant increases bleeding risk.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information for apixaban advises not taking NSAIDs such as ibuprofen unless a doctor has prescribed them.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK information: avoid unless prescribed; paracetamol preferred.
    What may be monitored
    Signs of bleeding
    Basis of the status

    Apixaban and DOACs + Aspirin

    MajorSpecialist supervision or close monitoring

    Aspirin with apixaban or another direct oral anticoagulant increases bleeding risk.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information for apixaban advises not taking aspirin or NSAIDs unless a doctor has prescribed them; the BNF notes increased bleeding risk.
