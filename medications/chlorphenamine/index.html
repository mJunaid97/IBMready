    Chlorphenamine

    Also known as: chlorpheniramine

    A first-generation antihistamine, sold over the counter, that relieves hay fever, hives and allergic reactions within an hour but causes drowsiness because it enters the brain; also given by injection for severe allergic reactions.

    What it is used for

    • Hay fever and other allergic rhinitis
    • Hives (urticaria), itchy rashes and insect bites
    • Symptom relief in allergic reactions and, by injection, as an adjunct in anaphylaxis after adrenaline
    • Some cold and flu remedies contain it for its drying effect

    How it works in the body

    Chlorphenamine blocks H1 histamine receptors, so the histamine released by mast cells in an allergic reaction can no longer widen small blood vessels, make them leaky or trigger itch and sneezing. Because it crosses into the brain and also blocks some muscarinic receptors, it causes drowsiness, dry mouth and blurred vision, which newer antihistamines largely avoid.

    Common side effects

    • Drowsiness and slowed reactions
    • Dry mouth, blurred vision, difficulty passing urine
    • Headache, feeling sick

    Serious: seek help

    • Confusion, agitation or falls, particularly in older people
    • Palpitations or a fast heartbeat
    • Signs of an allergic reaction to the medicine itself

    Cautions and interactions

    • Do not drive, cycle or operate machinery if drowsy; alcohol and other sedatives add to the effect
    • Avoided in older people where possible (falls, confusion) and in glaucoma, prostate enlargement and urinary retention
    • Not for children under the licensed age without medical advice
    • Adds sedation to opioids, benzodiazepines and some antidepressants
