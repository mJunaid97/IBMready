    Fexofenadine

    Also known as: Telfast, Allevia

    Fexofenadine is a non-drowsy antihistamine that relieves allergy symptoms such as those of hay fever and hives by blocking the effects of histamine. Some people still feel sleepy or dizzy, and the NHS advises against driving, cycling or using tools or machinery if that happens. Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat with difficulty breathing can be a sign of a serious allergic reaction, and the leaflets say to tell a doctor immediately.

    What it is used for

    • Allergy symptoms such as a runny nose, sneezing, and red, itchy or watery eyes, including hay fever
    • Hives, with their itching and redness
    • Conjunctivitis, eczema, insect bite and sting reactions and some food allergies, which the NHS lists among its uses

    How it works in the body

    Fexofenadine blocks the effects of histamine, a substance in the body that drives allergy symptoms. A 2024 clinical review describes it as a non-sedating, second-generation H1 antihistamine. The NHS says it seems less likely to cause sleepiness than other non-drowsy antihistamines.

    Common side effects

    • Headache, feeling sleepy, feeling sick and feeling dizzy are the common side effects the NHS lists.
    • Although fexofenadine is classed as non-drowsy, some people feel quite sleepy, and the NHS advises not driving, cycling or using tools or machinery while sleepy or dizzy.

    Serious: seek help

    • Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat with difficulty breathing can signal a serious allergic reaction; leaflets say to stop the medicine and tell a doctor immediately, and in an emergency the route is 999.

    Cautions and interactions

    • Grapefruit, orange and apple juice can reduce how well fexofenadine works, and MedlinePlus advises taking it with water rather than fruit juice.
    • Leaflets say that indigestion remedies containing aluminium and magnesium can lower how much fexofenadine is absorbed, and advise leaving a gap between the two.
    • A leaflet states that apalutamide, a prostate cancer medicine, may decrease the effect of fexofenadine.
    • The NHS says it is best not to drink alcohol while taking fexofenadine, as it can make you feel sleepy.
    • The NHS lists liver or kidney problems and heart problems among the reasons fexofenadine may not be suitable, and leaflets advise talking to a doctor or pharmacist first about liver or kidney problems, heart disease or older age.
    • One leaflet says antihistamines such as fexofenadine may cause a fast or irregular heartbeat.
    • The NHS lists having ever had an allergic reaction to fexofenadine or any other medicine among the reasons it may not be suitable.
    • The NHS lists pregnancy, trying to get pregnant and breastfeeding among the situations where fexofenadine may not be suitable, and UK leaflets advise against use in pregnancy unless necessary and say it is not recommended while breastfeeding.
    • The NHS says anyone due an allergy test should check first, because the medicine can affect the results.
    • The NHS says fexofenadine does not affect any type of contraception, including the combined pill and emergency contraception.

    What is fexofenadine used for?

    Fexofenadine is an antihistamine medicine that relieves the symptoms of allergies. The NHS lists hay fever, conjunctivitis, eczema, hives, insect bite and sting reactions and some food allergies among the problems it is used for. MedlinePlus describes it as treating allergy symptoms such as a runny nose, sneezing, and red, itchy or watery eyes, as well as itching and redness caused by hives.

    The NHS says tablets for treating hives are available from pharmacies. MedlinePlus has a separate page for products that combine fexofenadine with pseudoephedrine, which are different products from fexofenadine on its own.

    How does fexofenadine work?

    Fexofenadine blocks histamine, a substance in the body that drives allergy symptoms. The NHS says that by blocking the effects of histamine it reduces the symptoms, and that people should start to feel better within 1 to 2 hours. A 2024 clinical review describes it as a non-sedating, second-generation H1 antihistamine.

    The NHS classes fexofenadine as non-drowsy and says it seems less likely to cause sleepiness than other non-drowsy antihistamines. It also says fexofenadine is unlikely to cause harm when used for a long time, though it is best taken only for as long as it is needed.

    What are the side effects of fexofenadine?

    The NHS lists headaches, feeling sleepy, feeling sick and feeling dizzy as the common side effects. Fexofenadine is classed as a non-drowsy antihistamine, but some people still feel quite sleepy. The NHS advises not driving, cycling or using tools or machinery while feeling sleepy or dizzy. Leaflets say fexofenadine is unlikely to affect the ability to drive or use machinery, but advise checking first that the tablets do not cause sleepiness or dizziness.

    Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat with difficulty breathing can be a sign of a serious allergic reaction. The leaflets say to stop the medicine and tell a doctor immediately. In an emergency, call 999.

    Fexofenadine with food, drink and other medicines

    The NHS says grapefruit, orange and apple juice can reduce how well fexofenadine works. MedlinePlus likewise advises taking it with water rather than with fruit juices such as orange, grapefruit or apple juice. The NHS also says it is best not to drink alcohol while taking fexofenadine, as it can make you feel sleepy.

    Leaflets say that indigestion remedies containing aluminium and magnesium can lower how much fexofenadine is absorbed, and advise leaving a gap between the two. A leaflet states that apalutamide, a prostate cancer medicine, may decrease the effect of fexofenadine. The NHS says fexofenadine does not affect any type of contraception, including the combined pill and emergency contraception.

    Who can take fexofenadine, and who needs extra care?

    The NHS says tablets for adults and children aged 12 and over can be bought from pharmacies and supermarkets, while tablets for children under 12 are only available on prescription from a doctor. The NHS lists liver or kidney problems and heart problems among the reasons fexofenadine may not be suitable, and leaflets advise talking to a doctor or pharmacist first about liver or kidney problems, heart disease or older age. One leaflet says antihistamines such as fexofenadine may cause a fast or irregular heartbeat.

    The NHS lists pregnancy, trying to get pregnant and breastfeeding among the situations where fexofenadine may not be suitable. UK leaflets advise against use in pregnancy unless necessary and say it is not recommended while breastfeeding. The NHS also says anyone due an allergy test should check first, because the medicine can affect the results. The NHS lists having ever had an allergic reaction to fexofenadine or any other medicine among the reasons it may not be suitable. Leaflets differ in wording between products, so the leaflet that comes with a particular product applies to it.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is fexofenadine a non-drowsy antihistamine?

    The NHS classes fexofenadine as non-drowsy and says it seems less likely to cause sleepiness than other non-drowsy antihistamines. Even so, some people feel quite sleepy or dizzy, and the NHS advises not driving, cycling or using tools or machinery if that happens.

    Can fruit juice be drunk with fexofenadine?

    The NHS says grapefruit, orange and apple juice can reduce how well fexofenadine works. MedlinePlus advises taking it with water rather than fruit juice. The NHS also says it is best not to drink alcohol while taking fexofenadine.

    How quickly does fexofenadine work?

    The NHS says people should start to feel better within 1 to 2 hours. It says fexofenadine is unlikely to cause harm when used for a long time, but it is best taken only for as long as it is needed.

    Can fexofenadine be bought without a prescription?

    The NHS says tablets for adults and children aged 12 and over can be bought from pharmacies and supermarkets. Tablets for children under 12 are only available with a prescription from a doctor.

    Does fexofenadine affect contraception?

    The NHS says fexofenadine does not affect any type of contraception, including the combined pill and emergency contraception.

    Can fexofenadine be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding?

    The NHS lists pregnancy, trying to get pregnant and breastfeeding among the situations where fexofenadine may not be suitable. UK leaflets advise against use in pregnancy unless necessary and say it is not recommended while breastfeeding. A GP or pharmacist can advise on an individual situation.
