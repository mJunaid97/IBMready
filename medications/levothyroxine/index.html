    Levothyroxine

    Also known as: thyroxine, T4, Synthroid, Eltroxin

    Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism; the dose is set by regular TSH blood tests.

    Open in the 3D explorerLarynx anatomyCheck interactions
    Generic name
    Levothyroxine
    Drug class
    Thyroid hormones
    Brand names
    UK Eltroxin · US Synthroid, Levoxyl, Tirosint
    Also known as
    thyroxine, T4, Synthroid, Eltroxin
    Forms
    Tablets 25, 50, 100 micrograms and oral solution, once daily on an empty stomach 30-60 minutes before breakfast (or at bedtime)
    Onset and duration
    Symptoms improve over weeks; TSH takes 6-8 weeks to settle after each dose change. Lifelong.
    Monitoring
    TSH 6-8 weeks after starting or changing dose, then yearly once stable.

    What it is used for

    • Hypothyroidism from any cause (autoimmune, after surgery or radioiodine, congenital)
    • Suppressing TSH after thyroid cancer
    • Subclinical hypothyroidism when TSH is high, symptomatic, or in pregnancy

    How it works in the body

    A synthetic copy of thyroxine (T4), the hormone the thyroid normally releases. Tissues convert it to the active T3, which enters cell nuclei and switches on genes controlling metabolic rate, heat production, heart rate, gut motility, growth and brain development. Replacement restores the pituitary's TSH to normal, which is how the dose is tuned.

    Common side effects

    • Few at the right dose; at too high a dose: palpitations, weight loss, sweating, anxiety, insomnia, diarrhoea (symptoms of hyperthyroidism)
    • Temporary hair loss in the first months

    Serious: seek help

    • Angina or atrial fibrillation if started too fast in the elderly or those with heart disease (start at 25 micrograms)
    • Bone loss and atrial fibrillation with long-term over-replacement
    • Adrenal crisis if given before untreated adrenal insufficiency is corrected

    Cautions and interactions

    • Absorption falls with calcium, iron, antacids, proton pump inhibitors, soya and coffee: separate by 4 hours
    • Dose often needs to rise in pregnancy (increase by 25-50% and check TSH promptly), and with oestrogen, enzyme inducers
    • Stay on the same brand where possible
    • Do not use for weight loss in people with normal thyroid function

    Other medicines

    Levothyroxine + Omeprazole

    ModerateMonitoring or dose adjustment advised

    Reduced stomach acid lowers the absorption of levothyroxine, which can leave thyroid replacement inadequate.

    Why it can occur
    altered gastric pH
    What official information says
    The BNF lists proton pump inhibitors among drugs that may reduce levothyroxine absorption and advises monitoring.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official guidance: TSH rechecked after starting a PPI; the levothyroxine dose adjusted if needed.
    What may be monitored
    TSH

    Onset: Weeks
