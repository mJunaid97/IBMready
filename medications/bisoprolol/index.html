    Bisoprolol

    A once-daily beta blocker that selectively slows the heart and lowers its workload, used for heart failure, angina, high blood pressure and to control the rate in atrial fibrillation.

    Tablets 1.25 to 10 mg once daily, started low and increased slowly in heart failure
    Onset and duration
    Heart rate and blood pressure fall within hours; the benefit in heart failure builds over months. Not stopped suddenly: the dose is reduced gradually.

    Heart rate and blood pressure at each dose change; symptoms of heart failure and fluid retention during titration; blood glucose awareness in diabetes.

    What it is used for

    • Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, where it improves survival
    • Angina
    • High blood pressure, usually when other classes are unsuitable or as an add-on
    • Rate control in atrial fibrillation and other fast rhythms

    How it works in the body

    Bisoprolol blocks beta-1 adrenoceptors on heart muscle and the conducting system, so adrenaline and noradrenaline can no longer speed the heart or make it contract as forcefully. The heart beats more slowly, needs less oxygen and, in heart failure, is protected from the long-term damage of sustained sympathetic drive. It has little effect on the beta-2 receptors of the airways at usual doses, though it is still used with caution in asthma.

    Common side effects

    • Tiredness, cold hands and feet, a slow pulse
    • Headache, dizziness, feeling sick
    • Sleep disturbance

    Serious: seek help

    • Breathlessness or wheeze, particularly in asthma
    • Fainting or a very slow heartbeat
    • Worsening fluid retention early in heart failure treatment
    • Signs of an allergic reaction

    Cautions and interactions

    • Avoided in asthma unless a specialist judges the benefit greater; used cautiously in COPD
    • Not started during decompensated heart failure; introduced once the person is stable
    • Combined with verapamil or diltiazem it can slow the heart dangerously
    • Masks the warning signs of low blood sugar in diabetes
    • Never stopped abruptly: the dose is tapered
