    Tadalafil

    Also known as: Cialis, Cialis Together, Adcirca

    Tadalafil is a medicine mainly used to treat erection problems. The NHS says it is also sometimes prescribed for urinary problems linked to an enlarged prostate and for pulmonary arterial hypertension. The NHS says it is not usually taken with nitrates, and a painful or prolonged erection is a serious side effect for which hospital guidance advises 999 or A&E.

    What it is used for

    • Erection problems (erectile dysfunction), the main use the NHS names.
    • Urinary problems caused by an enlarged prostate (benign prostate enlargement).
    • Pulmonary arterial hypertension.
    • Second-line treatment for Raynaud's phenomenon and digital ulcers, according to one NHS hospital leaflet.

    How it works in the body

    Tadalafil belongs to a group of medicines called PDE5 inhibitors. StatPearls describes these medicines as stopping the PDE5 enzyme from breaking down a chemical messenger called cGMP. UK patient leaflets say tadalafil does not cause an erection by itself, and sexual stimulation is still needed. StatPearls says its effect on the prostate and bladder helps relieve enlarged prostate symptoms by relaxing these tissues and improving urine flow.

    Common side effects

    • The NHS lists indigestion, muscle aches, back pain, a blocked nose, a flushed face and headaches as common side effects.

    Serious: seek help

    • The NHS says serious side effects are possible but most are rare, and to call NHS 111 if you think you are having a serious side effect.
    • The serious side effects the NHS lists include an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) with a swollen throat or tongue, a raised itchy rash or difficulty breathing.
    • NHS medicine pages for similar drugs, such as sildenafil, give 999 for swelling of the lips, mouth or throat or difficulty breathing.
    • A painful or prolonged erection is listed as serious; hospital guidance advises 999 or A&E.
    • Sudden changes in hearing or eyesight are listed as serious, and MedlinePlus advises calling a doctor right away.
    • Seizures, palpitations and chest pain are also listed as serious.

    Cautions and interactions

    • The NHS says tadalafil is not usually taken with nitrates, such as GTN for chest pain, and advises asking a pharmacist or doctor if you take them.
    • StatPearls describes the nitrate-related fall in blood pressure as potentially life-threatening.
    • Poppers (amyl nitrate) or illegal drugs such as amphetamines, ecstasy and cocaine can increase the risk of serious side effects.
    • The NHS advises avoiding grapefruit juice.
    • Alcohol can cause dizziness or light-headedness by lowering blood pressure.
    • The NHS says tadalafil may not be suitable after a serious reaction to it before or a recent heart attack or stroke.
    • Liver or kidney problems, certain blood cancers such as leukaemia, sickle cell anaemia and a curved or bent penis that makes erections painful or difficult are also listed.
    • Past vision loss in one eye from reduced blood flow to the optic nerve is listed too.
    • The NHS advises avoiding tadalafil in pregnancy and while breastfeeding.
    • Local NHS guidance lists uncontrolled high blood pressure as a reason for caution.
    • A review says alpha-blockers call for caution when used with PDE5 inhibitors.

    What is tadalafil used for?

    The NHS says tadalafil is mainly used to treat erection problems. It is also sometimes prescribed for urinary problems linked to an enlarged prostate (benign prostate enlargement), and for pulmonary arterial hypertension, which is high blood pressure in the blood vessels to the lungs. Brand names include Cialis, Cialis Together and Adcirca.

    A hospital patient leaflet describes both sildenafil and tadalafil as second-line treatments for Raynaud's phenomenon and for digital ulcers, which are sores on the fingers and toes. The MHRA has reclassified Cialis Together tablets as a pharmacy medicine.

    How does tadalafil work?

    Tadalafil belongs to a group of medicines called PDE5 inhibitors. StatPearls says PDE5 inhibitors stop the PDE5 enzyme from breaking down cGMP, a chemical messenger. UK patient leaflets say tadalafil does not cause an erection by itself, and sexual stimulation is still needed.

    StatPearls adds that tadalafil's effect on the prostate and bladder helps relieve symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia by relaxing these tissues and improving urine flow. UK patient leaflets say the medicine may still be effective up to 36 hours after taking the tablet.

    What are the side effects of tadalafil?

    The NHS lists indigestion, muscle aches, back pain, a blocked nose, a flushed face and headaches as common side effects.

    The NHS says serious side effects are possible but most are rare, and to call NHS 111 if you think you are having a serious side effect. The serious side effects it lists include an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) with a swollen throat or tongue, a raised itchy rash or difficulty breathing, sudden changes in hearing or eyesight, a painful or prolonged erection, seizures, palpitations and chest pain. NHS medicine pages for similar drugs, such as sildenafil, give 999 for swelling of the lips, mouth or throat or difficulty breathing. For a painful or prolonged erection, hospital guidance advises 999 or A&E. MedlinePlus advises calling a doctor right away for a sudden loss of vision or hearing, which may come with ringing in the ears or dizziness.

    Tadalafil with other medicines, drugs and alcohol

    The NHS says tadalafil is not usually taken with nitrates, such as GTN for chest pain, and advises asking a pharmacist or doctor if you take them. StatPearls describes the nitrate-related low blood pressure as potentially life-threatening. UK product information says tadalafil makes the blood-pressure-lowering effect of nitrates stronger, so using them together is contraindicated.

    The NHS says poppers (amyl nitrate) and illegal drugs such as amphetamines, ecstasy and cocaine can increase the risk of serious side effects. The NHS advises avoiding grapefruit juice. Alcohol can cause dizziness or light-headedness through lower blood pressure. An NHS Highland guideline says tadalafil and sildenafil are contraindicated with nitrates and nicorandil, and a review calls for caution with alpha-blockers.

    Who needs extra care with tadalafil?

    The NHS says tadalafil may not be suitable for everyone. This includes people who have had a serious reaction to it before or who have recently had a heart attack or stroke. It also includes people with liver or kidney problems.

    Blood cancers such as leukaemia and sickle cell anaemia are also listed. So are past vision loss in one eye from reduced blood flow to the optic nerve, and a curved or bent penis that makes erections painful or difficult. Local NHS guidance lists uncontrolled high blood pressure as a reason for caution. The NHS advises avoiding tadalafil in pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can tadalafil be taken with nitrates?

    The NHS says tadalafil is not usually taken with nitrates, such as GTN for chest pain, and advises asking a pharmacist or doctor if you take them. StatPearls describes the nitrate-related fall in blood pressure as potentially life-threatening.

    Are poppers or alcohol a problem with tadalafil?

    The NHS says taking tadalafil with poppers (amyl nitrate) or illegal drugs such as amphetamines, ecstasy and cocaine can increase the risk of serious side effects. It advises avoiding grapefruit juice. Alcohol can cause dizziness or light-headedness because it lowers blood pressure.

    Can tadalafil be used for prostate symptoms?

    The NHS says tadalafil is sometimes prescribed for urinary problems linked to an enlarged prostate (benign prostate enlargement). StatPearls says its effect on the prostate and bladder helps relieve these symptoms by relaxing these tissues.

    What does the NHS say about sudden vision or hearing changes?

    The NHS lists sudden changes in hearing or eyesight as serious side effects of tadalafil. MedlinePlus says a sudden decrease or loss of vision or hearing is a reason to call a doctor right away. For sildenafil, the NHS gives a doctor or NHS 111 as the route for sudden loss of vision.

    Can tadalafil be used in pregnancy or breastfeeding?

    The NHS advises avoiding tadalafil in pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

    What does a painful erection mean on tadalafil?

    The NHS lists a painful or prolonged erection as a serious side effect. Hospital guidance advises calling 999 or going to A&E. The NHS lists seizures and chest pain among the other serious side effects.
