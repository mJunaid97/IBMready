    Alendronate

    Also known as: alendronic acid, bisphosphonate, Fosamax, risedronate, zoledronic acid

    A weekly bisphosphonate that slows bone breakdown, strengthening bone in osteoporosis and cutting the risk of hip and spine fractures.

    Generic name
    Alendronate
    Drug class
    Bisphosphonates
    Brand names
    UK Fosamax · US Fosamax, Binosto
    Also known as
    alendronic acid, bisphosphonate, Fosamax, risedronate, zoledronic acid
    Forms
    Tablets 70 mg once weekly (or 10 mg daily), swallowed with a full glass of plain water on an empty stomach, standing or sitting upright for 30 minutes before food, drink or other medicines; effervescent and liquid forms; risedronate weekly; ibandronate monthly; zoledronic acid infusion yearly
    Onset and duration
    Bone turnover falls within weeks; fracture reduction from about a year. Usually 5 years, then reviewed (drug 'holiday').
    Monitoring
    Bone density scan every 3-5 years; calcium and vitamin D; kidney function for intravenous forms.

    What it is used for

    • Osteoporosis: preventing hip, spine and wrist fractures in postmenopausal women and men at high risk
    • Bone protection during long courses of steroid tablets
    • Zoledronic acid infusions for osteoporosis (yearly), Paget disease, bone metastases and high calcium in cancer

    How it works in the body

    The drug binds tightly to bone mineral, and when osteoclasts (the cells that dissolve bone) resorb that surface they take it up, which disables their metabolism and triggers their death. Resorption falls while bone-building osteoblasts keep working for a time, so bone density rises by a few per cent and, more importantly, the microarchitecture is preserved; hip fractures fall by about 40% and spine fractures by half.

    Common side effects

    • Indigestion, heartburn, abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhoea
    • Muscle and joint aches; flu-like illness after the first zoledronic acid infusion

    Serious: seek help

    • Oesophageal ulcers and strictures if the dosing instructions are not followed
    • Osteonecrosis of the jaw (rare, mostly with high-dose cancer regimens; dental check before starting)
    • Atypical femoral fractures after many years of use (thigh pain is a warning)
    • Low calcium, eye inflammation, kidney impairment with intravenous forms

    Cautions and interactions

    • Correct vitamin D deficiency and ensure adequate calcium before starting
    • Not used with eGFR below 35, swallowing disorders, active oesophageal disease, or inability to sit upright
    • Dental review and good oral hygiene; tell the dentist
    • Review at 5 years (3 for zoledronate): continue in those at highest risk
