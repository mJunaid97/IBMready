    Furosemide

    Also known as: loop diuretic, frusemide, Lasix, bumetanide, water tablet

    A powerful loop diuretic that makes the kidneys excrete salt and water within an hour, used to clear fluid overload in heart failure, kidney disease and liver disease.

    Generic name
    Furosemide
    Drug class
    Loop diuretics
    Also known as
    loop diuretic, frusemide, Lasix, bumetanide, water tablet
    Forms
    Tablets 20, 40, 500 mg; oral solution; intravenous injection or infusion
    Onset and duration
    Oral within an hour, lasting about 6 hours (hence the name Lasix: 'lasts six'); intravenous within minutes.
    Monitoring
    Kidney function and electrolytes within a week of starting or changing dose, then regularly; weight and blood pressure.

    What it is used for

    • Fluid overload in heart failure: breathlessness, swollen legs, fluid on the lungs (intravenous in emergencies)
    • Oedema from kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome) and liver cirrhosis (with spironolactone)
    • High blood pressure when kidney function is poor
    • Thiazide-like diuretics (indapamide, bendroflumethiazide) are the gentler relatives used for blood pressure

    How it works in the body

    Blocks the sodium-potassium-chloride co-transporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, the segment that reabsorbs a quarter of all filtered sodium. Salt that stays in the tubule drags water with it, so urine output can rise several-fold within an hour. Potassium, magnesium and calcium are lost too, and the kidney compensates by activating the renin-angiotensin system.

    Common side effects

    • Passing lots of urine, urgency (take in the morning)
    • Dizziness on standing, dehydration, thirst, cramps
    • Low potassium, sodium and magnesium; raised uric acid (gout) and glucose

    Serious: seek help

    • Acute kidney injury from over-diuresis
    • Dangerous potassium or sodium disturbance and arrhythmia
    • Hearing loss (ototoxicity) with rapid high intravenous doses, especially with aminoglycosides
    • Collapse from low blood pressure in the frail

    Cautions and interactions

    • Daily weights in heart failure guide the dose: a gain of 2 kg in 3 days often means more diuretic
    • Pause on sick days with vomiting or diarrhoea
    • Care with lithium, digoxin, NSAIDs, aminoglycosides
    • Severe low potassium or sodium, anuria, dehydration: do not use until corrected
