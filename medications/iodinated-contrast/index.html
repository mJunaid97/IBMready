    Medication · Contrast & diagnostic agents

    Iodinated contrast agents

    Also known as: contrast dye, CT contrast, X-ray contrast medium, iodine contrast

    The iodine-containing dyes injected for CT scans and angiography that make blood vessels, organs and tumours show up, generally safe but with a small risk of allergic-type reactions and kidney injury in vulnerable people.

    What it is used for

    • Making blood vessels, organs and abnormal tissue visible on CT scans
    • Angiography and coronary angiography under X-ray
    • Some X-ray examinations of the urinary tract, gut and joints

    How it works in the body

    Iodine atoms absorb X-rays strongly. Injected into the bloodstream, the contrast medium briefly fills vessels and perfuses organs, so those structures block more X-rays and appear bright on the image while the surrounding tissue stays darker; timing the scan to the arterial or venous phase shows different things. The molecules are not metabolised and are removed by the kidneys within hours.

    Common side effects

    • A warm feeling, flushing and a metallic taste during the injection
    • Feeling sick, a mild rash or itching

    Serious: seek help

    • Allergic-type reactions, from hives to, rarely, anaphylaxis
    • Acute kidney injury in people with pre-existing kidney disease, dehydration or diabetes
    • Leakage into the tissues at the injection site
    • Effects on thyroid function in people with thyroid disease

    Cautions and interactions

    • Kidney function is checked before the scan in people at risk; extra fluids may be given
    • A previous contrast reaction, severe allergy or asthma is declared beforehand
    • Metformin may be paused around the scan in people with reduced kidney function (the radiology team advises)
    • Thyroid disease and planned radioactive iodine treatment
    • Pregnancy: used only when the scan is essential
