    Salbutamol

    Also known as: albuterol, Ventolin, blue inhaler, reliever inhaler, SABA, terbutaline

    The blue reliever inhaler: a short-acting beta-2 agonist that opens the airways within minutes to relieve asthma and COPD symptoms during an attack or before exercise.

    Generic name
    Salbutamol
    Drug class
    Bronchodilators
    Brand names
    UK Ventolin, Salamol · US ProAir, Ventolin HFA, Proventil
    Also known as
    albuterol, Ventolin, blue inhaler, reliever inhaler, SABA, terbutaline
    Forms
    Pressurised metered-dose inhaler 100 micrograms per puff (best with a spacer), dry powder inhaler, nebuliser solution, tablets and syrup (rarely), intravenous
    Onset and duration
    Within 5 minutes, peak 15-30 minutes, lasts 4-6 hours.
    Monitoring
    Peak flow and symptom diary; frequency of use as a marker of control.

    What it is used for

    • Relief of wheeze and breathlessness in asthma and COPD ('reliever')
    • Before exercise in exercise-induced asthma
    • Nebulised or intravenous in severe asthma attacks and COPD flares
    • Lowering high potassium in emergencies (nebulised)

    How it works in the body

    Stimulates beta-2 adrenergic receptors on the smooth muscle wrapped around the bronchi. This raises cyclic AMP inside the muscle cells, relaxing them within minutes so the airways widen and air moves more freely. It relieves the narrowing but does nothing for the underlying inflammation, which is why needing it more than twice a week signals poorly controlled asthma and the need for a preventer.

    Common side effects

    • Fine tremor, fast heartbeat, palpitations, headache
    • Feeling shaky or restless; muscle cramps

    Serious: seek help

    • Low potassium with high doses (with steroids, diuretics, theophylline)
    • Arrhythmias in people with heart disease at high doses
    • Paradoxical bronchospasm (rare); over-reliance masking worsening asthma, linked to asthma deaths

    Cautions and interactions

    • Using more than three canisters a year, or needing it daily, means the asthma plan needs review
    • Anti-inflammatory reliever (budesonide-formoterol) is now preferred as the sole reliever in many guidelines
    • Inhaler technique matters: slow deep breath, hold for 10 seconds; spacers for children and during attacks
    • Beta blockers antagonise the effect
