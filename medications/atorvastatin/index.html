    Atorvastatin

    Also known as: statin, Lipitor, simvastatin, rosuvastatin

    A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver, cutting the risk of heart attack and stroke in people at raised cardiovascular risk.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy
    Generic name
    Atorvastatin
    Drug class
    Statins
    Also known as
    statin, Lipitor, simvastatin, rosuvastatin
    Forms
    Tablets 10, 20, 40, 80 mg, once daily at any time (simvastatin at night)
    Onset and duration
    Cholesterol falls within two weeks; plaque benefits build over years. Taken lifelong.
    Monitoring
    Lipids and liver tests at baseline, 3 months and 12 months; creatine kinase only if muscle symptoms.

    What it is used for

    • Lowering LDL cholesterol to prevent heart attacks and strokes
    • Everyone with established cardiovascular disease (secondary prevention, usually 80 mg)
    • Primary prevention when 10-year cardiovascular risk is 10% or more, in diabetes, chronic kidney disease and familial hypercholesterolaemia (usually 20 mg)

    How it works in the body

    Blocks HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme the liver uses to make cholesterol. The liver responds by putting more LDL receptors on its surface to pull cholesterol out of the blood, so LDL falls by 40-55%. Statins also stabilise the fatty plaques in artery walls, making them less likely to rupture and cause a clot.

    Common side effects

    • Muscle aches (real in a minority; most reported aches occur equally on placebo)
    • Headache, nausea, bowel changes
    • Small rise in blood glucose (slightly earlier diagnosis of diabetes in those already at risk)

    Serious: seek help

    • Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown, very rare), more with high doses, interacting drugs and hypothyroidism
    • Liver enzyme rise (rare, usually harmless), haemorrhagic stroke risk very slightly higher in those with a previous bleed

    Cautions and interactions

    • Interactions: clarithromycin, some antifungals, ciclosporin, grapefruit juice (mainly simvastatin), gemfibrozil
    • Avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding; active liver disease
    • Unexplained muscle pain with dark urine needs a same-day creatine kinase test
    • Check thyroid function before starting if aches occur
