    Allopurinol

    A xanthine oxidase inhibitor that lowers uric acid in the blood, taken every day to prevent gout attacks and kidney stones rather than to treat an acute attack.

    Allopurinol
    Product type

    Drug class
    Xanthine oxidase inhibitors
    Brand names
    UK Zyloric · US Zyloprim
    Routes

    Forms
    Tablets 100 and 300 mg once daily after food, starting at 100 mg (50 mg in kidney disease) and titrating up to 900 mg
    Onset and duration
    Urate falls within days, but attacks can increase for the first 3-6 months as crystals dissolve, so colchicine or an NSAID is given as cover.
    Terminology
    RxNorm RxCUI 519 · ATC M04AA01 · FDA EPC Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitor [EPC]
    Monitoring
    Serum urate every 4 weeks during titration, then yearly; kidney and liver function.

    What it is used for

    • Preventing gout attacks by lowering blood uric acid (started after the first attack settles, especially with repeated attacks, tophi, kidney stones or kidney disease)
    • Preventing uric acid kidney stones
    • Preventing tumour lysis syndrome during chemotherapy

    How it works in the body

    Blocks xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that converts hypoxanthine and xanthine into uric acid at the end of purine breakdown. Blood urate falls below its saturation point, so no new crystals form and existing deposits in joints and tophi slowly dissolve over months. The dose is increased every few weeks until the urate target (below 360, or 300 micromol/L with tophi) is reached.

    Common side effects

    • Rash (stop if it appears), nausea, diarrhoea
    • Gout flares in the first months

    Serious: seek help

    • Allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome: severe skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson, toxic epidermal necrolysis) with liver and kidney involvement, rare but life-threatening, more likely in those with the HLA-B*58:01 gene (common in Han Chinese, Thai and Korean populations, who are screened), kidney disease and high starting doses
    • Bone marrow suppression, liver injury (rare)

    Cautions and interactions

    • Do not start during an acute attack; do not stop during an attack once established
    • Interactions: azathioprine and mercaptopurine (levels rise dangerously), warfarin, ACE inhibitors and thiazides (hypersensitivity risk)
    • Drink plenty of fluid; reduce dose in kidney impairment
    • Febuxostat is the alternative if allopurinol is not tolerated, with cardiovascular caution

    Other medicines

    Allopurinol + Amoxicillin

    Severity not gradedInteraction documented

    Allopurinol with amoxicillin increases the chance of a skin rash.

    Why it can occur
    other
    What official information says
    UK information lists allopurinol among medicines to tell a doctor about because of the increased risk of rash.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Documented; a rash during the course is reported and assessed rather than assumed to be penicillin allergy.
    What may be monitored
    Rash
