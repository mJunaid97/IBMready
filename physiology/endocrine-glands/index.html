    The major endocrine glands

    Pituitary, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals, pancreatic islets, gonads and pineal each make a signature set of hormones; several other organs, including the heart, kidneys, gut and fat, make hormones too.

    Open in the 3D explorerPituitary gland anatomy

    The pituitary controls growth, thyroid and adrenal function, reproduction, milk production and water balance. The thyroid sets the metabolic rate through thyroxine; the four parathyroids behind it raise blood calcium. The adrenal cortex makes cortisol (stress and glucose), aldosterone (salt and blood pressure) and androgens; its medulla makes adrenaline. The islets of the pancreas make insulin and glucagon; the testes make testosterone and the ovaries oestrogen and progesterone; the pineal makes melatonin.

    Less famous sources matter clinically: the heart releases natriuretic peptides when stretched (used to diagnose heart failure), the kidneys release erythropoietin to make red cells, the gut makes incretins that boost insulin, and fat tissue makes leptin, which signals energy stores to the brain.

    In this atlas the pituitary and adrenal glands are modelled; the thyroid and parathyroids are not part of the dataset.

    What makes a gland endocrine is the route its product takes. Endocrine glands are ductless: they secrete hormones directly into the interstitial fluid and bloodstream, which carries them to every tissue, whereas exocrine glands such as sweat glands deliver their secretions through ducts to a surface or cavity. The pancreas is both: most of its tissue is exocrine and makes digestive enzymes, while the scattered islets act as an endocrine gland, releasing insulin when blood glucose rises and glucagon when it falls. Because it relies on chemical signalling through the circulation, the endocrine system is slower than the nervous system and suited to maintaining the internal environment, growth and reproduction rather than to split-second responses.

    The glands form a hierarchy with the pea-sized pituitary at the base of the brain near the top: it releases adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), which tells the two adrenal glands sitting on top of the kidneys to make cortisol, a hormone that helps regulate blood pressure, damp down inflammation and turn food into energy. The system as a whole influences growth and development, metabolism, sexual function and mood, so a disorder of one gland tends to produce symptoms across many body systems.

    Endocrine disease arises when a gland makes too much or too little hormone, or when the body stops responding to a hormone normally. Thyroid disorders, whether an underactive or an overactive gland, are often autoimmune, as in Hashimoto's disease and Graves' disease, and glands can also be affected together: in multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1, an inherited disorder, tumours develop in the parathyroids, the pancreas and the pituitary. Assessment usually starts with blood tests that pair the gland's hormone with the pituitary hormone that controls it, followed by imaging of the gland concerned.

    Connected pages in detail

    What each linked page on Anatomy Nexus covers, in a line, for The major endocrine glands.

    Conditions

    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.
    • Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism) Excess thyroid hormone, most often from Graves' disease, in which antibodies stimulate the thyroid to overproduce.
    • Type 1 diabetes An autoimmune disease in which the insulin-producing beta cells of the pancreas are destroyed.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.

    Medical tests

    • Thyroid function tests TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together.
    • Hormone tests Blood levels of hormones and related chemicals, interpreted against the time of day.
    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • TSH test The single most sensitive blood test of thyroid function: TSH from the pituitary rises when the thyroid is underactive and falls when it is…

    Biomarkers

    • Free T4 (thyroxine) The unbound fraction of thyroxine, the main hormone the thyroid gland secretes.
    • TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) The pituitary hormone that drives the thyroid gland.

    Medications

    • Levothyroxine Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism.
    • Prednisolone and steroids A corticosteroid tablet that suppresses inflammation and the immune system, used in asthma flare-ups, arthritis.
