    Pancreatic functions

    One organ, two jobs: exocrine cells pour digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the duodenum; the islets of Langerhans release insulin and glucagon into the blood.

    Open in the 3D explorerPancreas anatomy

    About 1.5 litres of alkaline pancreatic juice a day neutralises stomach acid and delivers lipase, amylase and the protein-digesting enzymes, which are secreted as inactive precursors and switched on only in the gut so the pancreas does not digest itself. When that safeguard fails, the result is pancreatitis.

    The islets sense blood glucose directly. After a meal beta cells release insulin, which tells muscle, fat and liver to take up and store glucose; between meals alpha cells release glucagon, which makes the liver release it again. This pair holds blood glucose within a narrow band around 4 to 7 mmol/L.

    Type 1 diabetes destroys the beta cells; type 2 diabetes is mainly resistance to insulin's signal with a gradual failure of the beta cells to compensate.

    The islets contain several cell types, not just two. Beta cells make up about three quarters of each islet and release insulin when blood glucose is high; alpha cells make up about a fifth and release glucagon when it is low; the few delta cells secrete somatostatin, and others pancreatic polypeptide. Insulin enhances glucose uptake and use by its target cells and promotes storage of the excess, notably as glycogen in the liver, which lowers blood glucose; glucagon stimulates the breakdown of that glycogen, raising it again. Because the two hormones oppose each other, the islets act as a self-correcting controller that needs no input from the brain, although autonomic nerves and gut hormones released after a meal modulate the response.

    The exocrine tissue is the bulk of the organ. Its two main roles, in the NIDDK's summary, are making insulin and making the digestive juices that break down carbohydrates, proteins and fats in the intestine. Pancreatitis is inflammation that occurs when those enzymes damage the pancreas itself: acute pancreatitis comes on suddenly, with gallstones a common cause, and usually settles, whereas chronic pancreatitis does not heal or improve and gradually destroys both enzyme-producing and hormone-producing tissue, so that food is malabsorbed and diabetes can follow.

    Pancreatic function is assessed from both sides. Blood glucose and glycated haemoglobin reflect the endocrine side, and diabetes mellitus is diagnosed when insulin production fails or target cells stop responding to it. Serum amylase and lipase rise sharply in acute pancreatitis, while faecal elastase and the presence of fat in the stool indicate exocrine insufficiency. Imaging with ultrasound, CT or MRCP shows inflammation, gallstones, cysts and ductal change.

    What each linked page on Anatomy Nexus covers, in a line, for Pancreatic functions.

    Conditions

    • Type 1 diabetes An autoimmune disease in which the insulin-producing beta cells of the pancreas are destroyed.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • Pancreatitis Inflammation of the pancreas, acute when its own enzymes are activated inside the gland.

    Medical tests

    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • HbA1c The fraction of haemoglobin that has glucose stuck to it.

    Biomarkers

    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose, the body's main circulating fuel, in blood at the moment of sampling.
    • HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) Haemoglobin that has glucose attached to it.

    Medications

    • Insulin The hormone that moves glucose from the blood into cells, given by injection or pump to everyone with type 1 diabetes and to many with type 2 when…
    • Metformin The first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it reduces the glucose the liver releases and improves the body's response to insulin.
    • Gliclazide Gliclazide is a sulfonylurea tablet prescribed for type 2 diabetes that prompts the pancreas to make more insulin.

    Drug classes

    • Biguanides Metformin is the only biguanide in routine use and the first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it lowers glucose without causing hypoglycaemia or…
    • Insulins Injected replacements for the hormone the pancreas no longer makes or cannot make enough of: essential in type 1 diabetes and used in type 2…
    • Sulfonylureas Sulfonylureas are tablets for type 2 diabetes that prompt the pancreas to make more insulin.
