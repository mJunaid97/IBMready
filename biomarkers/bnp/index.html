    Natriuretic peptides (BNP and NT-proBNP)

    Also known as: BNP, NT-proBNP, B-type natriuretic peptide, brain natriuretic peptide

    Hormones released by the heart's ventricles when their walls are stretched by high pressure or volume. A normal level makes heart failure unlikely; a high level in someone breathless calls for an echocardiogram and specialist assessment.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy
    Also known as
    BNP, NT-proBNP, B-type natriuretic peptide, brain natriuretic peptide
    Units
    ng/L (pg/mL) for both peptides; BNP and NT-proBNP have separate thresholds and are not interchangeable
    Measured by
    BNP and NT-proBNP test, Echocardiogram, Electrocardiogram

    What it is and what it does

    Ventricular muscle cells make pro-BNP and split it into active BNP and the inactive fragment NT-proBNP; laboratories measure one or the other, and the two have different reference limits. BNP acts on the kidney to shed sodium and water and relaxes blood vessels, a natural counterweight to the fluid retention of heart failure. Because release is proportional to wall stress, the level rises with heart failure of either reduced or preserved ejection fraction, with valve disease, atrial fibrillation and pulmonary embolism. Guidelines use NT-proBNP bands to decide how urgently a person with suspected heart failure should be seen for echocardiography.

    Why it may be higher

    • Heart failure of any cause, valve disease, myocardial infarction
    • Atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, pulmonary hypertension
    • Chronic kidney disease (reduced clearance), sepsis, older age
    • Left ventricular hypertrophy, some chemotherapy

    Why it may be lower

    • Obesity lowers the level, which can mask heart failure
    • Treatment with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs and sacubitril/valsartan changes levels (sacubitril raises BNP but not NT-proBNP)

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Age, kidney function and body weight
    • Whether the laboratory measures BNP or NT-proBNP
    • Acute illness on the day of sampling
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    Conditions

    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Atrial fibrillation A common heart rhythm disorder in which the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting, producing an irregular and often fast pulse.
    • Pulmonary embolism Blockage of a pulmonary artery by a clot that has travelled from a vein, usually in the leg or pelvis.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.

    Symptoms

    • Shortness of breath The sensation of not getting enough air.
    • Swelling (oedema) Excess fluid in the tissues.
    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.

    Medical tests

    • BNP and NT-proBNP test A blood test for the natriuretic peptides the heart's ventricles release when they are stretched by high pressure or extra volume.
    • Echocardiogram An ultrasound of the beating heart.
    • Electrocardiogram Ten sticky electrodes on the chest and limbs record the heart's electrical activity from twelve angles over ten seconds.

    Biomarkers

    • Cardiac troponin A protein of the contractile apparatus of heart muscle cells that leaks into blood when those cells are injured.
    • Creatinine A waste product of muscle metabolism produced at a steady rate and cleared almost entirely by the kidneys.

    Medications

    • Furosemide A powerful loop diuretic that makes the kidneys excrete salt and water within an hour, used to clear fluid overload in heart failure.
    • Ramipril An ACE inhibitor that relaxes blood vessels and eases the heart's workload, used for high blood pressure, heart failure.
    • Losartan An angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) that relaxes blood vessels to lower blood pressure and protect the kidneys in diabetes.
    Educational content. A biomarker page explains what a substance is and why its level can change. It cannot determine the cause of your result: interpret a result using the range supplied by the laboratory that performed the test, with the clinician who ordered it.
