    Total cholesterol

    Also known as: cholesterol, serum cholesterol, TC

    The sum of all the cholesterol carried in blood by lipoproteins: LDL, HDL and the remainder. One line of a lipid profile, useful mainly as the starting point for the ratios and the non-HDL value that guide cardiovascular risk assessment.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy
    Also known as
    cholesterol, serum cholesterol, TC
    Units
    mmol/L (UK) or mg/dL (US); 1 mmol/L ≈ 38.7 mg/dL
    Measured by
    Lipid profile, Thyroid function tests

    What it is and what it does

    Cholesterol is a waxy molecule every cell needs for its membranes and for making steroid hormones, bile acids and vitamin D. It does not dissolve in water, so it travels in blood packaged with proteins as lipoproteins. The total is dominated by LDL cholesterol, the fraction deposited in artery walls, and HDL cholesterol, the fraction being carried back to the liver. A high total is therefore not always harmful (a large HDL share is protective) and a normal total can hide a poor pattern, which is why risk calculators use the ratio of total to HDL and guidelines increasingly track non-HDL cholesterol instead.

    Why it may be higher

    • Diet high in saturated fat, excess weight, inactivity
    • Familial hypercholesterolaemia and other inherited lipid disorders
    • Hypothyroidism, kidney disease with heavy proteinuria, cholestatic liver disease
    • Pregnancy; some medicines (corticosteroids, some antiretrovirals, isotretinoin)

    Why it may be lower

    • Hyperthyroidism, malnutrition, malabsorption
    • Severe acute illness, cancer, advanced liver disease
    • Lipid-lowering treatment

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Acute illness, surgery and heart attack lower cholesterol for several weeks
    • Weight change, alcohol and pregnancy
    • Fasting is no longer required for most purposes but changes triglycerides more than cholesterol
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    Medicines that can change it

    AtorvastatinRosuvastatin

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.

    Symptoms

    • Chest pain Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen.

    Medical tests

    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.
    • Thyroid function tests TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together.

    Biomarkers

    • LDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by low-density lipoprotein, the particle that deposits cholesterol in artery walls and drives atherosclerosis.
    • HDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by high-density lipoprotein, the particle that collects cholesterol from tissues and artery walls and returns it to the liver.
    • Triglycerides The main form in which fat is stored and transported: three fatty acids on a glycerol backbone.
    • TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) The pituitary hormone that drives the thyroid gland.

    Medications

    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.
    • Rosuvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by more, milligram for milligram, than any other statin.

    Guides and news

    • High cholesterol: diet, tests and medicines The NHS says cutting down on saturated fat can help lower cholesterol.
    • Cholesterol test results explained A cholesterol test reports total, HDL and non-HDL cholesterol plus triglycerides, and doctors read them with other risk factors.
    Educational content. A biomarker page explains what a substance is and why its level can change. It cannot determine the cause of your result: interpret a result using the range supplied by the laboratory that performed the test, with the clinician who ordered it.
