    LDL cholesterol

    Also known as: LDL, LDL-C, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, bad cholesterol

    The cholesterol carried by low-density lipoprotein, the particle that deposits cholesterol in artery walls and drives atherosclerosis. The main target of statin treatment and the lipid value most closely tied to heart attack and stroke risk.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy
    Also known as
    LDL, LDL-C, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, bad cholesterol
    Units
    mmol/L (UK) or mg/dL (US); usually calculated rather than measured directly
    Measured by
    Lipid profile

    What it is and what it does

    The liver packages triglycerides and cholesterol into VLDL particles; as tissues strip out the triglyceride, the particles shrink into cholesterol-rich LDL. LDL delivers cholesterol to cells through LDL receptors, but when particles are numerous or the artery wall is damaged they enter the wall, are oxidised and are engulfed by macrophages that become the foam cells of a fatty plaque. Statins lower LDL by making the liver display more LDL receptors. Laboratories usually calculate LDL from total cholesterol, HDL and triglycerides (the Friedewald equation), which becomes unreliable when triglycerides are high; non-HDL cholesterol (total minus HDL) avoids that problem and is the value UK guidance uses for treatment targets.

    Why it may be higher

    • Familial hypercholesterolaemia (very high levels from childhood, often with family history of early heart disease)
    • Saturated-fat-rich diet, obesity, inactivity
    • Hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, diabetes
    • Some medicines (corticosteroids, ciclosporin, some antipsychotics)

    Why it may be lower

    • Lipid-lowering treatment (statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors)
    • Hyperthyroidism, malabsorption, severe illness, advanced liver disease
    • Rare inherited disorders of lipoprotein synthesis

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Very high triglycerides make the calculated LDL invalid
    • Acute illness lowers the level for weeks
    • Fasting state changes the triglyceride input to the calculation
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    Medicines that can change it

    AtorvastatinRosuvastatin

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.

    Symptoms

    • Chest pain Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen.

    Medical tests

    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.

    Biomarkers

    • Total cholesterol The sum of all the cholesterol carried in blood by lipoproteins: LDL, HDL and the remainder.
    • HDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by high-density lipoprotein, the particle that collects cholesterol from tissues and artery walls and returns it to the liver.
    • Triglycerides The main form in which fat is stored and transported: three fatty acids on a glycerol backbone.

    Medications

    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.
    • Rosuvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by more, milligram for milligram, than any other statin.

    Guides and news

    • High cholesterol: diet, tests and medicines The NHS says cutting down on saturated fat can help lower cholesterol.
    • Cholesterol test results explained A cholesterol test reports total, HDL and non-HDL cholesterol plus triglycerides, and doctors read them with other risk factors.
