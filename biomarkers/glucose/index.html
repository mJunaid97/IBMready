    Biomarker · Glucose

    Blood glucose

    Also known as: blood sugar, plasma glucose, serum glucose

    The concentration of glucose, the body's main circulating fuel, in blood at the moment of sampling. Kept within narrow limits by insulin and glucagon, it rises after meals and in diabetes and falls dangerously in hypoglycaemia.

    Open in the 3D explorerPancreas anatomy
    Also known as
    blood sugar, plasma glucose, serum glucose
    Units
    mmol/L (UK) or mg/dL (US); 1 mmol/L = 18 mg/dL
    Measured by
    Blood glucose, HbA1c, Urinalysis

    What it is and what it does

    Glucose enters blood from the gut after meals and from the liver between them; insulin from the pancreas moves it into muscle and fat and switches off liver production, while glucagon, adrenaline and cortisol raise it. A fasting sample, a random sample and a sample two hours after a measured glucose drink each answer a different question, which is why the timing must be recorded with the result. Laboratories measure plasma glucose from a fluoride tube; fingerprick meters and continuous monitors estimate it from capillary blood or tissue fluid and read slightly differently.

    Why it may be higher

    • Diabetes and prediabetes
    • Acute illness, infection, heart attack, surgery (stress hyperglycaemia)
    • Corticosteroids and some other medicines
    • Cushing syndrome, pancreatitis, pancreatic cancer

    Why it may be lower

    • Too much insulin or a sulfonylurea relative to food and activity
    • Alcohol, especially without food
    • Severe liver disease, adrenal insufficiency, sepsis
    • Insulin-producing tumours (rare); reactive hypoglycaemia after gastric surgery

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Time since the last meal or drink and what it contained
    • Exercise, illness, stress and sleep
    • A delay between sampling and analysis lowers the value unless a fluoride tube is used
    • Meter readings differ from laboratory plasma values
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    LisinoprilMetforminParacetamol

    Conditions

    • Type 1 diabetes An autoimmune disease in which the insulin-producing beta cells of the pancreas are destroyed.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • Pancreatitis Inflammation of the pancreas, acute when its own enzymes are activated inside the gland.
    • Sepsis Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated body response to infection.
    • Polycystic ovary syndrome A common hormonal condition in which the ovaries make more androgens (hormones such as testosterone, which everyone makes.

    Symptoms

    • Dizziness An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint.
    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.
    • Weakness Loss of muscle power, distinct from tiredness or heaviness.

    Medical tests

    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • HbA1c The fraction of haemoglobin that has glucose stuck to it.
    • Urinalysis A plastic strip with chemical pads dipped into a fresh urine sample.

    Biomarkers

    • HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) Haemoglobin that has glucose attached to it.

    Medications

    • Metformin The first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it reduces the glucose the liver releases and improves the body's response to insulin.
    • Insulin The hormone that moves glucose from the blood into cells, given by injection or pump to everyone with type 1 diabetes and to many with type 2 when…
    • Prednisolone and steroids A corticosteroid tablet that suppresses inflammation and the immune system, used in asthma flare-ups, arthritis.
    • Lisinopril An ACE inhibitor that lowers blood pressure, protects the heart after a heart attack and in heart failure.
    • Paracetamol The first-line painkiller and fever reducer for most people (acetaminophen in North America).

    Guides and news

    • Eating well with type 2 diabetes Diabetes UK says there is no special diet only for type 2 diabetes, and NICE advises the same healthy balanced eating as for everyone.
    • HbA1c test: what it shows and why it matters The HbA1c test shows average blood glucose over the past two to three months and is used to diagnose and monitor diabetes.
    Educational content. A biomarker page explains what a substance is and why its level can change. It cannot determine the cause of your result: interpret a result using the range supplied by the laboratory that performed the test, with the clinician who ordered it.
