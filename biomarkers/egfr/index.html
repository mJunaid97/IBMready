    eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate)

    Also known as: eGFR, estimated GFR, glomerular filtration rate, GFR

    An estimate of how many millilitres of blood the kidneys filter each minute, calculated from creatinine together with age and sex. The number used to stage chronic kidney disease and to adjust drug doses.

    Also known as
    eGFR, estimated GFR, glomerular filtration rate, GFR
    Units
    mL/min/1.73 m²
    Measured by
    Kidney function tests, Creatinine and eGFR, Urine protein

    What it is and what it does

    The glomerular filtration rate is the best overall measure of kidney function but cannot be measured directly in routine care, so laboratories estimate it from serum creatinine using equations (the CKD-EPI equation in current UK and US practice; the 2021 version no longer includes a race term) that adjust for age and sex. The result is expressed per 1.73 m² of body surface area. An eGFR persistently below 60 for more than three months, or any value with markers of kidney damage such as albumin in the urine, defines chronic kidney disease, staged from G1 to G5. The estimate is least reliable at extremes of muscle mass, in pregnancy, in acute illness where creatinine is still changing, and near the normal range, where a cystatin C-based estimate can confirm the finding.

    Why it may be higher

    • A high value is normal in young adults and in pregnancy; falsely high estimates occur with low muscle mass (the equation assumes average muscle)

    Why it may be lower

    • Chronic kidney disease of any cause (diabetes, hypertension, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidneys, obstruction)
    • Acute kidney injury (the estimate lags behind the true fall because creatinine takes time to accumulate)
    • Falsely low with high muscle mass, creatine supplements, a recent meat meal, or drugs that block creatinine secretion

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Everything that changes creatinine independently of filtration
    • Which equation the laboratory uses
    • Steady-state assumption: the estimate is invalid while kidney function is changing rapidly
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    Conditions

    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • High blood pressure (hypertension) Persistently raised arterial pressure, usually defined as 140/90 mmHg or more in the clinic (135/85 on home readings).
    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.

    Symptoms

    • Swelling (oedema) Excess fluid in the tissues.
    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.

    Medical tests

    • Kidney function tests Creatinine and urea are waste products the kidneys should clear; how much builds up in blood shows how well they are filtering.
    • Creatinine and eGFR The everyday measure of how well the kidneys filter.
    • Urine protein Healthy kidney filters hold back almost all protein.

    Biomarkers

    • Creatinine A waste product of muscle metabolism produced at a steady rate and cleared almost entirely by the kidneys.
    • Urea The nitrogen-containing waste the liver makes from protein breakdown, cleared by the kidneys.
    • Potassium The main positively charged ion inside cells; only about 2% is in the blood, but that small fraction sets the electrical excitability of nerves.

    Procedures

    • Dialysis Dialysis takes over the kidneys' filtering work when they have failed.

    Medications

    • Metformin The first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it reduces the glucose the liver releases and improves the body's response to insulin.
    • Lisinopril An ACE inhibitor that lowers blood pressure, protects the heart after a heart attack and in heart failure.
    • Losartan An angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) that relaxes blood vessels to lower blood pressure and protect the kidneys in diabetes.
    • Ibuprofen A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) that eases pain, inflammation and fever by blocking prostaglandin production.
    • Rosuvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by more, milligram for milligram, than any other statin.

    Guides and news

    • eGFR and creatinine: kidney test results Creatinine and eGFR show how well the kidneys filter blood, and kidney disease is confirmed only when abnormal results persist.
