    TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)

    Also known as: TSH, thyrotropin, thyroid stimulating hormone

    The pituitary hormone that drives the thyroid gland. Because the pituitary responds to tiny changes in thyroid hormone, TSH moves in the opposite direction to thyroid function and is the single most sensitive test of thyroid status.

    Also known as
    TSH, thyrotropin, thyroid stimulating hormone
    Units
    mU/L (mIU/L)
    Measured by
    TSH test, Thyroid function tests

    What it is and what it does

    The hypothalamus releases TRH, the pituitary responds with TSH, and TSH tells the thyroid to make thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3); T4 and T3 then feed back to switch TSH off. When the thyroid fails, T4 falls and TSH climbs, often to many times the upper limit; when the thyroid is overactive, TSH is suppressed towards zero. The relationship is logarithmic, so TSH changes a great deal for a small change in T4, which is why it is the screening test and the value used to adjust levothyroxine. It takes about six weeks to settle after a dose change. TSH is unreliable when the pituitary itself is diseased, in the recovery phase of severe illness, and in the first trimester of pregnancy, when the placental hormone hCG mimics it.

    Why it may be higher

    • Primary hypothyroidism: autoimmune (Hashimoto) thyroiditis, after thyroid surgery or radioiodine, iodine deficiency
    • Under-replacement with levothyroxine, or poor absorption of it (taken with food, iron, calcium, PPIs)
    • Subclinical hypothyroidism (raised TSH with normal free T4)
    • Recovery from severe illness, some medicines (amiodarone, lithium), rarely a TSH-secreting pituitary tumour

    Why it may be lower

    • Hyperthyroidism: Graves disease, toxic nodules, thyroiditis
    • Over-replacement with levothyroxine
    • Pituitary or hypothalamic disease (secondary hypothyroidism: low TSH with low T4)
    • First trimester of pregnancy, severe non-thyroidal illness, high-dose steroids and dopamine

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations. The result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    What can affect the result

    • Time of day (highest overnight), acute illness, pregnancy
    • Biotin supplements interfere with some assays
    • Levothyroxine dose timing and adherence
    • Age: the reference interval rises in older adults
    Reference ranges. Reference intervals for this marker depend on the laboratory, the assay, the units, age, sex and pregnancy. Interpret a result against the interval printed on the laboratory report, not against a figure from the internet.

    Conditions

    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.
    • Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism) Excess thyroid hormone, most often from Graves' disease, in which antibodies stimulate the thyroid to overproduce.
    • Atrial fibrillation A common heart rhythm disorder in which the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting, producing an irregular and often fast pulse.
    • Osteoporosis Loss of bone mass and deterioration of bone architecture that makes bones fragile and prone to fracture after minimal trauma.

    Symptoms

    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.
    • Weakness Loss of muscle power, distinct from tiredness or heaviness.

    Medical tests

    • TSH test The single most sensitive blood test of thyroid function: TSH from the pituitary rises when the thyroid is underactive and falls when it is…
    • Thyroid function tests TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together.

    Biomarkers

    • Free T4 (thyroxine) The unbound fraction of thyroxine, the main hormone the thyroid gland secretes.
    • Total cholesterol The sum of all the cholesterol carried in blood by lipoproteins: LDL, HDL and the remainder.
    • Oestradiol The main oestrogen of the reproductive years, made chiefly by the growing ovarian follicle and, in smaller amounts.

    Medications

    • Levothyroxine Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism.
    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.

    Guides and news

    • Levothyroxine: timing and other medicines The NHS says levothyroxine replaces a missing thyroid hormone and is taken at the same time each day on an empty stomach.
    • Thyroid blood tests: TSH and T4 explained Thyroid tests read TSH and free T4 together, because free T4 helps to interpret an abnormal TSH.
    • Underactive and overactive thyroid An underactive thyroid does not make enough thyroid hormone, while an overactive thyroid makes too much; the symptoms, causes and treatments differ.
