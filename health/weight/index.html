    Body weight

    Also known as: obesity, BMI, waist circumference, weight loss, overweight

    Body fat is an active endocrine organ. Excess fat, particularly around the abdominal organs, releases inflammatory signals and free fatty acids that make tissues resistant to insulin, raise blood pressure and cholesterol, load the joints and narrow the airway in sleep. Losing 5-10% of body weight measurably improves all of these.

    Energy balance is regulated by the hypothalamus, which reads leptin from fat stores, insulin, and gut hormones such as ghrelin (hunger, from the stomach) and GLP-1 (fullness, from the small intestine). Modern food environments overwhelm these signals with energy-dense, highly palatable food and reduced movement; genetics sets how strongly each person's appetite responds. Weight is therefore not simply willpower, and biology fights weight loss by increasing hunger and lowering energy expenditure.

    Where fat sits matters more than how much. Visceral fat packed around the liver, pancreas and intestines drains straight into the portal vein and drives fatty liver, insulin resistance and inflammation; fat under the skin of the hips is metabolically quieter. Waist circumference above 94 cm in men or 80 cm in women marks raised risk, and BMI thresholds are lower in South Asian and some other populations.

    Weight loss of 5-10% reduces blood pressure, HbA1c and liver fat, eases knee pain and sleep apnoea, and a 15 kg loss can put type 2 diabetes into remission in about half of people within six years of diagnosis. Sustainable approaches combine reduced energy intake, protein and fibre to preserve fullness, resistance exercise to keep muscle, sleep and behavioural support; GLP-1 medicines and bariatric surgery act on the same hormone pathways for those with obesity-related disease.

    Effects on the body

    SystemEffect
    EndocrineInsulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome; reversed in part by weight loss
    ArteriesHigher blood pressure and LDL, more coronary disease and stroke
    Joints & ligamentsEach kilogram of body weight adds about 4 kg of load across the knee when walking; osteoarthritis risk rises steeply
    DigestiveFatty liver disease (now the commonest liver condition), gallstones, reflux, and higher risk of bowel, liver and oesophageal cancer
    RespiratoryObstructive sleep apnoea and breathlessness from reduced lung volumes

    Guidance

    • Know your numbers: BMI (weight in kg divided by height in m squared; 25-30 overweight, over 30 obesity, lower cut-offs of 23 and 27.5 for South Asian, Chinese, Black African and Middle Eastern backgrounds) and waist circumference
    • Aim first for 5-10% loss over 3-6 months, about 0.5-1 kg a week, through a sustained energy deficit of around 600 kcal a day
    • Prioritise protein, vegetables and fibre; cut sugary drinks, alcohol and ultra-processed snacks; keep regular meals
    • Combine with 150 minutes of activity and twice-weekly strength work to protect muscle; sleep 7-9 hours
    • Seek structured support (group programmes, dietitian); medication or surgery are options for obesity with related disease, prescribed and monitored by specialists

    Conditions

    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • High blood pressure (hypertension) Persistently raised arterial pressure, usually defined as 140/90 mmHg or more in the clinic (135/85 on home readings).
    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Osteoarthritis The commonest joint disease: progressive loss of articular cartilage with changes in the underlying bone, causing pain and stiffness.
    • Gallstones Hard deposits, usually of cholesterol, that form in the gallbladder when bile becomes supersaturated.
    • Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease Reflux of stomach acid into the oesophagus often enough to cause symptoms or damage the lining.
    • Cirrhosis Scarring of the liver from long-term damage, in which nodules of regenerating cells are surrounded by fibrous bands that distort blood flow and…
    • Colorectal (bowel) cancer A malignant tumour of the colon or rectum, almost always arising from a benign polyp (adenoma) over years.
    • Gout An inflammatory arthritis caused by deposition of uric acid crystals in joints, triggered when blood urate is persistently high.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.

    Symptoms

    • Knee pain Pain in or around the knee, the body's largest joint, where the femur, tibia and patella meet.
    • Shortness of breath The sensation of not getting enough air.
    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.
    • Back pain Pain in the spine or the muscles beside it, most often the lower back.

    Medical tests

    • HbA1c The fraction of haemoglobin that has glucose stuck to it.
    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.
    • Liver function tests A panel of enzymes and proteins that together show whether liver cells are injured, whether bile is flowing.

    Biomarkers

    • ALT (alanine aminotransferase) An enzyme concentrated in liver cells that leaks into blood when they are injured.
    • GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase) A membrane enzyme of bile-duct and liver cells that rises with cholestasis, alcohol and many medicines.
    • HDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by high-density lipoprotein, the particle that collects cholesterol from tissues and artery walls and returns it to the liver.
    • Triglycerides The main form in which fat is stored and transported: three fatty acids on a glycerol backbone.

    Guides and news

    • Eating well with type 2 diabetes Diabetes UK says there is no special diet only for type 2 diabetes, and NICE advises the same healthy balanced eating as for everyone.
    • Healthy weight: what BMI shows and what it misses BMI is an NHS check on whether weight suits height, but it cannot tell muscle from fat, so waist size and a GP discussion add to it.
    • Heartburn, acid reflux and GORD Heartburn is a burning feeling in the chest from acid reflux; when it keeps happening it is called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD).
    • How to read food labels: traffic lights Red, amber and green traffic light labels show whether a food is high, medium or low in fat, saturates, sugars and salt.
    • MHRA authorises orforglipron (Foundayo) The MHRA authorised orforglipron (Foundayo), a GLP-1 tablet, for weight management and type 2 diabetes.
    • MHRA seizes £2m of fake weight loss pens The MHRA and West Midlands Police seized counterfeit weight loss pens worth about £2 million in Birmingham.
    • WHO issues first child obesity guidelines On 7 October 2026 the WHO issued its first global guidelines on obesity in children and adolescents, with one document for each age group.
