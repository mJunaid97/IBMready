    Smoking and vaping

    Also known as: tobacco, nicotine, quitting smoking, e-cigarettes, vaping, passive smoking

    Tobacco smoke delivers nicotine, an addictive stimulant, in a cloud of 7,000 chemicals including 70 known carcinogens, carbon monoxide and tar. It damages every organ it reaches: it is the leading preventable cause of cancer, heart disease and lung disease, and kills half of long-term users. Stopping at any age recovers much of the risk, and the fall begins within hours.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy

    In the lungs, hot smoke paralyses and then destroys the cilia that sweep mucus and microbes out of the airways, inflames the bronchi (chronic bronchitis), and the enzymes released by inflammatory cells digest the walls of the alveoli (emphysema), so gas exchange fails. Tar coats the lining and its carcinogens mutate the DNA of bronchial cells: lung cancer risk is 15-30 times that of a never-smoker, and cancers of the mouth, throat, oesophagus, bladder, pancreas, kidney, stomach, cervix and bowel are all increased.

    Carbon monoxide occupies haemoglobin, cutting oxygen delivery; nicotine drives adrenaline, raising heart rate and blood pressure; and the chemicals injure the endothelium lining every artery, letting cholesterol enter the wall and making blood stickier. The result is atherosclerosis in the coronary, carotid, cerebral and leg arteries, doubling heart attack and stroke risk and causing most cases of peripheral arterial disease and aortic aneurysm.

    Nicotine reaches the brain in ten seconds and releases dopamine in the reward pathway; receptors multiply, so the brain demands nicotine to feel normal and withdrawal brings irritability, restlessness and craving. That biology, not weakness, is why stopping is hard and why nicotine replacement, varenicline and behavioural support together triple the chance of success. E-cigarettes deliver nicotine without combustion and are substantially less harmful than smoking, which makes them a useful quitting tool for smokers, but they are not harmless and are not for non-smokers or young people.

    Effects on the body

    SystemEffect
    RespiratoryCOPD (smoking causes 9 in 10 cases), lung cancer, pneumonia, worse asthma; passive smoke causes the same diseases in others
    ArteriesAtherosclerosis: heart attack, stroke, aneurysm, peripheral arterial disease, erectile dysfunction
    DigestiveCancers of the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, pancreas, bowel; peptic ulcers; Crohn disease flares
    UrinaryBladder and kidney cancer; faster decline in kidney disease
    SkeletonOsteoporosis, slower fracture and wound healing
    ReproductiveReduced fertility, miscarriage, stillbirth, low birth weight, sudden infant death

    Guidance

    • There is no safe level of smoking; stopping completely is the goal, and the benefits start within a day (carbon monoxide gone), weeks (breathing and circulation improve), a year (heart attack risk halved) and a decade (lung cancer risk halved)
    • Use support: stop-smoking services, nicotine replacement (patch plus a fast-acting form), varenicline or cytisine, or switching fully to vaping
    • Set a quit date, remove tobacco and lighters, plan for triggers, and treat a lapse as a lesson rather than a failure
    • Keep homes and cars smoke-free to protect children
    • Smokers aged 55-74 are offered lung cancer screening with low-dose CT in a growing number of countries

    Conditions

    • Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) Progressive, largely irreversible airflow obstruction caused by damage to the airways (chronic bronchitis) and destruction of alveoli (emphysema).
    • Lung cancer A malignant tumour arising from the lining of the airways or alveoli, divided into non-small-cell (about 85%) and small-cell types.
    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Peptic ulcer A break in the lining of the stomach or duodenum that extends through the mucosa, caused by acid acting on a weakened defence.
    • Osteoporosis Loss of bone mass and deterioration of bone architecture that makes bones fragile and prone to fracture after minimal trauma.
    • Pneumonia Infection of the alveoli, which fill with fluid and inflammatory cells so that part of the lung cannot exchange gas.
    • Colorectal (bowel) cancer A malignant tumour of the colon or rectum, almost always arising from a benign polyp (adenoma) over years.
    • Asthma A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways causing reversible narrowing, with episodes of wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough.

    Symptoms

    • Cough A reflex blast of air that clears the airways of mucus, irritants and foreign material.
    • Shortness of breath The sensation of not getting enough air.
    • Chest pain Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen.

    Medical tests

    • Spirometry A forced breath out into a tube measures how much air the lungs can move and how fast.
    • Pulse oximetry and blood gases A clip on the fingertip shines red and infrared light through the tissue and reads the colour of pulsing blood to estimate how saturated haemoglobin…
    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.

    Biomarkers

    • HDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by high-density lipoprotein, the particle that collects cholesterol from tissues and artery walls and returns it to the liver.

    Imaging

    • CT scan A rotating X-ray tube and computer build cross-sectional slices of the body in seconds, showing bone, organs.
    • X-ray The oldest and most widely available imaging test: a brief beam of X-rays casts a shadow picture of bone, air and soft tissue, ideal for fractures.

    Guides and news

    • Asthma and COPD: how they differ Asthma is a long-term airway condition whose symptoms come and go; COPD is mostly smoking-related and tends to get worse over time.
    • Under-18 ban on vapes and nicotine in force New rules took effect on 29 October 2026 making it illegal to sell any vape or non-medicinal nicotine product to anyone under 18.
    • Looking after your feet with diabetes Diabetes can reduce feeling in the feet and slow healing, so UK sources describe daily checks and an annual foot check.
    • General anaesthetic: before, during, after A general anaesthetic puts a person into controlled unconsciousness for surgery.
    • Heartburn, acid reflux and GORD Heartburn is a burning feeling in the chest from acid reflux; when it keeps happening it is called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD).
    • Looking after your teeth and gums The NHS describes brushing with fluoride toothpaste twice a day, cleaning between the teeth and cutting down on sugary food and drink.
    • Stopping smoking: the support the NHS offers NHS stop smoking services offer nicotine replacement, medicines and professional support.
    • WHO urges action on youth nicotine use On 29 May 2026, ahead of World No Tobacco Day, the WHO urged governments to protect young people from tobacco and nicotine addiction.
