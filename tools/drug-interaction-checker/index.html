    Drug Interaction Checker

    Check medicines for known interactions and understand what they may mean, how serious they may be, and what monitoring or professional advice may be relevant.

    Interaction information is educational and does not replace advice from a doctor, pharmacist, or other qualified healthcare professional.

    Before you use this tool. This checker cannot account for your doses, medical history, kidney or liver function, pregnancy, allergies or every product on the market. It shows what the cited official sources say about a pair of medicines; it does not decide whether a combination is right for you.

      How the checker works

      1. Names become ingredients. A brand name maps to its generic medicine; a combination product (for example co-codamol) is split into each active ingredient; a named substance without a page on this site is matched by name.
      2. Every pair is checked. For the medicines you add, every unique pair is compared against the sourced interaction records: medicine–medicine records, records written for a whole drug class (applied only to its members, as the source states), and duplicate ingredients or therapeutic duplication.
      3. Severity comes from the source. Each record carries the state its cited document's wording supports (contraindicated, avoid, specialist supervision, monitor or adjust, documented); the checker maps those states onto its display tiers and never grades an interaction from its own reading. Nothing is labelled minor by inference.
      4. Results are shown highest severity first, then the pairs with no known interaction in the available data, the related anatomy, tests and classes, and every source used.

      About our interaction data

      Source
      BNF (NICE), NHS, MedlinePlus (NIH), NICE, UK Government, paraphrased into records that each cite the document and section they came from
      Coverage
      72 interaction records · 39 medicines with pages · 14 brand and combination products · 73 named substances · 27 drug classes
      Last updated
      12 September 2026
      Review status
      Facts checked against the cited sources; not yet clinically reviewed

      The checker can detect medicine-to-medicine interactions recorded in the official information of the medicines represented on this site, duplicate active ingredients across brands and combination products, and therapeutic duplication within a drug class where official guidance advises against the combination. It cannot detect interactions that are not in the dataset, interactions with food, alcohol, supplements, diseases, pregnancy, allergies or laboratory tests, or the effect of a particular dose, route, age or kidney and liver function. Interaction databases differ from one another; a pair that is not found here may still interact.
