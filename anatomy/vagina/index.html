    Vagina Anatomy

    Also known as: birth canal, vaginal

    The vagina is a fibromuscular canal about 8 to 10 cm long that runs upwards and backwards from the vulva to the cervix, lying between the bladder and urethra in front and the rectum behind. It carries menstrual blood out of the body, receives the penis and semen during intercourse and stretches to become the birth canal, and its oestrogen-dependent lining keeps itself acidic and largely self-cleaning. The 3D model is the vagina of the NIH Human Reference Atlas fitted into the reference pelvis between the bladder and urethra and the rectum.

    Key facts

    LengthAbout 8 to 10 cm; the anterior wall is shorter than the posterior because the cervix enters the upper end at an angle
    LiningNon-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium, rich in glycogen under oestrogen; the vagina has no glands of its own
    AciditypH about 3.8 to 4.5 in the reproductive years, maintained by lactobacilli
    RelationsBladder base and urethra in front, rectum and pouch of Douglas behind, levator ani on each side, perineal body below
    Blood supplyVaginal branches of the uterine artery, the vaginal artery and branches of the internal pudendal artery
    Nerve supplyUpper part autonomic via the uterovaginal plexus, lower part somatic via the pudendal nerve (S2-S4)
    LymphUpper part to the internal and external iliac nodes, lowest part to the superficial inguinal nodes

    Location and relations

    The vagina runs upwards and backwards from its opening in the vestibule of the vulva to the cervix, which projects into its upper end. The recess around the cervix is the fornix, with anterior, posterior and two lateral parts; the posterior fornix is the deepest and is separated by only a thin wall from the rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas), so fluid collecting in the pelvis can be felt or drained through it. In front lie the base of the bladder and the urethra, embedded in the anterior vaginal wall. Behind lie the pouch of Douglas above, the rectum in the middle and the perineal body below, which separates the lower vagina from the anal canal. The levator ani muscles hug the canal on each side, and the ureters pass close to the lateral fornices, where they are at risk in pelvic surgery.

    Structure and layers

    The wall has four layers. The lining is a non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium that thickens and stores glycogen under oestrogen; the vagina has no glands of its own, and its moisture comes from fluid seeping through the wall, cervical mucus and the vestibular glands at the opening. Beneath it the lamina propria carries a dense venous plexus, then a muscularis of inner circular and outer longitudinal smooth muscle, and an outer adventitia that blends with the pelvic fascia. Transverse ridges called rugae let the canal distend in intercourse and childbirth. At the opening the hymen, a thin fold of mucosa of very variable shape, partly narrows the entrance in early life; it stretches or tears with tampon use, exercise or intercourse, and small remnants persist in adults. Its appearance varies widely and is not a reliable indication of whether a person has had sex. Lactobacilli ferment the glycogen of shed epithelial cells into lactic acid, producing the acidity that restrains other organisms.

    Blood supply and lymph

    Arterial blood comes from branches of the internal iliac artery: vaginal branches of the uterine artery to the upper part, the vaginal artery (from the internal iliac or the uterine artery) to the middle, and branches of the internal pudendal and middle rectal arteries to the lower part. Their anastomoses form longitudinal channels, the azygos arteries, on the anterior and posterior walls. Blood drains into a vaginal venous plexus in the lamina propria that joins the uterine plexus and empties into the internal iliac veins. Lymph from the upper vagina drains, like that of the cervix, to the internal and external iliac nodes; lymph from the lowest part, below the hymen, drains with the vulva to the superficial inguinal nodes in the groin. The split matters in cancer staging: a tumour of the lower vagina spreads to the groin nodes, one of the upper vagina into the pelvis.

    Innervation

    The upper three-quarters of the vagina are supplied by autonomic fibres from the uterovaginal plexus, with sympathetic fibres from the lower thoracic and upper lumbar cord and parasympathetic fibres from the pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2-S4). This part is sensitive to stretch but poorly sensitive to touch and temperature, which is why a speculum or a pessary high in the vagina is felt as pressure rather than touch. The lowest part, near the opening, is supplied by the somatic pudendal nerve (S2-S4) through its deep perineal branch and is richly sensitive to touch, pain and temperature. A pudendal nerve block therefore numbs the lower vagina and perineum for an instrumental delivery or the repair of a tear, whereas labour pain from the cervix and upper vagina travels with the autonomic fibres and is felt deep in the pelvis and low back.

    Function

    The vagina has four main roles. It carries menstrual blood and cervical mucus out of the body; the discharge changes through the cycle, becoming clear and stretchy around ovulation. During sexual arousal its walls lubricate with fluid transuded through the epithelium, the upper part lengthens and widens, and the lower third and the vestibule carry most of the sensation; semen is deposited near the cervix. In labour it stretches to several times its resting width to form the birth canal, the rugae flattening out, and recovers over the following weeks. Throughout the reproductive years it maintains its own microbiome: oestrogen drives glycogen into the epithelial cells, shed cells feed lactobacilli, and the lactic acid they make keeps the pH at about 3.8 to 4.5, which suppresses many bacteria and yeasts. Douching and scented washes disturb this balance and are not needed; the vagina cleans itself.

    Changes across life

    The lining depends on oestrogen. Before puberty the epithelium is thin, the pH near neutral and lactobacilli sparse; at puberty the epithelium thickens, glycogen accumulates, the pH falls and a physiological discharge begins. Pregnancy increases blood flow and softens the tissues, and vaginal birth stretches the canal and perineum; tears are graded from first degree (skin and vaginal lining) through second (perineal muscles) to third and fourth degree, which reach the anal sphincter and anal lining. After the menopause the loss of oestrogen thins the epithelium, reduces lactobacilli and raises the pH, causing dryness, soreness, painful sex, urinary symptoms and recurrent urinary tract infections: the genitourinary syndrome of menopause, which vaginal oestrogen relieves. Trans men and non-binary people taking testosterone can develop similar atrophy, because the therapy suppresses ovarian oestrogen production, and topical vaginal oestrogen can be used alongside testosterone without reversing masculinising effects. Vaginoplasty, carried out for trans women and some non-binary people and also for congenital absence of the vagina, constructs a neovagina from penile and scrotal skin, peritoneum or bowel; a skin-lined canal does not self-lubricate or carry the same flora, and it needs a lifelong dilation routine.

    Clinical relevance

    Thrush is an overgrowth of Candida causing itch and a thick white discharge; bacterial vaginosis is a shift away from lactobacilli that raises the pH and produces a thin, fishy-smelling discharge. Weakness of the pelvic floor and vaginal walls after childbirth and the menopause lets the bladder bulge into the anterior wall (cystocele), the rectum into the posterior wall (rectocele) or the uterus or vault descend: pelvic organ prolapse, often with urinary incontinence, managed with pelvic floor muscle training, pessaries, vaginal oestrogen or surgery. A fistula between the vagina and the bladder or rectum can follow obstructed labour, surgery or radiotherapy and causes continuous leakage. Vaginal oestrogen may also be considered after the menopause for women, and anyone with a vagina, who have recurrent urinary tract infections. The vagina is also the route for speculum examination and cervical screening, transvaginal ultrasound, vaginal hysterectomy and prolapse repair. Vaginal cancer is rare, but bleeding after sex or after the menopause always needs examination.

    About

    A fibromuscular canal about 8 to 10 cm long running up and back from the vulva to the cervix, between the bladder and urethra in front and the rectum behind, with an acidic, oestrogen-dependent lining.

    Function. The passage for menstrual blood, the organ of intercourse and the birth canal.

    Clinical note. Thrush and bacterial vaginosis alter its flora; prolapse of its walls follows childbirth and ageing; its lining thins after the menopause (genitourinary syndrome of menopause).

    The female reproductive in brief

    This layer brings the female body into an atlas whose reference model is male. Its 49 pieces come from the united female model of the Human Reference Atlas (HRA), the NIH-funded reference built from the Visible Human Female, scaled and placed so that the pubic symphysis, sacral promontory and hip bones of the female pelvis coincide with those of the BodyParts3D pelvis; the breasts sit on the reference chest wall at the level of the fourth to fifth rib. The uterus lies behind and above the bladder and in front of the rectum, tilted forward; the cervix opens into the top of the vagina, which runs down and back to the perineum; the uterine tubes arch from the upper corners of the uterus out over the ovaries, which lie against the side walls of the pelvis; and the broad, round, cardinal and uterosacral ligaments and the suspensory and ovarian ligaments hold everything in place. Each breast is shown as its body (fat and gland), the lobes of the mammary gland, the lactiferous ducts and sinuses, the suspensory ligaments, the areola with its tubercles and the nipple. Because two bodies share one frame, the female organs overlap the male bladder and prostate in places; switching this layer on hides the male reproductive organs, and the Female body preset shows the whole body with the female organs. The vulva and clitoris are not part of the HRA model and remain a written page.
