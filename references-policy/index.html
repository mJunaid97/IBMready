    References policy

    Where our information comes from, how sources are ranked and how they are shown.

    Source hierarchy

    Each reference on the site is tagged with a source and an evidence tier, shown next to it in the Sources list:

    TierSources
    Tier 1National health services and agencies (NHS, NHS England, WHO, NICE, NIH institutes, MedlinePlus, CDC, FDA), official clinical guidelines (Resuscitation Council UK, GINA) and professional societies (American Heart Association, Society for Endocrinology, British Burn Association, World Stroke Organization).
    Tier 2Peer-reviewed journals, medical textbooks (the open OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology), academic medical centres and university resources.
    Tier 3High-quality secondary resources, chiefly patient charities (British Heart Foundation, Stroke Association, Asthma + Lung UK, British Liver Trust, Epilepsy Action, St John Ambulance). Used for context and patient-facing guidance, not as the sole basis for a major clinical claim.

    Rules

    • Every reference is a first-class object with a title, a source, a tier, a URL and the date it was checked; references are never pasted as unstructured text.
    • Only https links to the original publisher are accepted; the build rejects anything else.
    • Medicines are sourced first from official medicines information (NHS medicines pages, FDA labelling and DailyMed, MedlinePlus) and guidelines; textbooks support foundational pharmacology. We do not reproduce copyrighted textbook text or diagrams.
    • Reference ranges are never presented as universal; where shown, pages say that values vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context.
    • Links are checked automatically for availability; broken links are fixed or replaced in the next release.
