    Biological target · Transporter

    Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (NKCC2)

    Also known as: NKCC2, SLC12A1, loop of Henle sodium transporter, bumetanide-sensitive cotransporter

    The transporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle that reabsorbs sodium, potassium and chloride from the filtrate. Loop diuretics such as furosemide block it, so the kidney passes far more salt and water, relieving the fluid overload of heart failure.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy
    Kind
    transporter
    Also called
    NKCC2, SLC12A1, loop of Henle sodium transporter, bumetanide-sensitive cotransporter
    Where it is found
    Apical membrane of the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle in the kidney; a related isoform is in the inner ear.

    What it does

    About a quarter of the sodium the kidney filters is reclaimed in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle by NKCC2, which carries one sodium, one potassium and two chloride ions together from the tubular fluid into the cell. Because this segment is impermeable to water, the salt it removes also builds the concentration gradient in the kidney medulla that later allows urine to be concentrated. Loop diuretics reach the transporter from the tubular side (they are secreted into the tubule by the proximal cells, so they still work when filtration is poor) and block it; salt that stays in the tubule drags water with it, the medullary gradient collapses, and a large, dilute diuresis follows within an hour of a dose. The lost potassium, magnesium and calcium explain the electrolyte disturbances of loop diuretics, the fall in blood volume explains dizziness and rising creatinine, and the related NKCC1 transporter in the inner ear accounts for their rare ototoxicity at high doses. NSAIDs blunt the effect by reducing the prostaglandins that support it.

    Role in the body

    Reabsorbs about 25% of filtered sodium and chloride and generates the medullary concentration gradient; also reabsorbs potassium, calcium and magnesium indirectly.

    Conditions

    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Cirrhosis Scarring of the liver from long-term damage, in which nodules of regenerating cells are surrounded by fibrous bands that distort blood flow and…

    Symptoms

    • Swelling (oedema) Excess fluid in the tissues.
    • Shortness of breath The sensation of not getting enough air.
    • Dizziness An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint.

    Biomarkers

    • Potassium The main positively charged ion inside cells; only about 2% is in the blood, but that small fraction sets the electrical excitability of nerves.
    • Sodium The main positively charged ion of the fluid outside cells and the chief determinant of blood osmolality.
    • Creatinine A waste product of muscle metabolism produced at a steady rate and cleared almost entirely by the kidneys.

    Medications

    • Furosemide A powerful loop diuretic that makes the kidneys excrete salt and water within an hour, used to clear fluid overload in heart failure.

    Drug classes

    • Loop diuretics Powerful diuretics that make the kidneys excrete salt and water within an hour.
