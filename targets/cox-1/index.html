    Cyclo-oxygenase 1 (COX-1)

    Also known as: COX-1, prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 1, PTGS1

    The 'housekeeping' form of cyclo-oxygenase, present all the time in platelets, the stomach lining and the kidney. It makes the thromboxane that lets platelets clump and the prostaglandins that protect the stomach and keep the kidneys perfused; aspirin and other NSAIDs block it.

    Open in the 3D explorerStomach anatomy
    Kind
    enzyme
    Also called
    COX-1, prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 1, PTGS1
    Where it is found
    Platelets, the gastric and duodenal mucosa, the kidney and vascular endothelium; present in most tissues at a basal level.

    What it does

    Cyclo-oxygenases convert arachidonic acid released from cell membranes into the prostaglandins and thromboxane that act as short-range messengers. COX-1 is constitutive: it is always present and supplies the everyday prostaglandins that keep the stomach lining coated in mucus and bicarbonate, maintain blood flow through the kidney when the body is stressed, and, in platelets, make thromboxane A2, the signal that recruits other platelets to a clot. Low-dose aspirin acetylates COX-1 irreversibly; because platelets have no nucleus they cannot replace the enzyme, so a small daily dose suppresses thromboxane for the platelet's whole life. Non-selective NSAIDs such as ibuprofen block COX-1 reversibly, which explains their shared risks: stomach ulcers and bleeding, reduced kidney perfusion in the dehydrated, and a temporary antiplatelet effect that can interfere with aspirin's.

    Role in the body

    Platelet aggregation (thromboxane A2), gastric mucosal protection and renal blood flow (prostaglandins).

    Conditions

    • Peptic ulcer A break in the lining of the stomach or duodenum that extends through the mucosa, caused by acid acting on a weakened defence.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.

    Biomarkers

    • Platelets Small cell fragments that plug damaged vessel walls and start the clotting cascade.

    Medications

    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Ibuprofen A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) that eases pain, inflammation and fever by blocking prostaglandin production.

    Drug classes

    • Antiplatelet drugs Medicines that stop platelets clumping into the clots that block arteries.
    • NSAIDs Painkillers that also reduce inflammation and fever: ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and the COX-2 selective coxibs.
