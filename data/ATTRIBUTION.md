# Attribution and data licences

## BodyParts3D (geometry, structure names, anatomical hierarchy)

The 3D geometry in `data/hd/glb`, `data/lite/glb`, the piece and structure lists in
`atlas.json`, and the concept hierarchy are derived from **BodyParts3D** release 4.0
(is-a tree, `isa_BP3D_4.0_obj_99`).

> BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, licensed under
> [CC Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
> https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/ · DOI 10.18908/lsdba.nbdc00837-000

Please cite: Mitsuhashi N, Fujieda K, Tamura T, Kawamoto S, Takagi T, Okubo K.
*BodyParts3D: 3D structure database for anatomical concepts.* Nucleic Acids Res.
2009;37(Database issue):D782–5. https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkn613

Changes made: meshes were welded, re-oriented to glTF conventions (y up), simplified with
meshoptimizer under a 0.2 mm (HD) or 0.6 mm (lite) error bound, and packed into per-system
glTF files with quantised, meshopt-compressed attributes. Pieces were grouped into systems,
organs and regions by rules described in `tools/classify.py` and `content/`.

Release 3.0 tables (`conventional_part_of.txt`, FMA.csv) were used during classification to
derive organ-system membership.

## Human Reference Atlas (the female body layer)

The `reproductive-female` system (`glb/reproductive-female.glb`, pieces `HF0001`–`HF0049`: uterus,
cervix, vagina, uterine tubes, ovaries and their ligaments, and the breasts) is derived from the
**3D Reference Organ Library** of the **Human Reference Atlas (HRA)**, united female model v1.5,
built by the HuBMAP Consortium with NIH funding from the Visible Human Female dataset of the U.S.
National Library of Medicine.

> Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Organ Library, HuBMAP Consortium / NIH, licensed under
> [CC Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
> https://humanatlas.io/ · meshes derived from the Visible Human Female (NLM)

The meshes were taken from the Anatria-3D redistribution of that model
(https://github.com/Nurkan1/Anatria-3D, `public/anatomy/*_female.glb`, Draco-compressed, CC BY 4.0).
Changes made: the reproductive and breast meshes were decoded, scaled and placed in the BodyParts3D
frame (the female pelvis fitted to the male pubic symphysis, sacral promontory and hip bones; the
breasts fitted to the reference chest wall and scaled to sit on it), re-wound where the source
winding was inward, simplified and packed like the male systems. Structure names follow
Terminologia Anatomica; concept ids are the UBERON or FMA ids the HRA assigns. The mapping and the
fit are in `tools/manifest/female-source.json` and `tools/build-female-atlas.mjs`. Two round-ligament
nodes are named for the wrong side in the source; sides here follow the geometry.

## Schematic anatomy (the gender-affirming surgery layer)

The `gender-affirming` system (`glb/gender-affirming.glb`, pieces `GA0001`–`GA0012`) is not derived
from any scan: it is procedural geometry generated by `tools/build-female-atlas.mjs` from the
dimensions in `tools/manifest/female-source.json`, drawn to typical post-operative anatomy described
in the surgical literature and placed in the reference pelvis. It is released under CC BY 4.0 with
the rest of this project's own work.

## Foundational Model of Anatomy (names, concept ids)

Structure names and FMA identifiers come from the Foundational Model of Anatomy ontology
(University of Washington), distributed with BodyParts3D.
