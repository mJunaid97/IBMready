    Kidney function tests

    Also known as: U&Es, urea and electrolytes, renal function, creatinine, eGFR

    Creatinine and urea are waste products the kidneys should clear; how much builds up in blood shows how well they are filtering. Creatinine is converted into an estimated filtration rate (eGFR), the standard measure of kidney function.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    CreatinineMuscle waste cleared by filtration; rises as kidney function falls. Depends on muscle mass.
    eGFREstimated glomerular filtration rate in mL/min/1.73 m²; over 90 normal, under 60 for three months defines chronic kidney disease, under 15 is kidney failure.
    UreaProtein waste; rises with poor kidney function but also dehydration, bleeding into the gut and high protein intake.
    Sodium and potassiumUsually reported together; potassium climbs dangerously when kidneys fail.

    Why it is ordered

    • Screening people with diabetes or high blood pressure
    • Before contrast scans and many drugs
    • Swelling, reduced urine, tiredness, nausea
    • Dehydration, vomiting, diarrhoea
    • Monitoring ACE inhibitors, diuretics, lithium and NSAIDs

    How it is done

    Venous blood; usually paired with a urine protein test.

    Preparation. None; avoid heavy exercise or a large meat meal the day before if possible.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Rising creatinine over hours to daysAcute kidney injury: dehydration, sepsis, obstruction, drugs; usually reversible if caught
    eGFR below 60 on repeated testsChronic kidney disease, staged 3-5 by the value
    High urea, normal creatinineDehydration or gut bleeding rather than kidney damage

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    eGFR is an estimate; it is inaccurate at extremes of muscle mass, in pregnancy and in rapidly changing illness. Half of kidney function can be lost before creatinine leaves the normal range.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
