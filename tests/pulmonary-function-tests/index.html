    Full pulmonary function tests

    Also known as: PFTs, lung function tests, gas transfer, lung volumes, body plethysmography

    Spirometry plus measurements it cannot make: the total volume of the lungs including the air that cannot be blown out, and how easily gas crosses from the air sacs into blood (gas transfer, DLCO). Together they classify almost any lung disease.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Total lung capacity and residual volumeMeasured in a sealed booth (plethysmography) or by gas dilution; high in emphysema (air trapping), low in fibrosis.
    Gas transfer (DLCO)How much of a trace of carbon monoxide crosses into blood in one breath; reduced in emphysema, fibrosis and pulmonary vascular disease, raised in lung bleeding.
    Flow-volume loopThe shape reveals upper airway obstruction and the site of narrowing.
    Respiratory muscle pressuresFor suspected neuromuscular weakness.

    Why it is ordered

    • Unexplained breathlessness with normal spirometry
    • Interstitial lung disease and sarcoidosis
    • Assessing fitness for lung resection or chemotherapy (bleomycin)
    • Neuromuscular disease affecting breathing
    • Grading emphysema

    How it is done

    Sitting in a clear booth breathing through a mouthpiece with a nose clip through a series of manoeuvres, about an hour.

    Preparation. As for spirometry; avoid smoking for 24 hours because carbon monoxide affects gas transfer.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Obstruction with high residual volume and low DLCOEmphysema
    Restriction with low DLCOPulmonary fibrosis or other interstitial disease
    Restriction with normal DLCOChest wall, obesity or muscle weakness rather than lung parenchyma
    Low DLCO with normal volumesPulmonary vascular disease, early fibrosis, anaemia

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Anaemia lowers DLCO; results are compared with predicted values that depend on age, sex, height and ethnicity.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
