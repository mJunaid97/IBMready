    Urine culture

    Also known as: MSU, midstream urine, urine microscopy, culture and sensitivity

    A urine sample is spread on culture plates and incubated for 24-48 hours to grow any bacteria, identify them and test which antibiotics kill them. It turns a dipstick suspicion into a proven infection with a targeted treatment.

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Colony count100,000 or more colony-forming units per mL of a single organism is significant; lower counts count in symptomatic patients and catheter samples.
    OrganismE. coli causes most infections; Proteus suggests stones; Staphylococcus saprophyticus in young women; mixed growth usually means contamination.
    SensitivitiesWhich antibiotics the organism is sensitive or resistant to.

    Why it is ordered

    • Infection in men, children, pregnancy and anyone with a catheter or kidney disease
    • Infections that keep coming back or fail treatment
    • Suspected kidney infection or sepsis from a urinary source
    • Before urological surgery

    How it is done

    Midstream sample in a sterile pot (or from a fresh catheter); refrigerate if it cannot reach the lab within hours.

    Preparation. Collect before starting antibiotics whenever possible.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Pure growth of E. coli, sensitive to nitrofurantoinConfirmed infection; treat with the narrowest effective antibiotic
    Mixed growthProbably contaminated; repeat with careful collection
    Growth without symptomsAsymptomatic bacteriuria: treat only in pregnancy or before urological procedures

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Results take two days, so treatment often starts empirically. Antibiotics taken beforehand can sterilise the sample.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
