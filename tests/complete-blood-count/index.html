    Full blood count

    Also known as: complete blood count, CBC, FBC

    The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample, with the size and haemoglobin content of the red cells.

    Open in the 3D explorerSpleen anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    HaemoglobinThe oxygen-carrying protein; low = anaemia, high = polycythaemia.
    Red cell size (MCV)Small cells point to iron deficiency, large cells to B12 or folate deficiency or alcohol.
    White cell count and differentialNeutrophils rise with bacterial infection and steroids, lymphocytes with viral infection, eosinophils with allergy and parasites.
    PlateletsClotting cells; low counts bleed, very high counts may clot or signal inflammation.

    Why it is ordered

    • Tiredness, pallor or breathlessness (anaemia)
    • Suspected infection or inflammation
    • Unexplained bruising or bleeding
    • Monitoring chemotherapy and many long-term drugs
    • Routine pre-operative check

    How it is done

    A few millilitres of blood from an arm vein into a tube with an anticoagulant; results within hours.

    Preparation. None.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Low haemoglobin with small red cellsIron deficiency, usually from blood loss (gut, periods) or poor intake
    High neutrophilsBacterial infection, inflammation, steroids or stress
    Low plateletsImmune destruction, marrow problems, liver disease, some drugs
    Everything low (pancytopenia)Bone marrow failure or infiltration, needs urgent specialist review

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Tells you that something is wrong, not what: an abnormal count is a prompt for further tests such as iron studies, a blood film or marrow examination.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
