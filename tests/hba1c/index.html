    HbA1c

    Also known as: glycated haemoglobin, A1c

    The fraction of haemoglobin that has glucose stuck to it. Because red cells live about 120 days, it reports the average blood glucose over the previous two to three months in a single number.

    Open in the 3D explorerPancreas anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    HbA1cReported in mmol/mol (and %). Below 42 (6.0%) normal; 42-47 (6.0-6.4%) prediabetes; 48 (6.5%) or above diabetes.

    Why it is ordered

    • Diagnosing type 2 diabetes
    • Monitoring diabetes control every 3-6 months
    • Setting and reviewing treatment targets

    How it is done

    Venous blood, no fasting.

    Preparation. None.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    48 mmol/mol or aboveDiabetes (if no condition affecting red cells)
    Target 48-58 in treated diabetesIndividualised: tighter in the young and newly diagnosed, looser in the frail
    Falling valueTreatment and lifestyle changes are working

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Unreliable when red cell life is abnormal: anaemia, haemoglobin variants, recent transfusion, pregnancy, kidney failure. Not for diagnosing type 1 diabetes, which moves too fast.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
