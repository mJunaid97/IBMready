    Urine protein

    Also known as: ACR, albumin:creatinine ratio, PCR, microalbumin, proteinuria test

    Healthy kidney filters hold back almost all protein. Measuring albumin leaking into urine, corrected for how dilute the sample is, detects kidney damage years before blood tests change, particularly in diabetes and high blood pressure.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Albumin:creatinine ratio (ACR)Below 3 mg/mmol normal; 3-30 moderately raised (formerly microalbuminuria); above 30 severe. Combined with eGFR to stage kidney disease.
    Protein:creatinine ratioAll proteins, used when large amounts leak (nephrotic range above 300 mg/mmol).
    24-hour collectionThe old gold standard, now rarely needed.

    Why it is ordered

    • Yearly in diabetes
    • High blood pressure, kidney disease, lupus
    • Frothy urine or unexplained swelling
    • Pregnancy with high blood pressure (pre-eclampsia)
    • Staging chronic kidney disease and deciding on ACE inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors

    How it is done

    An early-morning spot urine sample.

    Preparation. Avoid hard exercise the day before; repeat if there is infection or fever.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    ACR persistently 3 or moreKidney damage: start or intensify an ACE inhibitor/ARB, control blood pressure and glucose
    Nephrotic-range protein with low albumin and swellingNephrotic syndrome, needs a kidney biopsy in adults
    Protein with bloodGlomerulonephritis; urgent specialist referral

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Exercise, fever, infection and standing for long periods raise protein transiently; two out of three samples must be positive to confirm.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. This page cannot interpret an individual result.
