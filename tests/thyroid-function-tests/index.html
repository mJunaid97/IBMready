    Thyroid function tests

    Also known as: thyroid panel, thyroid blood test, free T4 and TSH

    TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together. Because the pituitary reacts to tiny changes in hormone level, TSH is the most sensitive single indicator of thyroid status.

    Why is it used?

    • Tiredness, weight change, feeling cold or hot, palpitations, anxiety
    • A goitre or neck lump
    • Atrial fibrillation, high cholesterol, depression
    • Monitoring levothyroxine or antithyroid drugs
    • Amiodarone or lithium treatment, pregnancy planning

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations; the result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    How is it performed?

    Venous blood, any time of day (take levothyroxine after the sample).

    Specimen. Venous blood (serum)

    Do I need to prepare?

    None; biotin supplements can interfere and should be paused for two days.

    What do the results generally mean?

    FindingWhat it may indicate
    High TSH, low T4Primary hypothyroidism
    High TSH, normal T4Subclinical hypothyroidism: usually confirmed by repeating the test after a few weeks to months; whether to treat depends on the TSH level, age, symptoms, antibodies and pregnancy
    Low TSH, high T4 or T3Hyperthyroidism: Graves disease, toxic nodules, thyroiditis
    Low TSH and low T4Pituitary (secondary) failure, or recent overtreatment
    Read against the laboratory's own reference interval. Each hormone has a laboratory reference interval that depends on the assay and, for TSH, on age and pregnancy. The combination of values is what matters: a single hormone outside its interval is interpreted with the others.

    What can affect the result?

    • Acute and severe illness (sick euthyroid pattern)
    • Biotin supplements interfering with the assay
    • Pregnancy and the first trimester rise in hCG
    • Amiodarone, lithium, glucocorticoids, dopamine, some antiepileptics
    • Levothyroxine timing and absorption

    Medicines. Levothyroxine and antithyroid drugs are the intended influences; amiodarone, lithium, interferons and immune checkpoint inhibitors can cause thyroid dysfunction; iron, calcium and proton pump inhibitors reduce levothyroxine absorption.

    What can the test not tell you?

    • The cause of thyroid dysfunction without antibodies, ultrasound or uptake scans
    • Whether a nodule is present or cancerous
    • Thyroid status reliably during severe non-thyroidal illness

    Acute illness and some drugs disturb results ('sick euthyroid'); repeat once recovered. TSH lags six weeks behind a dose change.

    Conditions

    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.
    • Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism) Excess thyroid hormone, most often from Graves' disease, in which antibodies stimulate the thyroid to overproduce.
    • Atrial fibrillation A common heart rhythm disorder in which the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting, producing an irregular and often fast pulse.
    • Osteoporosis Loss of bone mass and deterioration of bone architecture that makes bones fragile and prone to fracture after minimal trauma.
    • Alzheimer's disease The commonest cause of dementia: a progressive loss of memory and thinking ability caused by the build-up of amyloid plaques and tau tangles and the…
    • Anaemia A haemoglobin concentration below normal (roughly under 130 g/L in men and 120 g/L in women), reducing the blood's capacity to carry oxygen.
    • Anxiety and generalised anxiety disorder Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) is a long-term condition in which a person feels worried or anxious about many different things, much of the time.
    • Polycystic ovary syndrome A common hormonal condition in which the ovaries make more androgens (hormones such as testosterone, which everyone makes.
    • Thyroid cancer Thyroid cancer is a cancer that starts in the thyroid gland, a gland in the neck that makes hormones.

    Symptoms

    • Fatigue Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix.
    • Weakness Loss of muscle power, distinct from tiredness or heaviness.
    • Dizziness An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint.
    • Heavy periods Menstrual bleeding that is heavier or lasts longer than usual and gets in the way of daily life.
    • Numbness Reduced or absent feeling in part of the body.
    • Tingling (pins and needles) Prickling, buzzing or crawling sensations without an outside cause, produced when sensory nerves fire abnormally.

    Medical tests

    • TSH test The single most sensitive blood test of thyroid function: TSH from the pituitary rises when the thyroid is underactive and falls when it is…
    • Hormone tests Blood levels of hormones and related chemicals, interpreted against the time of day.
    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.

    Biomarkers

    • TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) The pituitary hormone that drives the thyroid gland.
    • Free T4 (thyroxine) The unbound fraction of thyroxine, the main hormone the thyroid gland secretes.
    • Total cholesterol The sum of all the cholesterol carried in blood by lipoproteins: LDL, HDL and the remainder.

    Imaging

    • Ultrasound High-frequency sound waves bounced off tissue give real-time, radiation-free images, used for pregnancy, the abdomen, the heart (echocardiography).

    Medications

    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.
    • Levothyroxine Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism.
    • Rosuvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by more, milligram for milligram, than any other statin.

    Guides and news

    • Levothyroxine: timing and other medicines The NHS says levothyroxine replaces a missing thyroid hormone and is taken at the same time each day on an empty stomach.
    • Numbness and tingling: causes and when to act Pins and needles are usually brief and harmless, but numbness or tingling that lasts, keeps returning or comes with weakness needs checking.
    • Thyroid blood tests: TSH and T4 explained Thyroid tests read TSH and free T4 together, because free T4 helps to interpret an abnormal TSH.
    • Tiredness and fatigue: causes and next steps Tiredness is common and often has an ordinary cause such as poor sleep, stress or illness.
    • Underactive and overactive thyroid An underactive thyroid does not make enough thyroid hormone, while an overactive thyroid makes too much; the symptoms, causes and treatments differ.
    • Unexplained weight loss: causes and next steps Losing weight without changing diet or exercise has many causes, from stress to thyroid or digestive conditions.
    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. This page cannot interpret an individual result.
