    Lipid profile

    Also known as: cholesterol test, lipid panel

    Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good', carried away for disposal) and triglycerides. Used mainly to estimate and manage the risk of heart attack and stroke.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Total cholesterolIdeally below 5 mmol/L for the general population.
    LDL cholesterolThe particle that builds atherosclerotic plaque; the main treatment target.
    HDL cholesterolHigher is protective; above 1.0 in men and 1.2 in women.
    TriglyceridesFat from food and the liver; very high levels can cause pancreatitis.
    Non-HDL cholesterolTotal minus HDL: all the harmful particles together.

    Why it is ordered

    • Cardiovascular risk assessment from age 40, earlier with family history
    • Known heart disease, stroke or peripheral artery disease
    • Diabetes, kidney disease, high blood pressure
    • Monitoring statin treatment
    • Xanthomas or very early heart disease in a family (familial hypercholesterolaemia)

    How it is done

    Venous blood, fasting no longer required for most purposes.

    Preparation. Usually none; a fasting sample if triglycerides are very high.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    High LDL and non-HDLRaised risk; combined with age, blood pressure and smoking into a 10-year risk score to decide on statins
    Total cholesterol above 7.5 with family historyPossible familial hypercholesterolaemia; genetic testing and family screening
    Very high triglyceridesRisk of pancreatitis; look for alcohol, diabetes, drugs

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Cholesterol is one risk factor among several; the numbers matter less than the overall risk. Acute illness lowers cholesterol for weeks.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
