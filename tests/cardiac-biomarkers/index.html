    Cardiac biomarkers

    Also known as: troponin, BNP, NT-proBNP, cardiac enzymes

    Proteins released into blood by heart muscle: troponin when heart cells die, and natriuretic peptides (BNP) when the ventricles are stretched by high pressure. Troponin is the test that diagnoses a heart attack; BNP is the test that screens for heart failure.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    High-sensitivity troponinStarts rising 1-3 hours after heart muscle injury, peaks at 24 hours and stays up for a week or more. A rise and fall pattern on two samples defines acute injury.
    BNP / NT-proBNPReleased by stretched ventricles; a normal level makes heart failure very unlikely. Used to triage breathlessness and to prioritise echocardiography.
    D-dimerNot a heart marker but often ordered alongside: a fibrin breakdown product used to rule out clots in low-risk patients.

    Why it is ordered

    • Chest pain or suspected heart attack (troponin on arrival and after 1-3 hours)
    • Breathlessness or ankle swelling where heart failure is possible (BNP)
    • Suspected myocarditis, pulmonary embolism, severe sepsis
    • Monitoring heart failure treatment

    How it is done

    Venous blood; a second troponin is timed from symptom onset or arrival.

    Preparation. None.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Troponin above the 99th percentile with a rise or fallAcute myocardial injury; with chest pain and ECG change, a myocardial infarction
    Chronically raised flat troponinKidney disease, heart failure, structural heart disease: injury without an acute event
    NT-proBNP over 2000 ng/LUrgent echocardiogram and specialist review within two weeks
    Normal BNPHeart failure very unlikely; look for another cause of breathlessness

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Troponin says the heart was injured, not why: pulmonary embolism, sepsis, kidney failure and strenuous exercise all raise it. BNP is lower in obesity and higher in kidney disease and atrial fibrillation.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
