    Ambulatory ECG monitoring

    Also known as: Holter monitor, 24-hour ECG, event recorder, loop recorder, patch monitor

    A portable recorder worn for 24 hours to two weeks (or an implanted loop recorder for up to three years) that captures the heart's rhythm during normal life, catching the intermittent arrhythmias a ten-second ECG misses.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Rhythm over timeEvery beat is recorded and analysed; runs of atrial fibrillation, pauses and extra beats are counted.
    Symptom correlationThe wearer presses a button or keeps a diary so symptoms can be matched to the tracing at that moment.
    Heart rate rangeSlowest and fastest rates, including during sleep.

    Why it is ordered

    • Palpitations that come and go
    • Unexplained blackouts or falls
    • Looking for atrial fibrillation after a stroke
    • Checking a pacemaker or drug treatment is controlling a rhythm
    • Suspected heart block

    How it is done

    Three to five chest electrodes lead to a small recorder on a belt, or a single adhesive patch; normal activities continue except bathing. Implantable recorders are injected under the skin of the chest.

    Preparation. None; keep a symptom diary.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Palpitations coincide with atrial fibrillationDiagnosis made; consider anticoagulation and rate or rhythm control
    Pause of more than 3 seconds with dizzinessSymptomatic bradycardia; likely pacemaker
    Symptoms with normal rhythmPalpitations are not due to an arrhythmia; reassuring

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Only useful if symptoms occur during the recording; rare events need longer monitoring or an implanted device.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. This page cannot interpret an individual result.
