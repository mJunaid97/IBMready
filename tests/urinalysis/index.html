    Urinalysis

    Also known as: urine dipstick, urine test, dipstick

    A plastic strip with chemical pads dipped into a fresh urine sample. In sixty seconds it screens for infection, blood, protein, glucose, ketones and how concentrated the urine is, with microscopy for cells and crystals when needed.

    Urine test
    Sample
    A midstream urine sample
    Used for
    Urinary infection, kidney disease, diabetes screening, pregnancy checks
    Measures
    Chemical pads for leucocytes, nitrite, blood, protein, glucose, ketones, concentration and acidity; cells and crystals on microscopy
    Result
    Semi-quantitative dipstick pads (negative to 3+), specific gravity and pH, plus microscopy when requested

    Why is it used?

    • Burning, frequency or urgency (suspected infection)
    • Abdominal or loin pain
    • Screening in diabetes, high blood pressure and pregnancy
    • Unexplained fever in children and older adults
    • Kidney disease monitoring

    A high or low value can be associated with several different situations; the result is interpreted with other results, symptoms, history and clinical findings.

    How is it performed?

    Midstream urine into a clean pot; dipped immediately or sent for microscopy and culture.

    Specimen. Midstream urine in a clean container, tested fresh

    Do I need to prepare?

    Clean the area and catch the middle of the stream; first-morning samples are most concentrated.

    What do the results generally mean?

    FindingWhat it may indicate
    Nitrites plus leucocytes with symptomsLikely urinary tract infection; a culture confirms the organism
    Blood and protein togetherGlomerular disease: needs kidney function tests and specialist review
    Glucose positiveCheck blood glucose for diabetes (or SGLT2 inhibitor treatment)
    Ketones with high glucosePossible diabetic ketoacidosis, an emergency
    Interpreted descriptively by a clinician, not against a numeric range. Dipstick results are graded rather than measured, and a positive pad is a screening finding: infection is confirmed by symptoms and culture, protein by a laboratory albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and blood by microscopy and, where persistent, further investigation.

    What can affect the result?

    • Contamination from the genital area or menstrual blood
    • Delay before testing (bacteria multiply, cells break down) and exposure of the strip to air
    • Very dilute or very concentrated urine
    • Vitamin C can cause false negatives for blood and glucose; some antibiotics and dyes discolour the sample
    • Vigorous exercise, fever and dehydration cause transient protein or blood

    Medicines. SGLT2 inhibitors put glucose in the urine by design; nitrofurantoin and some other drugs discolour it; vitamin C causes false negatives; NSAIDs and lithium can cause protein or blood.

    What can the test not tell you?

    • Whether bacteria in the urine are causing illness: older adults often carry bacteria without infection and should not be treated on the dipstick alone
    • How much protein is leaking (quantified by an albumin-to-creatinine ratio)
    • The cause of blood in the urine, which needs microscopy, imaging and sometimes cystoscopy

    Dipsticks in older adults often show bacteria without infection (asymptomatic bacteriuria) and should not drive antibiotics on their own. Menstrual blood and vigorous exercise cause false positives for blood.

    Conditions

    • Urinary tract infection Bacterial infection of the bladder (cystitis) or, when it ascends, the kidney (pyelonephritis), usually by gut bacteria such as Escherichia coli.
    • Kidney stones Hard crystals, most commonly calcium oxalate, that form in the kidney and can pass into and block the ureter.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Type 1 diabetes An autoimmune disease in which the insulin-producing beta cells of the pancreas are destroyed.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • High blood pressure (hypertension) Persistently raised arterial pressure, usually defined as 140/90 mmHg or more in the clinic (135/85 on home readings).
    • Appendicitis Acute inflammation of the appendix, usually because its opening into the caecum is blocked.
    • Bladder cancer Bladder cancer is cancer that forms in the tissues of the bladder, the organ that stores urine.
    • Endometriosis A chronic condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus (the endometrium) grows outside the uterus, most often on the ovaries.
    • Enlarged prostate (benign prostatic hyperplasia) An enlarged prostate is a common condition in which the prostate gland grows larger and can squeeze the urethra, the outlet tube for urine.

    Symptoms

    • Abdominal pain Pain between the ribs and the pelvis.
    • Back pain Pain in the spine or the muscles beside it, most often the lower back.
    • Fever A body temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or more, produced when the hypothalamus raises its set point in response to inflammatory signals.
    • Pelvic pain Pain felt low in the abdomen, below the navel and between the hip bones.

    Medical tests

    • Urine culture A urine sample is spread on culture plates and incubated for 24-48 hours to grow any bacteria, identify them and test which antibiotics kill them.
    • Urine protein Healthy kidney filters hold back almost all protein.
    • Kidney function tests Creatinine and urea are waste products the kidneys should clear; how much builds up in blood shows how well they are filtering.
    • Pregnancy test Detects human chorionic gonadotropin, a hormone made by the placenta from about a week after implantation.
    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • Creatinine and eGFR The everyday measure of how well the kidneys filter.

    Biomarkers

    • Albumin The most abundant protein in blood, made by the liver.
    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose, the body's main circulating fuel, in blood at the moment of sampling.
    • Bilirubin The yellow pigment made when old red cells are broken down, processed by the liver and excreted in bile.

    Procedures

    • Phalloplasty and metoidioplasty Construction of a penis, most often as gender-affirming surgery for trans men and some non-binary people assigned female at birth.
    • Urinary catheterisation A soft tube passed along the urethra (or through the lower abdomen) into the bladder drains urine when the bladder cannot empty or output must be…
    • Vaginoplasty Construction of a vagina and vulva, most often as gender-affirming surgery for trans women and some non-binary people.

    Medications

    • Amoxicillin A widely used penicillin antibiotic that kills bacteria by breaking their cell walls, prescribed for chest, ear, sinus.
    • Insulin The hormone that moves glucose from the blood into cells, given by injection or pump to everyone with type 1 diabetes and to many with type 2 when…
    • Rosuvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by more, milligram for milligram, than any other statin.

    Guides and news

    • Blood in urine: causes and what happens next Blood in the urine is not usually serious, but the NHS advises seeing a GP every time, even if the amount is small.
    • Cystoscopy: what happens and what to expect A cystoscopy uses a thin camera passed through the urethra to look inside the bladder.
    • Kidney stones: symptoms and treatment Kidney stones form when chemicals in urine build up; small ones may cause no symptoms, while larger ones can cause severe pain.
    • Pelvic pain in women: causes and when to act Pelvic pain in women often comes from period pain, ovarian cysts or endometriosis, but a few causes are serious.
    • UTI and cystitis: what they are A urinary tract infection (UTI) can affect the bladder, urethra or kidneys; cystitis is a UTI of the bladder, and antibiotics are not always needed.
