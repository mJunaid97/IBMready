    Electrolytes

    Also known as: sodium and potassium, serum electrolytes, bone profile

    The dissolved salts that set the water content of the body and the electrical behaviour of nerves, muscle and the heart: sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, calcium, magnesium and phosphate.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy

    What it measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    SodiumGoverns water balance; 135-145 mmol/L. Low sodium is the commonest electrolyte disorder in hospital.
    Potassium3.5-5.0 mmol/L. High or low levels cause dangerous heart rhythms.
    BicarbonateThe blood's main buffer; low in acidosis, high in alkalosis.
    CalciumCorrected for albumin; high in parathyroid overactivity and cancer, low after thyroid surgery and in vitamin D deficiency.
    Magnesium and phosphateFall with malnutrition, alcohol and refeeding; matter for heart rhythm and muscle.

    Why it is ordered

    • Confusion, weakness, seizures, palpitations
    • Vomiting, diarrhoea, dehydration
    • Kidney disease and diuretic treatment
    • Before and after surgery
    • Endocrine disease (adrenal, parathyroid)

    How it is done

    Venous blood; potassium is falsely high if the sample is squeezed or delayed.

    Preparation. None.

    Reading the result

    FindingUsual meaning
    Low sodiumOften too much water relative to salt: diuretics, heart or liver failure, SIADH, excess drinking
    High potassiumKidney failure, ACE inhibitors and spironolactone, tissue breakdown; needs an ECG
    Low potassiumVomiting, diarrhoea, loop diuretics, steroid excess
    High calciumPrimary hyperparathyroidism or malignancy in most adults

    Reference ranges vary by laboratory, method, age, sex and clinical context; a result is read against the person, not a table.

    Limitations

    Blood levels do not always reflect total body stores (potassium is mostly inside cells). Haemolysed samples give spurious potassium.

    Educational content. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, methods, ages and sexes, and results are always read against the person, the question asked and other findings. Educational only.
