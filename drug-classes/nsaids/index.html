    NSAIDs

    Also known as: non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, anti-inflammatories

    Painkillers that also reduce inflammation and fever: ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and the COX-2 selective coxibs. Excellent for short-term musculoskeletal pain, hazardous when used long term without care.

    Open in the 3D explorerStomach anatomy
    Biological target
    Cyclo-oxygenase 1 (COX-1), Cyclo-oxygenase 2 (COX-2)
    Also called
    non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, anti-inflammatories

    Mechanism of action

    NSAIDs block the cyclo-oxygenase enzymes COX-1 and COX-2, cutting production of prostaglandins, the local messengers that sensitise pain nerves, dilate vessels in inflamed tissue and reset the brain's thermostat in fever. The same prostaglandins protect the stomach lining and keep blood flowing through the kidney, so ulcers, kidney injury, fluid retention and raised blood pressure are the class hazards. COX-2 selective agents spare the stomach somewhat but carry more cardiovascular risk.

    ibuprofennaproxendiclofenaccelecoxibetoricoxibhigh-dose aspirin

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    OsteoarthritisRheumatoid arthritisGoutAnterior cruciate ligament injuryMigrainePeptic ulcerHerniated (slipped) disc

    Class effects

    • Indigestion, ulcers and bleeding
    • Kidney injury, fluid retention, raised blood pressure
    • Worsening asthma in sensitive people

    Cautions

    • Lowest dose for the shortest time, with food
    • Avoid in kidney disease, heart failure, active ulcers, late pregnancy
    • Never two NSAIDs together; care with anticoagulants, ACE inhibitors, diuretics, lithium, methotrexate
    • Topical gels give local relief with far fewer side effects

    Class warnings

    • All NSAIDs increase the risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding, kidney injury, fluid retention, raised blood pressure and thrombotic cardiovascular events; the lowest effective dose for the shortest time, with gastroprotection where indicated. UK
    • Contraindicated with a history of NSAID-related gastrointestinal bleeding or perforation, active peptic ulcer, severe heart failure and in the third trimester of pregnancy; avoided in severe kidney or liver impairment and in aspirin-sensitive asthma. UK

    Interaction records that name this class

    Losartan + NSAIDs (class)

    ModerateMonitoring or dose adjustment advised

    Any NSAID (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib, high-dose aspirin) with losartan can reduce its effect and cause acute kidney injury and hyperkalaemia.

    Why it can occur
    nephrotoxicity
    What official information says
    UK information advises telling a doctor before taking anti-inflammatory painkillers with losartan; the BNF advises monitoring renal function.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Avoid regular use where possible; monitor kidney function and potassium if combined.
    What may be monitored
    Creatinine and eGFR · Potassium
    Basis of the status
    BNF: Losartan interactions (increased risk of renal impairment; monitor)

    Aspirin + NSAIDs (class)

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Any NSAID (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib) with aspirin adds gastrointestinal bleeding risk, and some blunt aspirin's antiplatelet effect.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information advises against taking ibuprofen or naproxen with aspirin without advice.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK information: avoid unless advised by a pharmacist or doctor.
    What may be monitored
    Signs of gastrointestinal bleeding
    Basis of the status
    NHS: Taking ibuprofen with other medicines (do not take with aspirin or naproxen without advice)

    Ibuprofen + NSAIDs (class)

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combinationtherapeutic duplication

    Taking ibuprofen with another NSAID (naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib, high-dose aspirin) adds stomach, kidney and heart harm without extra pain relief.

    Why it can occur
    additive bleeding risk
    What official information says
    UK information advises not taking ibuprofen with similar painkillers such as aspirin or naproxen without talking to a pharmacist or doctor.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK information: one NSAID at a time.
    Basis of the status
    NHS: Taking ibuprofen with other medicines (do not take with similar painkillers)
