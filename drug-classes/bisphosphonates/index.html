    Drug class · Skeleton

    Bisphosphonates

    Also known as: bone-strengthening drugs

    Medicines that slow bone loss by disabling the cells that dissolve bone, cutting hip and spine fractures in osteoporosis and treating Paget's disease and cancer in bone.

    Open in the 3D explorerVertebral column anatomy
    Biological target
    Farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase in osteoclasts
    Also called
    bone-strengthening drugs

    Mechanism of action

    Bisphosphonates bind bone mineral; when osteoclasts resorb that surface they absorb the drug, which disables their metabolism and triggers their death. Resorption falls while bone-building continues for a time, so density rises a few per cent and the bone's fine architecture is preserved. Weekly alendronate and risedronate, monthly ibandronate and yearly zoledronic acid infusions are the main forms.

    alendronic acidrisedronateibandronatezoledronic acidpamidronate

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    OsteoporosisBone fractureRheumatoid arthritis

    Class effects

    • Indigestion and oesophageal irritation with tablets
    • Flu-like illness after the first infusion
    • Rarely osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fractures

    Cautions

    • Tablets taken upright with a full glass of water, 30 minutes before food
    • Correct vitamin D and calcium first; dental check
    • Not with eGFR below 35
    • Review after 3-5 years

    Class warnings

    • Oral bisphosphonates can cause oesophageal irritation and ulceration; they are taken on an empty stomach with a full glass of water, standing or sitting upright for 30 minutes. Osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fractures are rare with long-term use; dental review is advised before starting. UK
    • Contraindicated in abnormalities of the oesophagus that delay emptying, in inability to stand or sit upright for 30 minutes, and in hypocalcaemia; vitamin D and calcium are corrected first. UK

    Class interactions

    WithEffectSource
    Calcium, iron, antacids, food and drink other than waterMarkedly reduce absorption; taken at least 30 minutes before anything else. UK
    NSAIDs and aspirinAdditive gastrointestinal irritation. UK
