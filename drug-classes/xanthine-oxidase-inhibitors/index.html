    Xanthine oxidase inhibitors

    Also known as: urate-lowering therapy, allopurinol and febuxostat

    Medicines that lower uric acid by blocking the enzyme that makes it, dissolving the crystals that cause gout attacks and tophi when taken every day.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy
    Biological target
    Xanthine oxidase
    Also called
    urate-lowering therapy, allopurinol and febuxostat

    Mechanism of action

    Xanthine oxidase converts hypoxanthine and xanthine into uric acid at the end of purine breakdown. Blocking it drops blood urate below its saturation point, so no new crystals form and existing deposits slowly dissolve. Attacks can increase for the first months as crystals mobilise, so an NSAID or colchicine is given as cover, and the dose is raised until the urate target is reached.

    Conditions treated

    GoutKidney stonesChronic kidney disease

    Class effects

    • Rash, nausea
    • Gout flares in the first months

    Cautions

    • Allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome, rare but severe; HLA-B*58:01 screening in higher-risk populations
    • Interaction with azathioprine and mercaptopurine
    • Febuxostat: cardiovascular caution

    Class warnings

    • Allopurinol can cause severe hypersensitivity (rash, fever, organ involvement), more often with kidney impairment, high starting doses and the HLA-B*58:01 allele common in some East Asian populations; any rash is a reason to stop. Starting treatment can provoke gout flares, so cover with an anti-inflammatory is given. UK

    Class interactions

    WithEffectSource
    Azathioprine and mercaptopurineXanthine oxidase inhibition greatly increases their levels and toxicity (bone-marrow suppression); doses are cut to a quarter or the combination avoided. UK
