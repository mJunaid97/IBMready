    Tricyclic antidepressants

    Also known as: tricyclics, TCAs, tricyclic antidepressant

    Tricyclic antidepressants are a group of medicines that includes amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine and clomipramine. They are used to treat depression, and amitriptyline is also used for pain relief at lower doses than for depression. StatPearls says they cause more significant side effects than SSRIs because of their anticholinergic activity and their lower threshold for overdose.

    Mechanism of action

    StatPearls says these medicines work by blocking the reuptake of neurotransmitters such as serotonin and noradrenaline. The NHS says amitriptyline is thought to work by increasing levels of serotonin in the brain. StatPearls links many of their side effects to anticholinergic activity, which for imipramine comes from blocking muscarinic receptors.

    Cautions

    • A 2024 review concluded that, with one exception, all tricyclics are toxic and dangerous when taken in overdose.
    • The exception named in that review is lofepramine, a tricyclic used in the UK, which had the lowest risk of death in overdose; the same review noted a greater risk of heart events with lofepramine at clinical exposure, and that the evidence is affected by confounding.
    • Anyone who has taken more than the prescribed amount of amitriptyline and has a fast or irregular heartbeat, a seizure, drowsiness or passing out, difficulty breathing or sudden confusion needs a 999 call, according to the NHS.
    • Some local NHS prescribing guidance advises considering limiting the amount prescribed each time because of the risk of toxicity in overdose.
    • MedlinePlus says a small number of children, teenagers and young adults up to 24 years of age who took antidepressants such as clomipramine in clinical studies became suicidal, especially at the start of treatment and when the dose changes.
    • The NHS says to call 999 or go to A&E now for thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life; NHS 111 is open 24 hours every day and Samaritans is a free line on 116 123.

    Class warnings

    • The NHS pain page says taking more than the prescribed amount of amitriptyline can cause serious effects such as a change in heartbeat and seizures (fits) and advises contacting NHS 111 for advice; the NHS depression page says to call 999 now if a fast or irregular heartbeat, a seizure, drowsiness or passing out, difficulty breathing or sudden confusion follows.
