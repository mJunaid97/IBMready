    ACE inhibitors

    Also known as: angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, the -prils

    Medicines ending in -pril that block the enzyme which makes angiotensin II, lowering blood pressure, unloading a failing heart and protecting the kidneys in diabetes.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy
    Biological target
    Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE)
    Also called
    angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, the -prils

    Mechanism of action

    The kidneys release renin when pressure or salt falls; renin makes angiotensin I, and angiotensin-converting enzyme in the lungs and vessels turns it into angiotensin II, which constricts arteries, releases aldosterone (salt and water retention) and thickens heart muscle. Blocking the enzyme removes all of these effects, and the pressure inside the kidney's filters falls, which slows the leak of protein in diabetic kidney disease. ACE also breaks down bradykinin, so it accumulates and causes the characteristic dry cough.

    Conditions treated

    High blood pressure (hypertension)Heart failureHeart attack (myocardial infarction)Chronic kidney diseaseType 2 diabetes

    Class effects

    • Dry cough in 10-15%
    • First-dose dizziness
    • Raised potassium and a small rise in creatinine

    Cautions

    • Never in pregnancy
    • Angio-oedema (swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat) is an emergency needing immediate help (call 999); an ACE inhibitor is not restarted afterwards and the prescriber chooses an alternative
    • Pause during vomiting, diarrhoea or dehydration to protect the kidneys
    • Avoid with potassium supplements and most NSAIDs

    Class warnings

    • Kidney function and potassium must be checked before starting, 1-2 weeks after starting and after each dose change; angioedema is a rare class effect that can occur at any time. UK
    • Contraindicated in pregnancy (fetal toxicity) and after angioedema with an ACE inhibitor; not combined with an ARB or, in diabetes, with aliskiren. UK

    Interaction records that name this class

    Losartan + ACE inhibitors (class)

    Contraindicated or avoidOfficial guidance: avoid the combination

    Losartan with any ACE inhibitor (ramipril, lisinopril, enalapril, perindopril) duplicates renin-angiotensin blockade: more hypotension, kidney injury and hyperkalaemia without extra benefit.

    Why it can occur
    hyperkalaemia risk · Additive blockade of the renin-angiotensin system: more hyperkalaemia, low blood pressure and kidney injury
    What official information says
    NICE: do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB to treat hypertension.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Official UK guidance: not recommended.
    What may be monitored
    Blood pressure · Potassium · Kidney function

    NICE NG136 1.4: do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB

    Conditions

    • High blood pressure (hypertension) Persistently raised arterial pressure, usually defined as 140/90 mmHg or more in the clinic (135/85 on home readings).
    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.

    Medications

    • Ramipril An ACE inhibitor that relaxes blood vessels and eases the heart's workload, used for high blood pressure, heart failure.
    • Lisinopril An ACE inhibitor that lowers blood pressure, protects the heart after a heart attack and in heart failure.

    Guides and news

    • ACE inhibitors, ARBs and amlodipine compared ARBs work much like ACE inhibitors but do not typically cause a dry cough; amlodipine relaxes artery walls and can cause ankle swelling.
    • High blood pressure: what happens next High blood pressure usually causes no symptoms and is found by measuring it; treatment starts with lifestyle changes and may include medicine.
