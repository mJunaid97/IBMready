    Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists

    Also known as: aldosterone antagonists, aldosterone receptor antagonists, MRAs

    Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, also called aldosterone antagonists, are medicines that act against the hormone aldosterone. Spironolactone and eplerenone are the two members covered here, and a Northern Care Alliance heart failure leaflet covers both.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy
    Biological target
    Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) receptor
    Also called
    aldosterone antagonists, aldosterone receptor antagonists, MRAs

    Mechanism of action

    A review of spironolactone and eplerenone says they increase the amount of sodium lost in the urine and cause the kidneys to hold on to potassium when aldosterone levels are high, which is why they are described as facultative diuretics. Eplerenone is more selective for the mineralocorticoid receptor, whereas StatPearls says spironolactone also binds progesterone and androgen receptors, which underlies its anti-androgen effects such as breast enlargement in men. A 2024 meta-analysis in people with heart failure found lower rates of gynaecomastia with eplerenone than with spironolactone.

    Conditions treated

    Heart failure

    Class effects

    • Hyperkalaemia (high blood potassium) and acute kidney injury are described as the predominant safety concerns across the mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in pharmacovigilance data
    • Gynaecomastia (breast enlargement in men) is reported more often with spironolactone than with eplerenone in a 2024 heart failure meta-analysis

    Cautions

    • US prescribing information for spironolactone says that potassium supplements, potassium-containing salt substitutes, and medicines that can raise potassium, such as ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs and heparin, may lead to severe hyperkalaemia
    • Potassium and kidney function are checked before treatment and monitored during treatment, according to NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service guidance for spironolactone and a review of eplerenone
