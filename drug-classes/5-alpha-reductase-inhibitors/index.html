    Drug class · Reproductive

    5-alpha reductase inhibitors

    Also known as: 5-ARIs, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors

    A group of medicines that stop testosterone turning into the hormone DHT; finasteride, described by the NHS for an enlarged prostate and male pattern hair loss, is one of them. The MHRA has issued safety warnings about psychiatric side effects and sexual dysfunction for finasteride and dutasteride.

    Open in the 3D explorerProstate anatomy
    Also called
    5-ARIs, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors

    Mechanism of action

    These medicines work by stopping testosterone, a sex hormone, turning into another hormone called dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

    Members of the class

    finasteridedutasteride

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Class effects

    • Sexual side effects such as losing interest in sex and difficulty getting an erection are the main side effects the NHS lists for finasteride, and some men report them continuing after stopping.
    • The MHRA says finasteride is associated with depression, suicidal thoughts and sexual dysfunction, and has added a precautionary warning for dutasteride.

    Cautions

    • Finasteride can affect PSA blood test results, so the NHS advises telling the doctor about it if a PSA test is due.
    • For depression or suicidal thoughts, MHRA guidance for finasteride is to contact a healthcare professional urgently; if someone is in immediate danger, call 999 or go to A&E.
