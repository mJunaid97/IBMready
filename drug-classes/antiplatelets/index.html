    Antiplatelet drugs

    Also known as: platelet inhibitors

    Medicines that stop platelets clumping into the clots that block arteries. The backbone of treatment after heart attacks, strokes and stents.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy
    Biological target
    Cyclo-oxygenase 1 (COX-1)
    Also called
    platelet inhibitors

    Mechanism of action

    Platelets plug damaged vessels by sticking to each other; in a diseased artery the plug becomes the clot that causes a heart attack or stroke. Low-dose aspirin permanently disables COX-1 in platelets so they cannot make thromboxane; clopidogrel, ticagrelor and prasugrel block the P2Y12 receptor that responds to ADP. After a stent both are used together for months, because a clot on the stent is a catastrophe.

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    Coronary artery diseaseHeart attack (myocardial infarction)Stroke

    Class effects

    • Bruising and prolonged bleeding
    • Stomach irritation and ulcers (aspirin)
    • Breathlessness with ticagrelor

    Cautions

    • Not for primary prevention in healthy people
    • Combined with an anticoagulant only on specialist advice
    • A proton pump inhibitor protects the stomach in those at risk

    Class warnings

    • Bleeding, especially gastrointestinal, is the main class hazard; a proton pump inhibitor is often co-prescribed for those at risk, and antiplatelets are not stopped before procedures without the prescriber's advice, particularly after stents. UK

    Class interactions

    WithEffectSource
    AnticoagulantsAdditive bleeding risk; combinations are used only for specific indications under supervision. UK
    NSAIDsAdditive gastrointestinal bleeding, and ibuprofen can blunt aspirin's antiplatelet effect if taken beforehand. UK
    SSRIs and corticosteroidsIncreased gastrointestinal bleeding risk. UK
    Omeprazole and esomeprazole (with clopidogrel)Reduce activation of clopidogrel; an alternative PPI is preferred. UK
    Therapeutic duplication. Aspirin with another antiplatelet (dual antiplatelet therapy) is a deliberate, time-limited prescription after stents and acute coronary syndromes; taking two antiplatelets without such an indication adds bleeding risk. UK

    Connected pages in detail

    What each linked page on Anatomy Nexus covers, in a line, for Antiplatelet drugs.

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.

    Medications

    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Clopidogrel An antiplatelet medicine that stops platelets clumping by blocking their P2Y12 receptor, taken to prevent clots after a heart attack, stent.

    Guides and news

    • MHRA recalls Xeal Pharma clopidogrel liquids The MHRA issued a Class 2 recall on 26 October 2026 of all strengths of Xeal Pharma's clopidogrel oral liquids.
    • Stroke and TIA: spotting the signs A stroke and a TIA are both emergencies: the NHS and the Stroke Association say to call 999 even if the symptoms pass.
