    Nitrates

    Also known as: nitrovasodilators, GTN

    Vasodilators that relieve angina within minutes by relaxing veins and coronary arteries. Available as a spray for attacks and as long-acting tablets for prevention.

    Open in the 3D explorerCoronary vessels anatomy
    Biological target
    Guanylate cyclase in vascular smooth muscle via nitric oxide
    Also called
    nitrovasodilators, GTN

    Mechanism of action

    Nitrates release nitric oxide inside vascular smooth muscle, which raises cyclic GMP and relaxes the vessel. Veins dilate most, so less blood returns to the heart and its workload and oxygen demand fall; coronary arteries and collaterals widen, improving supply to ischaemic muscle. Continuous exposure causes tolerance, so long-acting preparations are timed to leave a nitrate-free interval each day.

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    Coronary artery diseaseHeart attack (myocardial infarction)Heart failure

    Class effects

    • Throbbing headache, flushing
    • Dizziness and fainting from low blood pressure

    Cautions

    • Dangerous drop in pressure with sildenafil and similar drugs
    • Chest pain not relieved by two doses five minutes apart may be a heart attack: call emergency services
    • Severe aortic stenosis and very low blood pressure
