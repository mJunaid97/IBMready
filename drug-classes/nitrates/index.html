    Nitrates

    Also known as: nitrovasodilators

    Vasodilators that relieve angina within minutes by relaxing veins and coronary arteries. Available as a spray for attacks and as long-acting tablets for prevention. Glyceryl trinitrate under the tongue works within a few minutes and is used both to treat an attack and before exertion that is known to bring one on; isosorbide mononitrate and dinitrate are the longer-acting tablets for prevention, and intravenous infusions are used in hospital. Nitrates relieve the symptom rather than the underlying coronary disease, and their chief practical limits are headache, a fall in blood pressure and the tolerance that develops with round-the-clock exposure.

    nitrovasodilators

    Mechanism of action

    Nitrates release nitric oxide inside vascular smooth muscle, which raises cyclic GMP and relaxes the vessel. Veins dilate most, so less blood returns to the heart and its workload and oxygen demand fall; coronary arteries and collaterals widen, improving supply to ischaemic muscle. Continuous exposure causes tolerance, so long-acting preparations are timed to leave a nitrate-free interval each day. Nitrates are converted in the body to nitric oxide, which activates guanylyl cyclase to turn GTP into cyclic GMP; the pooling of blood in dilated veins is the main therapeutic effect, since it lowers the volume returning to the heart (preload) and so the work the ventricle must do. Taken under the tongue the drug acts within minutes, which suits an attack; swallowed long-acting forms are built for steady prevention. Tolerance develops within about a day of continuous exposure and the clinical effect fades, so long-acting regimens leave a daily nitrate-free or low-nitrate period, and continuous intravenous infusions in hospital face the same problem; proposed explanations include oxidative stress in the vessel wall and increased breakdown of cyclic GMP. The interaction with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, vardenafil, tadalafil) follows directly from the chemistry: these drugs stop cyclic GMP being broken down, so the two together cause a profound fall in blood pressure and fainting, and the combination is absolutely contraindicated.

    Class effects

    • Throbbing headache, flushing
    • Dizziness and fainting from low blood pressure
    • Headache usually eases after a few days of regular use; paracetamol can be taken for it
    • Dizziness, weakness, tiredness or sleepiness after a dose, worse with alcohol
    • Rarely, new or worsening breathlessness, fainting, or chest pain different from the usual angina, which needs emergency assessment

    Cautions

    • Dangerous drop in pressure with sildenafil and similar drugs
    • Chest pain not relieved by two doses five minutes apart may be a heart attack: call emergency services
    • Severe aortic stenosis and very low blood pressure
    • The spray is used sitting down: a priming spray into the air if unused for over a week, one spray under the tongue with the mouth then closed and breathing through the nose, and a slow rise afterwards
    • The spray is carried at all times, its expiry date checked, and a new bottle tested with a spray on a tissue
    • Dizziness after a dose means sitting or lying down and no driving until it passes
    • Surgical teams are told about nitrate use, as they may ask for it to be stopped before an operation
    • Long-acting preparations are timed to leave a nitrate-free interval so that tolerance does not blunt them

    Class warnings

    • Headache, flushing and postural hypotension are common; tolerance develops with continuous exposure, so long-acting nitrates are given with a nitrate-free interval. UK
    • Contraindicated with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil) because of profound hypotension, and in severe aortic stenosis, hypotension and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. UK

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.

    Medications

    • Glyceryl trinitrate A fast-acting nitrate spray or tablet under the tongue that widens veins and coronary arteries to relieve an angina attack within minutes.

    Guides and news

    • Heart attack, cardiac arrest and angina A heart attack is a blocked blood supply to the heart muscle, cardiac arrest is the heart stopping, and angina is chest pain that eases with rest.
