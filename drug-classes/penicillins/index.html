    Penicillins

    Also known as: beta-lactam antibiotics, amoxicillin family

    The oldest family of antibiotics and still the first choice for many chest, ear, throat, skin and dental infections: amoxicillin, co-amoxiclav, flucloxacillin and benzylpenicillin.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy
    Biological target
    Penicillin-binding proteins
    Also called
    beta-lactam antibiotics, amoxicillin family

    Mechanism of action

    Penicillins bind the enzymes bacteria use to cross-link the peptidoglycan of their cell wall; growing bacteria with a weakened wall burst. Human cells have no cell wall, which makes the class remarkably selective. Bacteria resist by making beta-lactamase enzymes that destroy the drug; clavulanic acid in co-amoxiclav blocks the enzyme, and flucloxacillin is built to withstand the staphylococcal version. They are useless against viruses.

    Members of the class

    amoxicillinco-amoxiclavflucloxacillinphenoxymethylpenicillinbenzylpenicillinpiperacillin-tazobactam

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    PneumoniaUrinary tract infectionSepsisPeptic ulcerChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)

    Class effects

    • Nausea, diarrhoea, thrush
    • Rash; anaphylaxis in true allergy (rare)
    • Clostridioides difficile colitis with broad-spectrum use

    Cautions

    • Most reported penicillin allergies are not true allergy and can be assessed; true anaphylaxis excludes the whole class
    • Amoxicillin causes a rash in glandular fever
    • Every unnecessary course drives resistance

    Class warnings

    • Anaphylaxis in true penicillin allergy is the class hazard; a previous immediate reaction to any penicillin is a contraindication to all of them. Most people labelled penicillin-allergic are not, and formal delabelling widens their antibiotic options. UK
    • Antibiotic-associated colitis (C. difficile) can follow any course; severe or persistent diarrhoea needs assessment. UK

    Class interactions
