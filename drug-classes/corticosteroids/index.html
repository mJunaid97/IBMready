    Corticosteroids

    Also known as: steroids, glucocorticoids, cortisone

    Synthetic versions of cortisol that switch off inflammation of any cause: inhaled for asthma, as tablets or injections for flares of many diseases, and as creams, drops and joint injections for local use.

    Open in the 3D explorerAdrenal glands anatomy
    Biological target
    Glucocorticoid receptor
    Also called
    steroids, glucocorticoids, cortisone

    Mechanism of action

    Glucocorticoids enter cells and bind receptors that move to the nucleus, where they switch off genes for inflammatory cytokines and switch on anti-inflammatory ones, shrinking the whole inflammatory response regardless of its trigger. The same receptors in liver, bone, skin, eye and brain produce the side effects of systemic use, and prolonged dosing suppresses the pituitary-adrenal axis, so the body stops making its own cortisol. Inhaled and topical forms deliver tiny doses where they are needed and largely avoid these effects.

    Conditions treated

    AsthmaChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)Rheumatoid arthritisInflammatory bowel diseaseGoutOsteoporosisSepsis

    Class effects

    • Systemic: raised glucose and blood pressure, weight gain, mood change, thin skin, bone loss, infection risk, cataracts
    • Inhaled: oral thrush and hoarseness

    Cautions

    • Courses over three weeks must be tapered; carry a steroid card
    • Sick-day and surgery doses; adrenal crisis if stopped abruptly
    • Bone protection and a proton pump inhibitor for long courses
    • Live vaccines avoided on high doses

    Class warnings

    • Systemic corticosteroids raise blood glucose and blood pressure, thin bone and skin, increase infection risk, disturb mood and sleep, and suppress the adrenal glands; courses over three weeks are tapered, and a steroid card is carried. Chickenpox and measles exposure needs urgent advice in non-immune people. UK
    • Inhaled corticosteroids at high doses can also suppress the adrenal glands and affect growth and bone; the lowest dose that controls asthma is used and the mouth is rinsed after use. UK

    Interaction records that name this class

    Metformin + Corticosteroids (class)

    Severity not gradedInteraction documented

    Systemic corticosteroids raise blood glucose and can reduce diabetes control on metformin.

    Why it can occur
    pharmacodynamic antagonism
    What official information says
    UK information lists steroid tablets among medicines that affect blood sugar.
    What to discuss with a clinician
    Documented; glucose monitoring during and after a steroid course.
    What may be monitored
    Blood glucose · Blood glucose
