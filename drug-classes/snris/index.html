    SNRIs

    Also known as: serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors, serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, SNRI antidepressants

    SNRIs are a group of antidepressants whose NHS examples are duloxetine and venlafaxine. They act on both serotonin and noradrenaline. The NHS says venlafaxine is used to treat depression and sometimes anxiety and panic attacks, and duloxetine is used for depression, anxiety and nerve pain. NHS formulary guidance says venlafaxine and duloxetine can also increase heart rate and blood pressure.

    Mechanism of action

    SNRIs are thought to work by increasing the amount of the mood-enhancing chemicals serotonin and noradrenaline in the brain.

    Cautions

    • The NHS says antidepressants are not addictive, but withdrawal symptoms can occur if they are stopped suddenly or a dose is missed.
    • A Scottish NHS guide says venlafaxine carries a higher risk of withdrawal effects than other antidepressants.
    • NHS formulary guidance says venlafaxine and duloxetine can increase heart rate and blood pressure, and the NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service says they can cause dose-related rises in blood pressure and abnormal heart rhythms.
    • The routes to help for suicidal thoughts are 999 if in immediate danger, NHS 111 (mental health option) and Samaritans on 116 123.

    Class warnings

    • Stopping an SNRI suddenly can cause withdrawal symptoms, so the dose is usually lowered gradually. UK
