    Benzodiazepines

    Also known as: benzos, benzodiazepine

    Benzodiazepines are depressant medicines that slow the central nervous system, and diazepam is the member covered on this site. The MHRA says dependence can occur with therapeutic doses and can become established within weeks.

    Open in the 3D explorerBrain anatomy
    Also called
    benzos, benzodiazepine

    Mechanism of action

    NHS Inform describes benzodiazepines as drugs that slow down the central nervous system.

    Members of the class

    diazepamlorazepamclonazepam

    Members with a page on this site are linked.

    Conditions treated

    Epilepsy

    Cautions

    • The NHS advises against alcohol and grapefruit juice while taking diazepam because they can increase the risk of side effects.
    • The NHS says diazepam should not be taken in pregnancy unless a doctor or pharmacist advises it. The manufacturer's patient leaflet says that regular use during pregnancy may cause withdrawal symptoms in the baby.
    • NHS Inform says combining benzodiazepines with alcohol, gabapentinoids or opioids raises the risk of overdose.
    • NHS Inform says stopping benzodiazepines suddenly can cause withdrawal effects that could be life-threatening.
    • Licensed benzodiazepines such as diazepam, lorazepam and clonazepam are Class C drugs listed in Schedule 4 Part 1 of the Misuse of Drugs Regulations.
