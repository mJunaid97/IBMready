    Contrast agents

    Also known as: contrast media, contrast dyes, imaging contrast

    Substances given to make blood vessels, organs, the gut or joints stand out on imaging: iodinated agents for X-ray and CT, gadolinium agents for MRI, microbubbles for ultrasound and barium for the gut.

    Open in the 3D explorerKidneys anatomy
    Biological target
    The imaging signal of the tissue the agent occupies (X-ray attenuation, MRI relaxation, ultrasound reflection)
    Also called
    contrast media, contrast dyes, imaging contrast

    Mechanism of action

    Each agent changes how the tissue it fills interacts with the imaging energy. Iodine and barium absorb X-rays, so filled vessels and cavities appear bright on X-ray and CT; gadolinium shortens the relaxation time of nearby water protons, brightening perfused tissue on MRI; microbubbles reflect ultrasound strongly and outline blood flow. Timing the scan to when the agent is in arteries, veins or tissue shows different structures.

    Conditions treated

    Chronic kidney diseaseCoronary artery diseasePulmonary embolismStroke

    Class effects

    • A warm flush, metallic taste and nausea during injection
    • Allergic-type reactions, rarely severe
    • Kidney injury with iodinated agents in people at risk; gadolinium retention concerns in repeated MRI

    Cautions

    • Kidney function checked before iodinated and gadolinium agents in people at risk
    • Previous contrast reactions and severe allergy declared beforehand
    • Pregnancy: used only when the imaging is essential
