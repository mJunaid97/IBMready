    Condition · Lungs & breathing

    Lung cancer

    Also known as: bronchial carcinoma

    A malignant tumour arising from the lining of the airways or alveoli, divided into non-small-cell (about 85%) and small-cell types.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy

    Overview

    Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death because it is usually found late: symptoms appear only when a tumour blocks an airway, invades the chest wall or spreads. Smoking causes about eight in ten cases. Low-dose CT screening of heavy smokers finds tumours early enough to cure.

    Causes

    • Tobacco smoke
    • Radon gas
    • Asbestos, diesel exhaust and other occupational carcinogens
    • Air pollution
    • Family history

    Risk factors

    • Smoking, including second-hand smoke
    • COPD and pulmonary fibrosis
    • Previous radiotherapy to the chest
    • Age over 60

    Symptoms

    CoughShortness of breathChest painFatigue

    What a clinician may find

    • Finger clubbing
    • Hoarse voice
    • Enlarged lymph nodes in the neck
    • Signs of a pleural effusion or collapsed lobe
    • Weight loss

    Complications

    • Spread to lymph nodes, liver, bones, brain and adrenals
    • Airway obstruction and pneumonia
    • Superior vena cava obstruction
    • Paraneoplastic syndromes

    Diagnosis

    Chest X-ray then CT of the chest and abdomen; tissue by bronchoscopy or CT-guided biopsy; PET-CT for staging; molecular tests on the tumour to guide targeted therapy.

    BiopsyFull blood countLiver function testsGenetic testing
    X-rayCT scanPET-CT scanMRI scan

    Treatment

    Depends on type and stage: surgery or radical radiotherapy for early non-small-cell cancer; chemotherapy, immunotherapy and targeted drugs (for EGFR, ALK and other mutations) for advanced disease; chemotherapy and radiotherapy for small-cell cancer; palliative care for symptoms throughout.

    Bronchoscopy

    Prevention

    Not smoking is the single most effective measure; radon testing of homes; workplace protection; CT screening for eligible heavy smokers.

    When to seek care

    See a doctor within days for a cough lasting three weeks, coughing blood, unexplained breathlessness, weight loss or a new hoarse voice, especially if you smoke or have smoked.
