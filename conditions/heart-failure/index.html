    Heart failure

    Also known as: congestive heart failure, cardiac failure

    A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy

    Overview

    Heart failure is the end result of damage from heart attacks, high blood pressure, valve disease or cardiomyopathy. Blood backs up behind the failing ventricle: fluid in the lungs causes breathlessness, fluid in the tissues causes swelling. It is a chronic condition managed with drugs that reduce the heart's workload and, in some cases, devices.

    Causes

    • Coronary artery disease and previous heart attacks
    • High blood pressure
    • Valve disease
    • Cardiomyopathies (genetic, viral, alcohol, chemotherapy)
    • Atrial fibrillation
    • Congenital heart disease

    Risk factors

    • Age
    • Diabetes
    • Obesity
    • Smoking
    • Excess alcohol

    Symptoms

    Shortness of breathSwelling (oedema)FatigueCough

    What a clinician may find

    • Raised jugular venous pressure
    • Crackles at the lung bases
    • Pitting ankle oedema
    • Enlarged liver
    • Third heart sound

    Complications

    • Pulmonary oedema (acute breathlessness)
    • Kidney impairment
    • Arrhythmias and sudden death
    • Repeated hospital admissions

    Diagnosis

    Blood natriuretic peptide (BNP) is raised; echocardiography measures the ejection fraction and finds the cause. ECG, chest X-ray and blood tests for kidney function and anaemia complete the picture.

    BNP and NT-proBNP testEchocardiogramElectrocardiogramKidney function testsFull blood countElectrolytesTroponin test
    X-rayMRI scan

    Treatment

    Diuretics relieve fluid overload; ACE inhibitors or ARNI, beta blockers, mineralocorticoid antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors prolong life in reduced ejection fraction. Salt and fluid moderation, daily weights, vaccination and exercise rehabilitation. Selected patients receive pacemakers, defibrillators or, rarely, transplantation.

    Cardiac catheterisation
    FurosemideRamiprilMetoprolol and beta blockers

    Prevention

    Treating blood pressure and coronary disease early, limiting alcohol, and controlling diabetes.

    When to seek care

    Seek urgent help for breathlessness at rest, waking at night gasping, pink frothy sputum, chest pain, fainting, or rapid weight gain (more than 2 kg in three days).
