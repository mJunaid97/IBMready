    Gallstones

    Also known as: cholelithiasis, biliary colic, cholecystitis

    Hard deposits, usually of cholesterol, that form in the gallbladder when bile becomes supersaturated.

    Open in the 3D explorerGallbladder & bile ducts anatomy

    Overview

    Gallstones are present in about one adult in ten and most cause no trouble. Symptoms begin when a stone blocks the gallbladder outlet (biliary colic), inflames the gallbladder (cholecystitis), or escapes into the bile duct where it causes jaundice, infection of the bile (cholangitis) or pancreatitis.

    Causes

    • Cholesterol supersaturation of bile with sluggish gallbladder emptying
    • Pigment stones from red cell breakdown (haemolysis) or infection

    Risk factors

    • Female sex, pregnancy and oestrogen
    • Age over 40
    • Obesity and rapid weight loss
    • Family history
    • Diabetes
    • Certain drugs (fibrates, octreotide)

    Symptoms

    Abdominal painNauseaVomitingFever

    What a clinician may find

    • Right upper quadrant tenderness worse on breathing in (Murphy's sign)
    • Fever in cholecystitis
    • Jaundice with a duct stone

    Complications

    • Acute cholecystitis and empyema
    • Bile duct obstruction and cholangitis
    • Acute pancreatitis
    • Gallstone ileus
    • Gallbladder cancer (rare)

    Diagnosis

    Ultrasound shows stones and a thickened gallbladder wall; liver function tests and lipase detect duct obstruction and pancreatitis; MRCP or endoscopic ultrasound finds duct stones.

    Liver function testsFull blood countCRP test
    UltrasoundMRI scanCT scan

    Treatment

    Silent stones need no treatment. Symptomatic stones: laparoscopic removal of the gallbladder (cholecystectomy), ideally within days of a cholecystitis attack; duct stones are removed endoscopically (ERCP). Pain relief, fluids and antibiotics for infection.

    CholecystectomyGastroscopy
    IbuprofenParacetamolMorphine and opioidsAmoxicillin

    Prevention

    Healthy weight lost gradually, regular meals, a diet high in fibre and low in refined carbohydrate.

    When to seek care

    Urgent assessment for abdominal pain lasting more than a few hours, pain with fever or shivering, or yellowing of the skin or eyes.
