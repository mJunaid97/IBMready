    Deep vein thrombosis

    Also known as: DVT, blood clot in the leg

    A blood clot in a deep vein, most often of the calf or thigh.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy

    Overview

    Clots form when blood flow slows, the vein wall is injured or the blood clots too easily (Virchow's triad). A leg DVT causes pain and swelling; its danger is that a fragment can break off and lodge in the lungs as a pulmonary embolism.

    Causes

    • Immobility: surgery, long flights, bed rest, plaster casts
    • Cancer and chemotherapy
    • Pregnancy, the combined pill and hormone replacement
    • Inherited clotting tendencies (factor V Leiden and others)
    • Injury to the vein

    Risk factors

    • Age
    • Obesity
    • Smoking
    • Previous DVT
    • Dehydration

    Symptoms

    Swelling (oedema)Knee pain

    What a clinician may find

    • One calf swollen more than 3 cm larger than the other, warm, tender, with dilated surface veins

    Complications

    • Pulmonary embolism
    • Post-thrombotic syndrome: chronic swelling, pain and skin ulcers

    Diagnosis

    A clinical probability score, a D-dimer blood test to rule out low-risk cases, and compression ultrasound of the leg veins to confirm.

    Full blood countKidney function tests
    Ultrasound

    Treatment

    Anticoagulation for at least three months, usually with a direct oral anticoagulant (apixaban, rivaroxaban) or warfarin; compression stockings for symptoms; early mobilisation. Large clots occasionally need catheter-directed clot removal.

    Apixaban and DOACsWarfarin

    Prevention

    Move early after surgery, use prescribed stockings and injections in hospital, walk and hydrate on long journeys, stop smoking.

    When to seek care

    Same-day assessment for a swollen painful calf; emergency care if breathlessness or chest pain develops.
