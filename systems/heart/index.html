    Heart

    Chambers, valves and walls of the heart, with the pericardium.

    Open in the 3D explorer Quiz me on this system Locate structures Flashcards

    Overview

    The heart is a four-chambered muscular pump about the size of a fist. The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the venae cavae and passes it through the tricuspid valve to the right ventricle, which pumps it to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns to the left atrium, crosses the mitral valve into the thick-walled left ventricle and is ejected through the aortic valve into the aorta. The model separates the atrial and ventricular walls, the chambers, the valve leaflets and cusps, and the papillary muscles that anchor the mitral and tricuspid valves.

    Functions

    • Pump blood through the pulmonary and systemic circulations
    • Keep blood flowing one way with four valves
    • Generate its own rhythm through the conduction system

    Clinical notes

    • Coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction
    • Heart failure when the ventricles cannot pump enough
    • Valve disease: stenosis or regurgitation
    • Arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation
