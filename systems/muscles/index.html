    Muscles

    Skeletal muscles, tendons and aponeuroses that move the body.

    Open in the 3D explorer Quiz me on this system Locate structures Flashcards

    Overview

    Skeletal muscle makes up roughly 40% of body mass. Each muscle spans one or more joints, attaching to bone through tendons or flat aponeuroses; when its fibres shorten it pulls the bones together. Muscles work in groups: agonists produce a movement, antagonists control it, and stabilisers hold neighbouring joints still. The model includes the muscles of the limbs, trunk, neck, face, larynx and pharynx, and the diaphragm.

    Functions

    • Produce movement and maintain posture
    • Stabilise joints
    • Drive breathing (diaphragm and intercostals)
    • Generate heat

    Clinical notes

    • Strains and tears from overload
    • Compartment syndrome when pressure rises within a fascial compartment
    • Muscular dystrophies progressively weaken muscle fibres

    Organs & groups in this system

    Diaphragm

    The dome-shaped muscle separating chest from abdomen; the main muscle of breathing.
