    Ultrasound

    Also known as: sonography, ultrasound scan, Doppler, echo, point-of-care ultrasound, POCUS

    High-frequency sound waves bounced off tissue give real-time, radiation-free images, used for pregnancy, the abdomen, the heart (echocardiography), blood vessels and guiding needles.

    Open in the 3D explorerLiver anatomy

    How it works

    A hand-held probe sends pulses of high-frequency sound into the body and listens for echoes from tissue boundaries. The depth of each echo builds a live moving image. Doppler ultrasound measures the frequency shift from moving blood to show flow direction and speed in colour. It is safe, portable and real-time, but sound cannot cross bone or air.

    What it shows

    • Abdomen: gallstones, liver size and texture, kidney obstruction, aortic aneurysm, fluid
    • Pelvis: pregnancy, ovaries and uterus, bladder volume, prostate (transrectal)
    • Vessels: deep vein thrombosis, carotid narrowing, varicose veins, arterial blockages
    • Heart: the echocardiogram
    • Soft tissue: thyroid nodules, breast lumps, lymph nodes, tendons, muscle tears, abscesses
    • Guidance for needles: biopsies, drains, injections, cannulas

    Best for

    • First test for the gallbladder, kidneys, thyroid, scrotum, pregnancy and leg veins
    • Any test that must be repeated or done at the bedside
    • Guiding procedures in real time
    • Children and pregnancy

    Not the right tool for

    • Anything behind bone (brain in adults, spinal cord) or air (lungs beyond the surface, gas-filled bowel)
    • Deep structures in larger patients (image quality falls with depth)
    • A comprehensive survey: it shows what the operator points at, so quality depends on skill

    What to expect

    Modality
    Ultrasound
    Preparation
    Fasting for gallbladder scans so it is full; a full bladder for pelvic scans; otherwise none.
    Duration
    10-30 minutes.
    Radiation
    No ionising radiation; diagnostic ultrasound has no known harmful effects at the energies used.
    Contrast
    Microbubble contrast (tiny gas bubbles injected into a vein) improves characterisation of liver and kidney lesions.
    Risks
    None known; brief discomfort from probe pressure on a tender area.

    Common uses

    RegionTypical question
    Right upper abdomenGallstones, jaundice, abnormal liver tests
    PelvisEarly pregnancy and its location, fibroids, ovarian cysts, the endometrium after bleeding; transvaginal for detail
    Kidneys and bladderKidney injury, obstruction, stones, bladder emptying
    Leg veinsSuspected deep vein thrombosis
    NeckThyroid nodules, lymph nodes, carotid stenosis after a stroke
    MusculoskeletalTendon tears, joint effusions, guiding steroid injections
