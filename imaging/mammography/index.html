    Mammography

    Also known as: mammogram, breast screening, breast X-ray, tomosynthesis

    A low-dose X-ray of the compressed breast that can detect cancers years before they can be felt, used for screening programmes and for investigating breast symptoms.

    Open in the 3D explorerRib cage anatomy

    How it works

    A low-dose X-ray of each breast, compressed between two plates for a few seconds to spread the tissue thin and hold it still. Two views are taken of each side. Digital tomosynthesis takes a sweep of images to reconstruct thin slices, reducing overlap. Cancers show as masses, distortions or clusters of tiny calcifications, often years before they can be felt.

    What it shows

    • Breast cancer, including ductal carcinoma in situ (calcifications)
    • Benign lumps: cysts, fibroadenomas (with ultrasound to tell them apart)
    • Breast density, which affects both risk and how easily cancers are seen

    Best for

    • Population screening of women aged roughly 50-70, every 2-3 years depending on country
    • Investigating a new lump, nipple change or skin change in women over 40
    • Follow-up after breast cancer treatment

    Not the right tool for

    • Younger women with dense breasts, where ultrasound and MRI see more
    • Assessing implants (MRI)
    • Ruling out cancer completely: about 1 in 8 cancers is missed, so a new lump is always investigated even after a normal screen

    What to expect

    Preparation
    No deodorant or talc on the day (they mimic calcifications); book after a period if breasts are tender.
    Duration
    About 10-15 minutes, with each compression lasting a few seconds.
    Radiation
    About 0.4 mSv for two views of both breasts, similar to seven weeks of natural background radiation.
    Contrast
    Contrast-enhanced mammography with iodine is an emerging alternative to breast MRI.
    Risks
    Discomfort from compression; a small radiation dose; false alarms leading to recalls and biopsies; overdiagnosis of cancers that would never have caused harm.
