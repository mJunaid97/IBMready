    X-ray

    Also known as: plain radiograph, chest X-ray, CXR, radiograph, plain film

    The oldest and most widely available imaging test: a brief beam of X-rays casts a shadow picture of bone, air and soft tissue, ideal for fractures, chest infections and dental checks.

    Open in the 3D explorerLungs anatomy

    How it works

    A brief pulse of X-rays passes through the body onto a digital detector. Dense tissue such as bone absorbs most of the beam and appears white; air absorbs almost none and appears black; soft tissue and fluid are shades of grey in between. The image is a two-dimensional shadow of everything in the beam's path.

    What it shows

    • Bone: fractures, dislocations, arthritis, bone tumours and infection
    • Chest: pneumonia, fluid, collapsed lung, heart size, tumours
    • Abdomen: bowel obstruction, free air from a perforation, some stones
    • Teeth and jaw
    • Foreign bodies made of metal or glass

    Best for

    • A first look at bones and the chest: fast, cheap and available everywhere
    • Following fracture healing
    • Bowel obstruction and perforation as a screen

    Not the right tool for

    • Soft tissues such as ligaments, cartilage, brain and spinal cord
    • Early stress fractures and small bone injuries (MRI or CT)
    • Subtle lung disease (CT)

    What to expect

    Preparation
    None. Remove metal from the area; tell the radiographer about any possibility of pregnancy.
    Duration
    A few minutes; the exposure lasts a fraction of a second.
    Radiation
    A chest X-ray is about 0.1 mSv, comparable to a few days of natural background radiation; a lumbar spine film is around 1 mSv.
    Contrast
    Barium swallows and enemas coat the gut to outline it on fluoroscopy; iodine contrast is used for joints (arthrogram) and blood vessels (angiography).
    Risks
    The radiation dose is tiny for a single film; the abdomen and pelvis are shielded or avoided in pregnancy unless essential.

    Common uses

    RegionTypical question
    ChestCough, breathlessness, chest pain, fever, suspected pneumonia or heart failure
    Limbs and spineInjury, joint pain, suspected fracture, osteoarthritis
    AbdomenObstruction, perforation, swallowed objects
