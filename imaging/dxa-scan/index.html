    DXA scan (bone density)

    Also known as: DEXA scan, bone density scan, bone densitometry, dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry

    A quick, very low-dose X-ray measurement of bone mineral density at the hip and spine, reported as a T-score, that diagnoses osteoporosis and estimates fracture risk.

    Open in the 3D explorerVertebral column anatomy

    How it works

    Two X-ray beams of different energies pass through the bone; the difference in how much each is absorbed gives the amount of mineral per area of bone. The scanner passes over the lower spine and hip while the person lies still on a padded table for about 10-20 minutes.

    What it shows

    • Bone mineral density at the hip and lumbar spine, and sometimes the forearm
    • A T-score comparing the result with young healthy adults and a Z-score comparing it with people of the same age and sex
    • Change in density on repeat scans, to monitor treatment

    Best for

    • Diagnosing osteoporosis and osteopenia
    • Estimating fracture risk together with clinical risk factors (FRAX)
    • Deciding on and monitoring bone-protective treatment
    • Assessing bone loss from long-term corticosteroids or other causes

    Not the right tool for

    • Finding the cause of bone or back pain
    • Detecting fractures, tumours or arthritis in detail: X-ray, CT or MRI do that
    • Children and people with spinal hardware, where interpretation needs specialist adjustment

    What to expect

    Modality
    DXA bone densitometry
    Preparation
    No calcium supplements on the day of the scan; wear clothes without metal; mention recent contrast X-rays or nuclear scans, which can interfere.
    Duration
    10-20 minutes
    Radiation
    Very low: a fraction of a chest X-ray, less than a day of natural background radiation.
    Contrast
    None
    Risks
    Minimal radiation exposure; not done in pregnancy unless essential.

    Common uses

    RegionTypical question
    Hip and spineBone density for osteoporosis diagnosis and fracture risk
    ForearmWhen hip or spine cannot be measured, or in hyperparathyroidism
    Whole bodyBody composition (fat and lean mass) in specialist settings
