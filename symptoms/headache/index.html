    Headache

    Also known as: head pain, cephalalgia

    Pain felt anywhere in the head. Almost everyone has headaches; the great majority are primary headaches (tension-type or migraine) rather than a sign of disease. The brain itself has no pain receptors: headache arises from the meninges, blood vessels, muscles of the scalp and neck, sinuses and the nerves that supply them.

    Seek urgent care if

    • A headache that reaches maximum intensity within seconds to a minute (a thunderclap headache)
    • Headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, drowsiness or a rash that does not fade under a glass
    • Headache after a head injury, especially with vomiting or drowsiness
    • Headache with new weakness, numbness, slurred speech, double vision or a seizure
    • A new or changing headache in someone over 50, in pregnancy, or with cancer or a weakened immune system

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    Tension-type headacheA tight band around the head, often with neck and scalp muscle tension, stress or poor sleep.
    MigraineThrobbing, usually one-sided, worse with movement, with nausea and sensitivity to light and sound; sometimes preceded by an aura.
    Medication-overuse headacheFrequent painkiller use (more than 10 to 15 days a month) can itself keep the headache going.
    Dehydration, hunger, alcohol, caffeine withdrawalCommon, short-lived and reversible.
    InfectionsColds, flu and sinusitis often bring a dull headache with facial pressure.

    Less common causes

    Cluster headacheExcruciating pain around one eye in bouts lasting weeks, with a watering eye and blocked nostril.
    Giant cell arteritisNew headache over 50 with scalp tenderness and jaw pain on chewing; can threaten sight.
    Raised pressure inside the skullFrom a tumour, bleed or blocked fluid drainage: worse lying down, on waking and with straining, with vomiting.
    Meningitis and subarachnoid haemorrhageSudden severe headache with fever, stiff neck, rash or collapse.

    Conditions

    • Migraine A recurrent headache disorder with attacks of moderate to severe throbbing pain, typically one-sided, lasting 4 to 72 hours.
    • High blood pressure (hypertension) Persistently raised arterial pressure, usually defined as 140/90 mmHg or more in the clinic (135/85 on home readings).
    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Brain tumours A brain tumour is a growth of abnormal cells in or around the brain, and it may be non-cancerous or cancerous.
    • COVID-19 COVID-19 is an illness caused by a virus called coronavirus (SARS-CoV-2), with symptoms that overlap with colds and flu.
    • Epilepsy A tendency to recurrent, unprovoked seizures: bursts of abnormal synchronised electrical activity in the brain.
    • Fibromyalgia A long-term condition that causes pain all over the body.
    • Hay fever (allergic rhinitis) Hay fever is an allergic reaction to pollen, also called seasonal allergic rhinitis.
    • Flu (influenza) Flu is a respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses.
    • Meningitis Meningitis is an infection of the protective membranes that surround the brain and spinal cord.

    Medical tests

    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • CRP test A blood test for C-reactive protein, a protein the liver releases within hours of significant inflammation, infection or tissue injury.
    • Blood pressure measurement The pressure of blood against the artery walls, recorded as two numbers: the systolic pressure when the heart contracts and the diastolic pressure…
    • ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) An old but still useful test that measures how far red blood cells settle in a narrow tube in one hour.

    Biomarkers

    • Sodium The main positively charged ion of the fluid outside cells and the chief determinant of blood osmolality.

    Imaging

    • CT scan A rotating X-ray tube and computer build cross-sectional slices of the body in seconds, showing bone, organs.
    • MRI scan Magnetic resonance imaging uses a strong magnet and radio waves, not radiation, to produce detailed pictures of soft tissue: the brain, spinal cord.

    Procedures

    • Cataract surgery The eye's clouded natural lens is broken up with ultrasound through a tiny corneal incision and replaced with a folded artificial lens.

    Medications

    • Amitriptyline Amitriptyline is a prescription-only medicine from a group called tricyclic antidepressants, used for nerve pain and to help prevent migraine.
    • Amlodipine A long-acting calcium channel blocker that relaxes artery walls to lower blood pressure and prevent angina.
    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Diclofenac Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that eases pain and swelling in problems affecting joints, muscles and bones.
    • Doxycycline Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic, available on prescription.
    • Duloxetine Duloxetine is an antidepressant medicine that is also used for anxiety and nerve pain.
    • Estradiol (oestrogen) The main natural oestrogen, given as tablets, patches, gel, spray or vaginal preparations to replace what the ovaries no longer make after the…
    • Glyceryl trinitrate A fast-acting nitrate spray or tablet under the tongue that widens veins and coronary arteries to relieve an angina attack within minutes.
    • Ibuprofen A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) that eases pain, inflammation and fever by blocking prostaglandin production.
    • Mirtazapine Mirtazapine is an antidepressant, prescribed mainly for depression and sometimes for obsessive compulsive disorder (OCD) and anxiety.

    Guides and news

    • Altitude sickness symptoms and prevention Altitude sickness is illness from being high up with less oxygen; the NHS says mild cases ease with rest, and going up slowly helps prevent it.
    • Dizziness and vertigo: causes and warning signs Dizziness is common and has many causes, from inner ear problems to low blood pressure.
    • Fever in adults: causes and when to get help A high temperature in adults is usually a sign of infection and often settles at home.
    • Head injury and concussion: when to get help Most head injuries are not serious, but the NHS says being knocked out and not waking, a fit or being unable to stay awake need a 999 call.
    • Headaches: types, causes and warning signs Tension-type headache is the most common type, and a headache that starts suddenly and is very severe needs a 999 call.
    • Heat exhaustion and heatstroke: what to know Heat exhaustion usually eases with cooling and rest, while heatstroke is an emergency for which the NHS says to call 999.
    • How travellers can reduce malaria risk UK guidance sums up malaria prevention as ABCD: awareness of risk, bite avoidance, antimalarial tablets where needed, and prompt diagnosis.
    • Migraine and tension headache compared Tension headache is usually a dull, tight pressure on both sides of the head, while migraine often throbs and brings nausea or sensitivity to light.
    • Stress: how it shows up and where to get help Stress is a reaction to mental or emotional pressure that can affect mood, body and behaviour.
