    Swelling (oedema)

    Also known as: oedema, edema, fluid retention, puffy ankles

    Excess fluid in the tissues. Swelling of both ankles that pits when pressed usually means fluid retention from the heart, kidneys, liver or medications; swelling of one limb suggests a local problem such as a clot or infection; a swollen joint points to the joint itself.

    Seek urgent care if

    • Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, or with difficulty breathing
    • A single swollen, painful, warm leg, particularly after travel, surgery or immobility
    • Swelling with chest pain or sudden breathlessness
    • Red, hot, spreading swelling with fever

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    Prolonged standing, heat and pregnancyMild ankle swelling that clears overnight.
    MedicationsCalcium-channel blockers (amlodipine), NSAIDs, steroids, some diabetes drugs.
    Chronic venous insufficiency and varicose veinsAching heavy legs with skin discolouration.
    Heart failureAnkle swelling with breathlessness, especially lying flat.
    Injury, sprain or insect biteLocal swelling with pain and warmth.

    Less common causes

    Deep vein thrombosisOne calf swollen, tender and warm; a clot can travel to the lungs.
    CellulitisSpreading red, hot, painful swelling with fever.
    Kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome)Puffy eyes in the morning, frothy urine, generalised swelling.
    Liver cirrhosisSwollen abdomen (ascites) and legs with jaundice.
    LymphoedemaFirm swelling after lymph node surgery or radiotherapy.
    Angioedema and anaphylaxisSudden swelling of lips, tongue and face.

    Often occurs with

    Shortness of breathFatigueJoint painRash

    Conditions

    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Deep vein thrombosis A blood clot in a deep vein, most often of the calf or thigh.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Cirrhosis Scarring of the liver from long-term damage, in which nodules of regenerating cells are surrounded by fibrous bands that distort blood flow and…
    • Gout An inflammatory arthritis caused by deposition of uric acid crystals in joints, triggered when blood urate is persistently high.
    • ACL injury A sprain or tear of the anterior cruciate ligament, the band inside the knee that stops the tibia sliding forward on the femur and controls rotation.
    • Breast cancer A malignant tumour arising from the cells that line the ducts or lobules of the breast.
    • Cellulitis Cellulitis is an infection of the deeper layers of the skin caused by bacteria.
    • Colorectal (bowel) cancer A malignant tumour of the colon or rectum, almost always arising from a benign polyp (adenoma) over years.
    • Conjunctivitis Inflammation of the conjunctiva, the clear tissue that covers the white of the eye and lines the inside of the eyelids.

    Medical tests

    • Kidney function tests Creatinine and urea are waste products the kidneys should clear; how much builds up in blood shows how well they are filtering.
    • Liver function tests A panel of enzymes and proteins that together show whether liver cells are injured, whether bile is flowing.
    • Urine protein Healthy kidney filters hold back almost all protein.
    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • Biopsy Removal of a small piece of tissue or a few cells so a pathologist can examine them under the microscope.
    • BNP and NT-proBNP test A blood test for the natriuretic peptides the heart's ventricles release when they are stretched by high pressure or extra volume.
    • Creatinine and eGFR The everyday measure of how well the kidneys filter.
    • Echocardiogram An ultrasound of the beating heart.
    • Prothrombin time and INR The prothrombin time measures how many seconds plasma takes to clot once the tissue-factor pathway is triggered.

    Biomarkers

    • Albumin The most abundant protein in blood, made by the liver.
    • Natriuretic peptides (BNP and NT-proBNP) Hormones released by the heart's ventricles when their walls are stretched by high pressure or volume.
    • Creatinine A waste product of muscle metabolism produced at a steady rate and cleared almost entirely by the kidneys.
    • eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) An estimate of how many millilitres of blood the kidneys filter each minute, calculated from creatinine together with age and sex.

    Imaging

    • Ultrasound High-frequency sound waves bounced off tissue give real-time, radiation-free images, used for pregnancy, the abdomen, the heart (echocardiography).
    • X-ray The oldest and most widely available imaging test: a brief beam of X-rays casts a shadow picture of bone, air and soft tissue, ideal for fractures.
    • Mammography A low-dose X-ray of the compressed breast that can detect cancers years before they can be felt.

    Procedures

    • Caesarean section Birth of a baby through a cut in the lower abdomen and in the lower segment of the uterus.
    • Chest masculinisation (top surgery) Removal of most of the breast tissue and excess skin, with resizing and repositioning of the nipples, to give a flat chest with a male contour.
    • Colonoscopy A flexible camera is passed around the whole large intestine after laxative bowel preparation to find and remove polyps.
    • Dialysis Dialysis takes over the kidneys' filtering work when they have failed.
    • Joint aspiration and injection A needle drawn into a swollen joint removes fluid for analysis and relief of pressure, and may be followed by a corticosteroid injection.
    • Knee replacement The worn joint surfaces of the knee are replaced with metal components and a polyethylene insert to relieve the pain of severe arthritis when…
    • Phalloplasty and metoidioplasty Construction of a penis, most often as gender-affirming surgery for trans men and some non-binary people assigned female at birth.

    Medications

    • Furosemide A powerful loop diuretic that makes the kidneys excrete salt and water within an hour, used to clear fluid overload in heart failure.
    • Allopurinol A xanthine oxidase inhibitor that lowers uric acid in the blood.
    • Amlodipine A long-acting calcium channel blocker that relaxes artery walls to lower blood pressure and prevent angina.
    • Apixaban and DOACs A direct oral anticoagulant that blocks clotting factor Xa, preventing strokes in atrial fibrillation and treating deep vein thrombosis and…
    • Cetirizine and antihistamines A second-generation (less sedating) antihistamine that blocks histamine H1 receptors to relieve hay fever, hives and other allergic symptoms.
    • Combined oral contraceptive pill The combined pill is a hormonal contraceptive that contains two hormones, oestrogen and a progestogen.
    • Diclofenac Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that eases pain and swelling in problems affecting joints, muscles and bones.
    • Levothyroxine Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism.
    • Lisinopril An ACE inhibitor that lowers blood pressure, protects the heart after a heart attack and in heart failure.
    • Losartan An angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) that relaxes blood vessels to lower blood pressure and protect the kidneys in diabetes.

    Guides and news

    • ACE inhibitors, ARBs and amlodipine compared ARBs work much like ACE inhibitors but do not typically cause a dry cough; amlodipine relaxes artery walls and can cause ankle swelling.
    • Long flights and DVT: risk and how to lower it Long journeys can raise the chance of a leg blood clot (DVT); the NHS and TravelHealthPro describe moving, drinking water and sometimes stockings.
    • Swollen ankles and legs: causes and red flags Swollen ankles and legs often come from sitting or standing for long periods, but can signal a clot or a heart, kidney or liver problem.
