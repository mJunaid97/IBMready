    Swelling (oedema)

    Also known as: oedema, edema, fluid retention, puffy ankles

    Excess fluid in the tissues. Swelling of both ankles that pits when pressed usually means fluid retention from the heart, kidneys, liver or medications; swelling of one limb suggests a local problem such as a clot or infection; a swollen joint points to the joint itself.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy

    What it is

    Excess fluid in the tissues. Swelling of both ankles that pits when pressed usually means fluid retention from the heart, kidneys, liver or medications; swelling of one limb suggests a local problem such as a clot or infection; a swollen joint points to the joint itself.

    Common causes

    Prolonged standing, heat and pregnancyMild ankle swelling that clears overnight.
    MedicationsCalcium-channel blockers (amlodipine), NSAIDs, steroids, some diabetes drugs.
    Chronic venous insufficiency and varicose veinsAching heavy legs with skin discolouration.
    Heart failureAnkle swelling with breathlessness, especially lying flat.
    Injury, sprain or insect biteLocal swelling with pain and warmth.

    Less common causes

    Deep vein thrombosisOne calf swollen, tender and warm; a clot can travel to the lungs.
    CellulitisSpreading red, hot, painful swelling with fever.
    Kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome)Puffy eyes in the morning, frothy urine, generalised swelling.
    Liver cirrhosisSwollen abdomen (ascites) and legs with jaundice.
    LymphoedemaFirm swelling after lymph node surgery or radiotherapy.
    Angioedema and anaphylaxisSudden swelling of lips, tongue and face.

    Often occurs with

    Shortness of breathFatigueJoint painRash

    Seek urgent care if

    • Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, or with difficulty breathing
    • A single swollen, painful, warm leg, particularly after travel, surgery or immobility
    • Swelling with chest pain or sudden breathlessness
    • Red, hot, spreading swelling with fever
