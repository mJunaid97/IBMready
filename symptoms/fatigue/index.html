    Fatigue

    Also known as: tiredness, exhaustion, lack of energy

    Persistent tiredness that rest does not fix. It is one of the commonest reasons to see a doctor and usually reflects sleep, mood, workload or lifestyle rather than disease, but it is also the presenting feature of anaemia, thyroid disease, diabetes, infections and heart, lung, liver and kidney conditions.

    Seek urgent care if

    • Fatigue with chest pain, breathlessness at rest or fainting
    • Fatigue with unexplained weight loss, night sweats, persistent fever or bleeding
    • Thoughts of self-harm alongside exhaustion and low mood (in immediate danger call 999; otherwise contact your GP or NHS 111, or call Samaritans free on 116 123, any time)

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    Poor or short sleepIncluding shift work, insomnia and obstructive sleep apnoea (snoring, unrefreshing sleep, daytime dozing).
    Depression and anxietyLow mood, loss of interest, early waking and poor concentration.
    AnaemiaEspecially iron deficiency in menstruating women and people with poor diet or bleeding.
    Underactive thyroidWeight gain, feeling cold, constipation and dry skin.
    Viral infections and their aftermathGlandular fever, flu, COVID-19 and other infections can leave weeks of fatigue.
    LifestyleAlcohol, inactivity, excess caffeine, crash dieting.

    Less common causes

    DiabetesWith thirst, frequent urination and weight loss.
    Heart failure, COPD, kidney and liver diseaseFatigue with breathlessness, swelling or jaundice.
    Coeliac disease and inflammatory bowel diseaseFatigue with bowel symptoms and weight loss.
    Chronic fatigue syndrome / MEDisabling fatigue over six months, worsened by exertion, diagnosed after excluding other causes.
    CancerWith weight loss, night sweats or a lump.
    Medication side effectsBeta blockers, antihistamines, opioids, antidepressants.

    Often occurs with

    WeaknessShortness of breathDizzinessHeadacheJoint pain

    Conditions

    • Anaemia A haemoglobin concentration below normal (roughly under 130 g/L in men and 120 g/L in women), reducing the blood's capacity to carry oxygen.
    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • Heart failure A syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can do so only at raised filling pressures.
    • Chronic kidney disease Persistent reduction in kidney function (a filtration rate below 60 ml/min) or evidence of kidney damage such as protein in the urine.
    • Cirrhosis Scarring of the liver from long-term damage, in which nodules of regenerating cells are surrounded by fibrous bands that distort blood flow and…
    • Alzheimer's disease The commonest cause of dementia: a progressive loss of memory and thinking ability caused by the build-up of amyloid plaques and tau tangles and the…
    • Anxiety and generalised anxiety disorder Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) is a long-term condition in which a person feels worried or anxious about many different things, much of the time.
    • Atrial fibrillation A common heart rhythm disorder in which the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting, producing an irregular and often fast pulse.
    • Coeliac disease Coeliac disease is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system reacts to gluten and damages the lining of the small bowel.

    Medical tests

    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • Thyroid function tests TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together.
    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • Kidney function tests Creatinine and urea are waste products the kidneys should clear; how much builds up in blood shows how well they are filtering.
    • Liver function tests A panel of enzymes and proteins that together show whether liver cells are injured, whether bile is flowing.
    • CRP test A blood test for C-reactive protein, a protein the liver releases within hours of significant inflammation, infection or tissue injury.
    • Biopsy Removal of a small piece of tissue or a few cells so a pathologist can examine them under the microscope.
    • BNP and NT-proBNP test A blood test for the natriuretic peptides the heart's ventricles release when they are stretched by high pressure or extra volume.
    • Creatinine and eGFR The everyday measure of how well the kidneys filter.
    • ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) An old but still useful test that measures how far red blood cells settle in a narrow tube in one hour.

    Biomarkers

    • Albumin The most abundant protein in blood, made by the liver.
    • ALT (alanine aminotransferase) An enzyme concentrated in liver cells that leaks into blood when they are injured.
    • AST (aspartate aminotransferase) An enzyme found in liver cells but also in heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, brain and red cells.
    • Bilirubin The yellow pigment made when old red cells are broken down, processed by the liver and excreted in bile.
    • Natriuretic peptides (BNP and NT-proBNP) Hormones released by the heart's ventricles when their walls are stretched by high pressure or volume.
    • Creatinine A waste product of muscle metabolism produced at a steady rate and cleared almost entirely by the kidneys.
    • C-reactive protein (CRP) A protein the liver pours into blood within hours of any significant inflammation, infection or tissue injury.
    • eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) An estimate of how many millilitres of blood the kidneys filter each minute, calculated from creatinine together with age and sex.
    • Ferritin The protein that stores iron inside cells; the small amount in blood mirrors the body's iron reserve.
    • Free T4 (thyroxine) The unbound fraction of thyroxine, the main hormone the thyroid gland secretes.

    Imaging

    • PET-CT scan Positron emission tomography tracks a mildly radioactive sugar or tracer to show where cells are most active.

    Procedures

    • Blood transfusion Donated red cells, platelets or plasma are given into a vein, matched to the recipient's blood group and checked at every step, to treat anaemia.
    • Colonoscopy A flexible camera is passed around the whole large intestine after laxative bowel preparation to find and remove polyps.
    • Dialysis Dialysis takes over the kidneys' filtering work when they have failed.

    Medications

    • Bisoprolol A once-daily beta blocker that selectively slows the heart and lowers its workload, used for heart failure, angina.
    • Citalopram A selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor antidepressant used for depression and panic disorder, taken once daily.
    • Insulin The hormone that moves glucose from the blood into cells, given by injection or pump to everyone with type 1 diabetes and to many with type 2 when…
    • Levothyroxine Synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) that replaces what an underactive thyroid cannot make, restoring energy, weight and metabolism.
    • Metformin The first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it reduces the glucose the liver releases and improves the body's response to insulin.
    • Metoprolol and beta blockers A beta blocker that slows the heart and reduces its oxygen demand, used for angina, after heart attacks.
    • Prednisolone and steroids A corticosteroid tablet that suppresses inflammation and the immune system, used in asthma flare-ups, arthritis.
    • Sertraline and SSRIs A selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) antidepressant used for depression, anxiety disorders, panic and obsessive-compulsive disorder.
    • Testosterone The main androgen, given as a gel, patch, injection or implant to replace what the testes cannot make in confirmed hypogonadism and as the…
    • Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) Tirzepatide is a prescription medicine used to manage type 2 diabetes and to treat obesity.

    Guides and news

    • Anxiety: signs, treatment and getting help Anxiety becomes a health problem when worry is hard to control and affects daily life.
    • Getting active with a long-term condition UK guidance describes building up activity gradually from the current level.
    • Depression: signs and getting help Depression is more than feeling unhappy for a few days.
    • Fever in adults: causes and when to get help A high temperature in adults is usually a sign of infection and often settles at home.
    • Grief and bereavement: what to expect Grief is a natural response to loss, and the NHS says feelings usually ease gradually.
    • Heavy periods: causes, tests and treatment Heavy periods are common and often have no clear cause, but fibroids, endometriosis and other conditions can be behind them.
    • What is jet lag and what helps? Jet lag is a temporary sleep disturbance after long flights across time zones; the NHS says it usually improves within a few days.
    • Sleep problems: what helps and when to get help The NHS says insomnia usually improves by changing sleeping habits, and that a GP can arrange CBT when habits alone do not help.
    • Stress: how it shows up and where to get help Stress is a reaction to mental or emotional pressure that can affect mood, body and behaviour.
    • Tiredness and fatigue: causes and next steps Tiredness is common and often has an ordinary cause such as poor sleep, stress or illness.
