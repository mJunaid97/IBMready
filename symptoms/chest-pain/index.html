    Chest pain

    Also known as: chest tightness

    Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen. The chest holds the heart, lungs, oesophagus, great vessels, ribs and muscles, and pain from any of them can feel similar, which is why chest pain is assessed urgently until a heart or lung cause is excluded.

    Seek urgent care if

    • Central chest pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or spreading to the arm, jaw or back, especially with sweating, nausea or breathlessness: call emergency services
    • Chest pain with sudden breathlessness, coughing blood or a swollen painful leg
    • Tearing pain radiating to the back
    • Chest pain with fainting, a very fast or irregular pulse, or blue lips

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    Musculoskeletal painStrained chest muscles or inflamed rib cartilage (costochondritis): sharp, localised, worse on pressing or moving.
    Acid reflux and oesophageal spasmBurning behind the breastbone after meals or lying flat, often with acid taste.
    Anxiety and panicTightness with rapid breathing, tingling and a racing heart; a diagnosis made only after other causes are excluded.
    AnginaHeavy, squeezing pressure on exertion or stress, relieved by rest, from narrowed coronary arteries.
    Chest infection and pleurisySharp pain on breathing in, with cough or fever.

    Less common causes

    Heart attack (myocardial infarction)Crushing central pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back with sweating, nausea and breathlessness; may be silent in diabetes and the elderly.
    Pulmonary embolismSudden sharp pain with breathlessness, sometimes coughing blood, often with a swollen leg.
    Aortic dissectionTearing pain radiating to the back, often with very high blood pressure.
    PneumothoraxSudden one-sided pain and breathlessness, especially in tall young men or lung disease.
    PericarditisSharp pain eased by sitting forward, worse lying back.

    Often occurs with

    Shortness of breathNauseaDizzinessSwelling (oedema)Cough

    Conditions

    • Coronary artery disease Narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty plaque (atherosclerosis), limiting blood flow to the heart muscle.
    • Heart attack (myocardial infarction) Death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is suddenly blocked, usually by a clot forming on a ruptured plaque.
    • Pulmonary embolism Blockage of a pulmonary artery by a clot that has travelled from a vein, usually in the leg or pelvis.
    • Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease Reflux of stomach acid into the oesophagus often enough to cause symptoms or damage the lining.
    • Pneumonia Infection of the alveoli, which fill with fluid and inflammatory cells so that part of the lung cannot exchange gas.
    • Angina Angina is chest pain or discomfort that happens when the heart muscle does not get enough blood.
    • Asthma A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways causing reversible narrowing, with episodes of wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough.
    • Atrial fibrillation A common heart rhythm disorder in which the atria quiver chaotically instead of contracting, producing an irregular and often fast pulse.
    • Bronchitis Bronchitis is inflammation of the airways in the lungs, usually caused by an infection.
    • Lung cancer A malignant tumour arising from the lining of the airways or alveoli, divided into non-small-cell (about 85%) and small-cell types.

    Medical tests

    • Electrocardiogram Ten sticky electrodes on the chest and limbs record the heart's electrical activity from twelve angles over ten seconds.
    • Troponin test A blood test for cardiac troponin, a protein released when heart muscle cells are injured.
    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • Echocardiogram An ultrasound of the beating heart.
    • Ambulatory ECG monitoring A portable recorder worn for 24 hours to two weeks (or an implanted loop recorder for up to three years) that captures the heart's rhythm during…
    • Lipid profile Measures the fats carried in blood: total cholesterol, LDL ('bad', deposited in artery walls), HDL ('good'.
    • Spirometry A forced breath out into a tube measures how much air the lungs can move and how fast.
    • Cardiac stress test The heart is made to work hard, by treadmill or bicycle exercise or by a drug that mimics exercise, while its electrical activity.

    Biomarkers

    • LDL cholesterol The cholesterol carried by low-density lipoprotein, the particle that deposits cholesterol in artery walls and drives atherosclerosis.
    • Total cholesterol The sum of all the cholesterol carried in blood by lipoproteins: LDL, HDL and the remainder.
    • Cardiac troponin A protein of the contractile apparatus of heart muscle cells that leaks into blood when those cells are injured.

    Imaging

    • X-ray The oldest and most widely available imaging test: a brief beam of X-rays casts a shadow picture of bone, air and soft tissue, ideal for fractures.
    • CT scan A rotating X-ray tube and computer build cross-sectional slices of the body in seconds, showing bone, organs.
    • Fluoroscopy and angiography Continuous X-ray imaging shown as a live moving picture, used to watch swallowing.

    Procedures

    • Coronary angioplasty and stenting A balloon and stent passed through a catheter from the wrist or groin reopen a narrowed or blocked coronary artery.
    • Cardiac catheterisation Thin catheters passed from the wrist or groin to the heart measure chamber pressures, film the coronary arteries with contrast dye and.
    • Coronary artery bypass grafting Open-heart surgery that bypasses blocked coronary arteries with the patient's own chest-wall artery and leg vein or arm artery.
    • Gastroscopy A flexible camera passed through the mouth examines the oesophagus, stomach and duodenum, allowing biopsies and treatment.

    Medications

    • Apixaban and DOACs A direct oral anticoagulant that blocks clotting factor Xa, preventing strokes in atrial fibrillation and treating deep vein thrombosis and…
    • Aspirin A low-dose antiplatelet that stops platelets clumping into clots, used long term after heart attacks and strokes.
    • Atorvastatin A high-intensity statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by blocking its production in the liver.
    • Bisoprolol A once-daily beta blocker that selectively slows the heart and lowers its workload, used for heart failure, angina.
    • Clopidogrel An antiplatelet medicine that stops platelets clumping by blocking their P2Y12 receptor, taken to prevent clots after a heart attack, stent.
    • Combined oral contraceptive pill The combined pill is a hormonal contraceptive that contains two hormones, oestrogen and a progestogen.
    • Glyceryl trinitrate A fast-acting nitrate spray or tablet under the tongue that widens veins and coronary arteries to relieve an angina attack within minutes.
    • Metoprolol and beta blockers A beta blocker that slows the heart and reduces its oxygen demand, used for angina, after heart attacks.
    • Morphine and opioids The reference strong opioid for severe pain, such as after major surgery, injury or in advanced cancer; highly effective but sedating.
    • Omeprazole A proton pump inhibitor that switches off acid production in the stomach.

    Guides and news

    • Long flights and DVT: risk and how to lower it Long journeys can raise the chance of a leg blood clot (DVT); the NHS and TravelHealthPro describe moving, drinking water and sometimes stockings.
    • Chest pain: causes and when to get help Most chest pain does not come from the heart, but sudden pain that does not ease or spreads to the arm, jaw, neck or back needs a 999 call.
    • Which NHS service to use: 999, 111, GP, A&E The NHS says 999 is for life-threatening emergencies and NHS 111 for urgent problems or when unsure, with pharmacies.
    • ECG: what it records and what it can show An ECG records the heart's electrical activity through patches on the skin.
    • Heart attack, cardiac arrest and angina A heart attack is a blocked blood supply to the heart muscle, cardiac arrest is the heart stopping, and angina is chest pain that eases with rest.
    • Heartburn, acid reflux and GORD Heartburn is a burning feeling in the chest from acid reflux; when it keeps happening it is called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD).
    • Troponin blood test: what it shows A troponin test measures proteins released when heart muscle is damaged, and a raised result does not always mean a heart attack.
