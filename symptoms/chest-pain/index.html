    Chest pain

    Also known as: chest tightness, angina

    Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen. The chest holds the heart, lungs, oesophagus, great vessels, ribs and muscles, and pain from any of them can feel similar, which is why chest pain is assessed urgently until a heart or lung cause is excluded.

    What it is

    Pain or pressure anywhere between the neck and the upper abdomen. The chest holds the heart, lungs, oesophagus, great vessels, ribs and muscles, and pain from any of them can feel similar, which is why chest pain is assessed urgently until a heart or lung cause is excluded.

    Common causes

    Musculoskeletal painStrained chest muscles or inflamed rib cartilage (costochondritis): sharp, localised, worse on pressing or moving.
    Acid reflux and oesophageal spasmBurning behind the breastbone after meals or lying flat, often with acid taste.
    Anxiety and panicTightness with rapid breathing, tingling and a racing heart; a diagnosis made only after other causes are excluded.
    AnginaHeavy, squeezing pressure on exertion or stress, relieved by rest, from narrowed coronary arteries.
    Chest infection and pleurisySharp pain on breathing in, with cough or fever.

    Less common causes

    Heart attack (myocardial infarction)Crushing central pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back with sweating, nausea and breathlessness; may be silent in diabetes and the elderly.
    Pulmonary embolismSudden sharp pain with breathlessness, sometimes coughing blood, often with a swollen leg.
    Aortic dissectionTearing pain radiating to the back, often with very high blood pressure.
    PneumothoraxSudden one-sided pain and breathlessness, especially in tall young men or lung disease.
    PericarditisSharp pain eased by sitting forward, worse lying back.

    Often occurs with

    Shortness of breathNauseaDizzinessSwelling (oedema)Cough

    Seek urgent care if

    • Central chest pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or spreading to the arm, jaw or back, especially with sweating, nausea or breathlessness: call emergency services
    • Chest pain with sudden breathlessness, coughing blood or a swollen painful leg
    • Tearing pain radiating to the back
    • Chest pain with fainting, a very fast or irregular pulse, or blue lips
