    Fever

    Also known as: high temperature, pyrexia

    A body temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or more, produced when the hypothalamus raises its set point in response to inflammatory signals, most often from infection. Fever is a response, not a disease, and it helps the immune system; the cause and the person's overall condition matter more than the number.

    What it is

    A body temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or more, produced when the hypothalamus raises its set point in response to inflammatory signals, most often from infection. Fever is a response, not a disease, and it helps the immune system; the cause and the person's overall condition matter more than the number.

    Common causes

    Viral infectionsColds, flu, COVID-19, glandular fever; usually with sore throat, cough or aches.
    Bacterial infectionsTonsillitis, ear infection, chest infection, urinary infection, cellulitis, dental abscess.
    Gastroenteritis
    Vaccination reactionsMild, brief fever within a day or two.
    Childhood viral illnessesRoseola, hand-foot-and-mouth and many others.

    Less common causes

    SepsisInfection overwhelming the body: fast breathing, fast pulse, confusion, mottled skin, low urine output.
    MeningitisFever with headache, stiff neck, light sensitivity, a non-blanching rash.
    Pneumonia, pyelonephritis, endocarditis, abscessesDeep-seated bacterial infections.
    Tropical infectionsMalaria, dengue and typhoid after travel.
    Non-infectious causesInflammatory diseases, some cancers (lymphoma), drug reactions, heat stroke.

    Often occurs with

    HeadacheFatigueCoughVomitingRashJoint pain

    Seek urgent care if

    • Fever with a rash that does not fade when a glass is pressed on it, a stiff neck, or severe headache
    • Fever with fast breathing, chest pain, confusion, drowsiness or mottled cold skin
    • Fever in a baby under three months (38 °C or more) or under six months (39 °C or more)
    • Fever with a seizure, or in someone on chemotherapy or with a weakened immune system
    • Fever after travel to a malaria area
    • A temperature above 40 °C, or fever lasting more than five days
