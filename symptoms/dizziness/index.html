    Dizziness

    Also known as: light-headedness, vertigo, faintness

    An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint, usually a blood-flow problem), imbalance, and vague woozy feelings. Sorting out which one the person means is the first step.

    Open in the 3D explorerEar anatomy

    What it is

    An umbrella word for several sensations: vertigo (the room spins, an inner-ear or brain problem), light-headedness (about to faint, usually a blood-flow problem), imbalance, and vague woozy feelings. Sorting out which one the person means is the first step.

    Common causes

    Benign paroxysmal positional vertigoBrief spinning triggered by rolling over or looking up, from loose crystals in the inner ear.
    Vestibular neuritis and labyrinthitisDays of constant vertigo with nausea after a viral infection.
    Postural (orthostatic) hypotensionLight-headedness on standing, common with dehydration, blood pressure tablets and age.
    MigraineVertigo can occur with or without headache.
    Anxiety and hyperventilationFloating, unreal feelings with rapid breathing.
    Medication side effects and alcoholSedatives, blood pressure drugs and anticonvulsants are frequent culprits.

    Less common causes

    Stroke or TIA in the brainstem or cerebellumSudden vertigo with double vision, slurred speech, weakness or inability to walk.
    Heart rhythm problemsDizziness with palpitations or blackouts.
    Meniere's diseaseAttacks of vertigo with hearing loss, ringing and ear fullness.
    Anaemia, low blood sugar or heart valve diseaseFaintness on exertion.

    Often occurs with

    NauseaVomitingHeadacheWeaknessFatigue

    Seek urgent care if

    • Dizziness with new weakness, numbness, slurred speech, double vision or difficulty walking
    • Dizziness with chest pain, palpitations or a blackout
    • Dizziness with a severe headache or after a head injury
    • Vertigo with sudden hearing loss
