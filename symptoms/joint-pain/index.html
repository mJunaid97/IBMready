    Joint pain

    Also known as: arthralgia, sore joints

    Pain in one or more joints. One hot swollen joint, several symmetrical swollen joints, and aching joints without swelling are three different problems: infection or crystals, inflammatory arthritis, and wear or viral illness respectively.

    Seek urgent care if

    • A single hot, red, swollen joint with fever or inability to move it
    • Joint pain with a rash that does not fade under pressure
    • Joint pain after injury with deformity or inability to bear weight

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    OsteoarthritisWear of cartilage in knees, hips, hands and spine; pain with use, stiffness after rest.
    Injury and overuseSprains, tendon and bursa inflammation.
    Viral infectionsAching joints with flu, parvovirus, rubella, chikungunya and others.
    GoutSudden agonising red joint, classically the big toe.
    Rheumatoid arthritisSymmetrical swelling of small joints of hands and feet with morning stiffness over an hour.

    Less common causes

    Septic arthritisA single hot swollen joint with fever; an emergency.
    Psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritisInflammatory arthritis linked to psoriasis, the spine or a recent infection.
    Lupus and other connective tissue diseasesJoint pain with rash, mouth ulcers, fatigue.
    Lyme diseaseSwollen knee weeks after a tick bite.
    Haemophilia and leukaemiaJoint bleeding or bone pain in children.

    Conditions

    • Osteoarthritis The commonest joint disease: progressive loss of articular cartilage with changes in the underlying bone, causing pain and stiffness.
    • Rheumatoid arthritis A chronic autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the synovial lining of joints.
    • Gout An inflammatory arthritis caused by deposition of uric acid crystals in joints, triggered when blood urate is persistently high.
    • Bone fracture A break in the continuity of a bone, from a hairline crack to a shattered, displaced or open injury.
    • ACL injury A sprain or tear of the anterior cruciate ligament, the band inside the knee that stops the tibia sliding forward on the femur and controls rotation.
    • Gonorrhoea A bacterial sexually transmitted infection caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae that can affect the genitals, rectum, throat and eyes.
    • Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) Insufficient thyroid hormone, most often because the immune system destroys the thyroid gland, slowing the body's metabolism.
    • Inflammatory bowel disease Chronic inflammation of the gut driven by an abnormal immune response: ulcerative colitis affects the colon's lining continuously from the rectum.
    • Lupus A long-term autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks healthy tissue, most often causing joint aches, skin rashes and tiredness.
    • Menopause and perimenopause Menopause is when periods stop altogether, and perimenopause is the stage before this when periods change and symptoms begin.

    Medical tests

    • CRP test A blood test for C-reactive protein, a protein the liver releases within hours of significant inflammation, infection or tissue injury.
    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • Hormone tests Blood levels of hormones and related chemicals, interpreted against the time of day.
    • ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) An old but still useful test that measures how far red blood cells settle in a narrow tube in one hour.
    • Vitamin D test Measures 25-hydroxyvitamin D, the storage form the liver makes from vitamin D obtained from sunlight on skin and from food or supplements.

    Biomarkers

    • C-reactive protein (CRP) A protein the liver pours into blood within hours of any significant inflammation, infection or tissue injury.

    Imaging

    • X-ray The oldest and most widely available imaging test: a brief beam of X-rays casts a shadow picture of bone, air and soft tissue, ideal for fractures.
    • Ultrasound High-frequency sound waves bounced off tissue give real-time, radiation-free images, used for pregnancy, the abdomen, the heart (echocardiography).
    • MRI scan Magnetic resonance imaging uses a strong magnet and radio waves, not radiation, to produce detailed pictures of soft tissue: the brain, spinal cord.

    Procedures

    • Joint aspiration and injection A needle drawn into a swollen joint removes fluid for analysis and relief of pressure, and may be followed by a corticosteroid injection.

    Medications

    • Alendronate A weekly bisphosphonate that slows bone breakdown, strengthening bone in osteoporosis and cutting the risk of hip and spine fractures.
    • Allopurinol A xanthine oxidase inhibitor that lowers uric acid in the blood.
    • Diclofenac Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that eases pain and swelling in problems affecting joints, muscles and bones.
    • Hydroxychloroquine Hydroxychloroquine is a medicine used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, lupus and some skin conditions.
    • Ibuprofen A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) that eases pain, inflammation and fever by blocking prostaglandin production.
    • Methotrexate Methotrexate is an immunosuppressant used for inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis and Crohn's disease.
    • Naproxen Naproxen is a painkiller that also treats inflammation, used for conditions such as period pain, back pain, gout and arthritis.
    • Paracetamol The first-line painkiller and fever reducer for most people (acetaminophen in North America).
    • Prednisolone and steroids A corticosteroid tablet that suppresses inflammation and the immune system, used in asthma flare-ups, arthritis.
    • Tramadol An opioid painkiller for moderate to severe pain that also boosts serotonin and noradrenaline signalling.

    Guides and news

    • Joint pain: causes and when to see a GP Joint pain has many causes, including osteoarthritis, injury and gout, and the NHS advises an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 for a hot.
    • Knee pain: causes and when to get help Knee pain can often be treated at home, and NHS 111 is the route for a badly swollen knee or a hot, red knee with a high temperature.
    • Osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis Osteoarthritis makes joints painful and stiff; rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the joint lining.
