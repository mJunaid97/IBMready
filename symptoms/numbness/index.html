    Numbness

    Also known as: loss of sensation, hypoaesthesia

    Reduced or absent feeling in part of the body. Its distribution maps the fault: a glove or stocking pattern points to a general nerve disorder (neuropathy), a single nerve's territory to compression, one side of the body to the brain or spinal cord.

    Seek urgent care if

    • Sudden numbness on one side of the body, face droop or slurred speech: possible stroke, call emergency services
    • Numbness in the saddle area with difficulty passing urine or controlling bowels
    • Rapidly spreading numbness and weakness
    • Numbness after a neck or back injury

    In an emergency call 999 (112 elsewhere in Europe). For an urgent problem that is not an emergency, contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day.

    Common causes

    Pressure on a nerveSitting on a foot, sleeping on an arm: resolves in minutes.
    Carpal tunnel syndromeNumb thumb, index and middle fingers, worse at night, from median nerve compression at the wrist.
    Sciatica or a trapped nerve in the neckNumbness in a strip down the leg or arm with back or neck pain.
    Peripheral neuropathyGradual glove-and-stocking numbness from diabetes, alcohol, vitamin B12 deficiency or chemotherapy.
    Migraine auraTingling spreading up an arm to the face over minutes, followed by headache.
    Hyperventilation and anxietyNumbness around the mouth and fingertips.

    Less common causes

    Stroke or transient ischaemic attackSudden numbness of the face, arm or leg on one side.
    Multiple sclerosisEpisodes of numbness lasting days to weeks, often with vision or balance problems.
    Spinal cord compressionNumbness below a level on the trunk with weakness and bladder problems.
    Cauda equina syndromeNumbness in the saddle area with bladder or bowel disturbance.
    Guillain-Barre syndromeAscending numbness and weakness over days after an infection.

    Often occurs with

    Tingling (pins and needles)WeaknessBack painHeadacheDizziness

    Conditions

    • Stroke Sudden loss of brain function because blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blocked artery (ischaemic.
    • Herniated (slipped) disc Protrusion of the soft nucleus of an intervertebral disc through its fibrous outer ring, most often at the lower lumbar levels.
    • Type 2 diabetes A metabolic disease in which the body's cells respond poorly to insulin (insulin resistance) and the pancreas gradually fails to compensate.
    • Migraine A recurrent headache disorder with attacks of moderate to severe throbbing pain, typically one-sided, lasting 4 to 72 hours.
    • Anaemia A haemoglobin concentration below normal (roughly under 130 g/L in men and 120 g/L in women), reducing the blood's capacity to carry oxygen.
    • Fibromyalgia A long-term condition that causes pain all over the body.
    • Bone fracture A break in the continuity of a bone, from a hairline crack to a shattered, displaced or open injury.
    • Multiple sclerosis (MS) A lifelong condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks nerves in the brain and spinal cord.
    • Peripheral arterial disease A long-term condition in which the arteries that supply blood to the legs become narrowed or blocked.
    • Sciatica Pain, and sometimes tingling, numbness or weakness, in one leg when the sciatic nerve is irritated or compressed.

    Medical tests

    • Blood glucose The concentration of glucose in blood, measured from a vein or a fingerprick.
    • HbA1c The fraction of haemoglobin that has glucose stuck to it.
    • Full blood count The most commonly ordered blood test: an automated count of the red cells, white cells and platelets in a small sample.
    • Thyroid function tests TSH from the pituitary and the thyroid hormones T4 and T3 measured together.
    • Vitamin B12 and folate Two blood tests, usually ordered together, for the vitamins the bone marrow needs to make normal red cells and the nervous system needs to keep…

    Imaging

    • MRI scan Magnetic resonance imaging uses a strong magnet and radio waves, not radiation, to produce detailed pictures of soft tissue: the brain, spinal cord.
    • CT scan A rotating X-ray tube and computer build cross-sectional slices of the body in seconds, showing bone, organs.

    Medications

    • Insulin The hormone that moves glucose from the blood into cells, given by injection or pump to everyone with type 1 diabetes and to many with type 2 when…
    • Metformin The first-line tablet for type 2 diabetes: it reduces the glucose the liver releases and improves the body's response to insulin.

    Guides and news

    • Looking after your feet with diabetes Diabetes can reduce feeling in the feet and slow healing, so UK sources describe daily checks and an annual foot check.
    • Numbness and tingling: causes and when to act Pins and needles are usually brief and harmless, but numbness or tingling that lasts, keeps returning or comes with weakness needs checking.
    • Stroke and TIA: spotting the signs A stroke and a TIA are both emergencies: the NHS and the Stroke Association say to call 999 even if the symptoms pass.
    • Vitamin B12 and folate tests explained Blood tests for B12 and folate help diagnose deficiency, but a B12 test can miss cases, so symptoms matter too.
