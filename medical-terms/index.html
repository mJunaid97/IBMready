    Medical terminology

    The vocabulary that anatomy, physiology and clinical medicine are written in: directions, planes, movements, regions, structural words, word parts, the handful of disease processes behind most diagnoses, and the clinical terms that describe how illness behaves. Most terms link straight into the 3D atlas so you can see what the word points at, and to the pages that use them.

    Planes & sections

    The imaginary flat surfaces used to slice the body for description and imaging.

    Planes & sections

    Sagittal plane

    A vertical plane dividing the body into left and right parts; the midsagittal (median) plane passes exactly through the midline.

    A slice from front to back.

    Example
    A sagittal MRI of the spine shows the vertebrae in profile.
    Atlas
    See it in 3D ↗
    Used in
    MRI scanX-ray
    Planes & sections

    Coronal (frontal) plane

    A vertical plane dividing the body into front and back parts.

    A slice from side to side.

    Example
    Coronal CT images show both kidneys in one frame.
    Atlas
    See it in 3D ↗
    Used in
    MRI scanX-ray
    Planes & sections

    Transverse (axial) plane

    A horizontal plane dividing the body into upper and lower parts.

    A slice across, like cutting a loaf.

    Example
    Most CT scans are viewed as transverse slices.

    Cross-section

    A cut made at right angles to the long axis of a structure.

    Disease processes

    The handful of ways tissue goes wrong. Almost every diagnosis is one of these processes happening in a particular place.

    Disease processes

    Inflammation · say “in-fluh-MAY-shun”

    The body's protective response to injury or infection: blood vessels dilate and leak, and white cells move into the tissue.

    Red, hot, swollen, painful: the body fighting back.

    Example
    Appendicitis is inflammation of the appendix; the suffix -itis always means inflammation.
