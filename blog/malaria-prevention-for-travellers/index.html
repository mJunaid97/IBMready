    Malaria and travel: the ABCD approach, bite prevention and when to seek help

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    UK guidance sums up malaria prevention as ABCD: awareness of risk, bite avoidance, antimalarial tablets where needed, and prompt diagnosis.

    Key points

    • UK guidance describes an ABCD approach to malaria: awareness of risk, bite avoidance, chemoprophylaxis (antimalarial tablets) where appropriate, and prompt diagnosis.
    • Malaria is passed on by the bite of an infected mosquito, and the NHS lists DEET-based repellent and insecticide-treated nets among the ways to avoid bites.
    • The NHS says travellers heading to an area where malaria is found can get advice from a GP, nurse, pharmacist or travel clinic before they go.
    • TravelHealthPro says no antimalarial tablet is completely effective, but taking tablets together with bite avoidance gives substantial protection.
    • The NHS advises asking for an urgent GP appointment or getting help from NHS 111 if malaria symptoms follow travel, and UK guidance says malaria can appear up to a year after a trip.

    What is malaria and how is it caught?

    Malaria is an illness caused by a parasite. The NHS says it is spread by the bite of an infected mosquito.

    The NHS says symptoms usually appear between 7 and 18 days after a bite. Sometimes symptoms do not appear for months after travel, and rarely not for years.

    What does the ABCD approach mean?

    UK guidance describes an ABCD approach to preventing malaria in travellers. A stands for awareness of risk, which includes checking whether a destination carries malaria risk. B stands for bite avoidance.

    C stands for chemoprophylaxis, the medical term for antimalarial tablets taken to prevent infection where they are appropriate. D stands for diagnosis, meaning that anyone with symptoms gets medical help promptly.

    How can mosquito bites be avoided?

    The NHS advises using a DEET-based insect repellent on the skin and sleeping under an insecticide-treated mosquito net. UK guidance on bite avoidance also describes covering exposed skin.

    The NHS also says there is no scientific evidence that electric buzzers, savoury yeast extract, tea tree oil, bath oils, garlic or vitamin B protect against bites.

    Who gives advice about antimalarial tablets?

    The NHS says travellers heading to an area where malaria is found can get advice from a GP, nurse, pharmacist or travel clinic before they go. UK government guidance says to seek this advice in plenty of time, and that it is never too late to ask.

    The GOV.UK chemoprophylaxis guidance compares tablets. It reports that people taking mefloquine are more likely to report abnormal dreams, trouble sleeping, anxiety and low mood than those taking atovaquone-proguanil or doxycycline.

    Do the tablets give complete protection?

    TravelHealthPro says no antimalarial tablet is 100% effective, but taking tablets together with mosquito bite avoidance gives substantial protection. Its guidance for people visiting friends and relatives says tablets are started before travel and continued as directed.

    GOV.UK says people who have visited or lived in a country with malaria before do not have the same protection as local residents.

    What are the symptoms of malaria?

    The NHS lists a high temperature or feeling hot, cold and shivery, sweating, headaches, confusion, muscle pains, and yellow skin or whites of the eyes (jaundice). It also lists feeling very tired and sleepy, especially in children.

    The NHS says jaundice may be less noticeable on brown or black skin.

    Who should be contacted if symptoms appear after travel?

    The NHS advises asking for an urgent GP appointment or getting help from NHS 111 if there are symptoms after travel to a country where malaria is found. NHS 111 Wales applies this to travel in the last 12 months.

    UK guidance says malaria can develop up to a year after a trip. It advises telling the doctor about the travel. The NHS says anyone who travelled with the person should also get help straight away.
