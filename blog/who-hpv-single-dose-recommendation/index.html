    WHO immunization advisers recommend a single-dose HPV schedule for girls aged 9 to 14

    Health news Published 29 October 2026 · sources checked 3 November 2026

    On 29 October 2026 the WHO reported that its immunization advisers, meeting on 5 to 8 October, recommend a single-dose HPV vaccine schedule for girls aged 9 to 14.

    Key points

    • The WHO's Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE) met from 5 to 8 October 2026.
    • SAGE recommends HPV vaccination of girls aged 9 to 14 years with a single-dose schedule, which it describes as the most efficient immunization strategy for cervical cancer prevention.
    • SAGE says countries may consider routine single-dose vaccination of girls aged 4 to 8 years.

    What was announced

    A message from the director of the World Health Organization's immunization department, dated 29 October 2026, reports on the October meeting of its Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE), held from 5 to 8 October. SAGE recommends human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination of girls aged 9 to 14 years using a single-dose schedule, which it describes as the most efficient immunization strategy for cervical cancer prevention.

    According to the meeting highlights, countries may also consider routine single-dose vaccination of girls aged 4 to 8 years, and single-dose coverage could later extend to further target groups. The director's message adds that a single dose can be used for people up to age 30.

    What it means

    The 2022 SAGE position allowed one or two doses for the primary target group of girls aged 9 to 14; the October 2026 advice is for a single dose.

    Source Message by the Director of the Department of Immunization, Vaccines and Biologicals at WHO - October 2026 · World Health Organization · 29 October 2026
