    WHO: one in two people facing cataract blindness need access to surgery

    Health news Published 11 February 2026 · sources checked 3 November 2026

    On 11 February 2026 the WHO said that one in two people worldwide who need cataract surgery cannot get it, citing a new Lancet Global Health study.

    Key points

    • The WHO released the news item on 11 February 2026.
    • It reports a Lancet Global Health study showing that one in two people facing cataract-related blindness need access to surgery.
    • The WHO says the African Region has the biggest gap, with three in four people who need cataract surgery untreated.
    • The WHO describes cataract surgery as one of the most cost-effective medical interventions.

    What was announced

    On 11 February 2026 the World Health Organization (WHO) urged countries to expand access to cataract surgery. It cited a study in The Lancet Global Health showing that one in two people worldwide facing cataract-related blindness need access to surgery. The WHO says the gap is widest in the African Region, where three in four people who need cataract surgery remain untreated.

    What it means

    The WHO describes cataract surgery as one of the most cost-effective medical interventions. It notes that coverage of cataract surgery has increased by about 15% over the past twenty years, and recalls the 2021 World Health Assembly target to raise effective coverage by 30 percentage points by 2030.
