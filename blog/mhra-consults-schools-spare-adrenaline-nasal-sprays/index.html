    MHRA consults on school adrenaline sprays

    Health news Published 19 October 2026 · sources checked 3 November 2026

    The MHRA has opened a UK-wide consultation on letting schools buy and keep spare adrenaline nasal sprays for emergencies, alongside auto-injectors.

    Key points

    • The MHRA consultation was published on 19 October 2026 and closes on 14 December 2026.
    • It asks for views on changing the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 so schools can buy and hold spare adrenaline nasal sprays.
    • Schools could choose to hold auto-injectors, nasal sprays or both.
    • The existing safeguards would stay in place.

    What was announced

    The MHRA has opened a UK-wide consultation on proposals to let schools buy and keep spare adrenaline nasal sprays for the emergency treatment of anaphylaxis, a serious allergic reaction. Schools in England can already hold spare adrenaline auto-injectors, and the consultation asks for views on allowing them to hold nasal sprays too.

    Under the proposals, schools could choose to hold auto-injectors, nasal sprays or both, and either product could be used whichever device a pupil was prescribed. The consultation was published on 19 October 2026 and closes on 14 December 2026.

    What it means

    The MHRA says the existing safeguards would remain: emergency adrenaline could only be given to children known to be at risk of anaphylaxis, and by appropriately trained staff. School supplies would act as a backup and would not replace the expectation that children carry their own prescribed adrenaline medicine.

    The MHRA approved an adrenaline nasal spray in July 2025, the first needle-free emergency treatment for anaphylaxis in the UK. The site's food allergy page gives background on allergic reactions.
