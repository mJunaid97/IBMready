    Eating well with type 2 diabetes: what Diabetes UK, NICE and the NHS say

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    Diabetes UK says there is no special diet only for type 2 diabetes, and NICE advises the same healthy balanced eating as for everyone.

    Open in the 3D explorerPancreas anatomy

    Key points

    • Diabetes UK says there is no special diet exclusively for people with type 2 diabetes.
    • Diabetes UK says the total amount of carbohydrate eaten has the biggest effect on glucose levels after eating.
    • NICE sets an initial weight-loss goal of 5 to 10 per cent of body weight for adults with type 2 diabetes who are overweight.
    • NICE says nutrition advice should be individual and ongoing, from a healthcare professional with expertise in nutrition.

    Is there a special diet for type 2 diabetes?

    Diabetes UK says there is no such thing as a special diet exclusively for people with type 2 diabetes. Its advice is to choose healthier foods more often and to keep treats occasional. NICE tells clinicians to emphasise healthy balanced eating advice that applies to the general population when advising adults with type 2 diabetes. The NHS says changes to diet and activity can help control blood sugar.

    Diabetes UK advises avoiding foods labelled 'diabetic' or 'suitable for diabetics'. NICE also discourages the use of foods marketed specifically for people with diabetes.

    How do carbohydrates affect blood glucose?

    Diabetes UK says that even low-carb eating shouldn't be no-carb eating. It says the total amount of carbohydrate eaten has the biggest effect on glucose levels after eating. Its guidance favours higher-fibre wholegrain foods over refined carbohydrates such as white bread. NICE also says a limited amount of sucrose-containing food can replace other carbohydrate in the meal plan, provided total energy intake is not excessive.

    What about fruit, vegetables and protein?

    Diabetes UK says whole fruit is good for everyone, and that with diabetes it is no different. Diabetes UK's type 2 meal plan includes at least five portions of fruit and vegetables a day. Diabetes UK lists pulses such as kidney beans, chickpeas and lentils as protein sources, and suggests using beans and pulses to replace some or all of the meat in a dish. It recommends at least two portions of oily fish each week.

    One NHS trust leaflet (North Bristol) describes, in its weight-loss section, a plate with half vegetables or salad, a quarter starchy carbohydrate foods, and a quarter meat, fish, cheese, eggs or pulses.

    Where do weight and activity come in?

    For adults with type 2 diabetes who are overweight, NICE sets an initial body weight loss target of 5 to 10 per cent. NICE adds that smaller losses may still help and that larger losses over the longer term have an advantageous effect on metabolism. The NHS page on food and keeping active suggests at least two and a half hours of activity a week, with brisk walking, climbing stairs and more vigorous housework or gardening as examples. The same page describes a short-term low-calorie diet as linked to weight loss and lower blood sugar in some people, and says it is not safe or suitable for everyone with type 2 diabetes.

    Who gives personal advice?

    NICE says people should get individual and ongoing nutrition advice from a healthcare professional with specific expertise in nutrition, adapted to their needs and culture. The NHS notes that people taking medicines that can cause hypos are advised not to miss meals.
