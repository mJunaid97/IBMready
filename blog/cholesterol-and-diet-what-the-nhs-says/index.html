    High cholesterol: what diet can do and when medicine is considered

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    The NHS says cutting down on saturated fat can help lower cholesterol, and that medicines such as statins are considered when lifestyle changes are not enough.

    Open in the 3D explorerHeart anatomy

    Key points

    • High cholesterol means too much of a fatty substance called cholesterol in the blood, and it usually causes no symptoms.
    • The NHS advises cutting down on fatty food, especially saturated fat, and swapping to unsaturated fats.
    • Soluble fibre from foods such as oats, barley, beans and lentils is described as helping to lower cholesterol.
    • A blood test measures cholesterol, and an NHS Health Check in England may offer it.

    What is cholesterol and why does it matter?

    High cholesterol means having too much of a fatty substance called cholesterol in the blood. Too much cholesterol in the blood can build up in the artery walls and make them narrower over time. HDL is often called good cholesterol because it helps clear excess cholesterol from the blood, while LDL is often called bad cholesterol because it can build up in artery walls. High cholesterol usually causes no symptoms, which is why a blood test is used to find it.

    Who is more likely to have it?

    Causes and risk factors listed include eating a lot of food high in saturated fat and a family history of high cholesterol. It can also run in families, and the British Heart Foundation lists ethnicity, being male and having been through the menopause among the factors that raise risk.

    What does diet change?

    The NHS advises cutting down on fatty food, especially food containing saturated fat. The NHS suggests unsaturated fats instead, such as oily fish, nuts, seeds and olive or rapeseed oil, and lists meat pies, butter, cakes and coconut or palm oil among foods to limit. Soluble fibre from oats, barley, beans, pulses and lentils is described as helping to lower cholesterol. Plant sterols and stanols, added to some foods such as spreads, can also help lower cholesterol. The British Heart Foundation says cholesterol in food has a minimal impact on blood cholesterol levels, and that cutting down on saturated fat matters more.

    When are medicines considered?

    The NHS says medicine may be needed if cholesterol has not gone down after changes to diet and lifestyle, or if a person is at high risk of a heart attack or stroke. Statins are the most common medicine for high cholesterol, and the NHS says people usually need to take them for life.

    How is cholesterol tested?

    Testing is a blood test. In England it can be offered as part of an NHS Health Check for people aged 40 to 74 who do not already have cardiovascular disease. A GP may also suggest a test for someone with high blood pressure, diabetes, overweight, smoking or a family history of heart disease. Targets depend on factors such as age and other health conditions, so a clinician advises what applies to each person.
