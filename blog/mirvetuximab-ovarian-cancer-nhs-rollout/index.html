    NHS England begins offering mirvetuximab soravtansine for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer

    Health news Published 4 June 2026 · sources checked 3 November 2026

    NHS England has begun offering mirvetuximab soravtansine to hundreds of women with ovarian cancer that has stopped responding to platinum-based chemotherapy.

    Key points

    • NHS England announced on 4 June 2026 that mirvetuximab soravtansine would be available on the NHS from that day.
    • It follows a recommendation by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
    • NHS England describes it as the first new addition to NHS treatment for this type of cancer in over 20 years.

    What was announced

    NHS England says hundreds of women with hard-to-treat ovarian cancer can have mirvetuximab soravtansine on the NHS from 4 June 2026. Following the NICE recommendation, it is for epithelial ovarian, peritoneal or fallopian tube cancer that has become resistant to platinum-based chemotherapy and whose tumours contain the folate receptor alpha (FR alpha) protein that the drug targets.

    What it means

    The drug combines a homing antibody with a cancer-killing medicine. NHS England says it may have more tolerable side effects than traditional chemotherapy.
