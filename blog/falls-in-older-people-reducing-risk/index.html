    Falls in older people and reducing the risk

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    Strength and balance exercises and simple changes at home can lower the risk of falls in older people, according to the NHS. NICE recommends offering falls prevention exercise programmes.

    Open in the 3D explorerBrain anatomy

    Key points

    • The NHS says the risk of falling can be lowered by exercises for strength and balance and by simple changes at home.
    • NICE guideline NG249 recommends offering a falls prevention exercise programme.
    • Clearing clutter and trailing wires, using non-slip mats, keeping rooms and stairs well lit and wearing well-fitting footwear with good grip are among the home-safety suggestions.
    • Regular sight tests are recommended, and in England the NHS provides free sight tests for people aged 60 or over.

    Why do falls happen?

    Causes of falls include muscle weakness and balance changes with age, conditions that affect mobility or balance (such as arthritis, stroke or Parkinson's), low blood pressure, problems with eyesight or hearing, and dementia. Anyone can have a fall, but it is more likely in older people or those with mobility problems. Some medicines can affect balance and increase the chance of a fall. The NHS advises speaking to a GP or pharmacist if medicines might be affecting balance. Postural hypotension is a drop in blood pressure on standing up from lying or sitting, which can cause dizziness and may lead to a fall.

    What exercise helps?

    The NHS says regular exercises for strength and balance can improve strength and balance and reduce the risk of falls. The NHS notes there is evidence that regular tai chi can reduce the risk of falls. Programmes are described as best when tailored to the individual and overseen by a trained professional.

    What can be changed at home?

    Suggestions include clearing clutter and trailing wires, using non-slip mats, keeping stairs well lit and wearing well-fitting footwear with good grip. Regular sight tests are recommended, and free NHS sight tests are available to people aged 60 or over in England.

    What does NICE recommend?

    NICE guideline NG249 covers falls assessment and prevention in older people and in people aged 50 and over at higher risk, and recommends offering a falls prevention exercise programme.

    What can a GP or falls team do?

    A GP can check balance with simple tests, review medicines and refer to local falls services. Some areas have a falls team that offers a full assessment to older people who have fallen or are at high risk, and a GP surgery can say what is available locally.

    What does the NHS advise after a fall?

    Advice after a fall includes calling for help, using a lifeline alarm if there is one, and keeping warm by covering up with a rug, blanket or towel. Advice for someone who is not hurt includes getting up slowly, and changing position regularly while waiting for help if they cannot get up. The NHS says to call 999 if someone has fallen and may have injured their head, back, neck or hip, cannot get up, is confused, drowsy or vomiting, or has severe bleeding; NHS 111 can help if they may be in pain, injured or unwell, and a GP can be seen after any fall.
