    WHO progress assessment: integration, innovation and community leadership key to ending HIV, hepatitis, STIs

    Health news Published 27 July 2026 · sources checked 3 November 2026

    On 27 July 2026 the WHO published a progress assessment of its 2022 to 2030 strategies on HIV, viral hepatitis and sexually transmitted infections.

    Key points

    • The WHO published the assessment on 27 July 2026.
    • The WHO names integration of services into primary health care as one of the biggest opportunities to speed progress.
    • It points to long-acting prevention such as twice-yearly lenacapavir.

    What was announced

    On 27 July 2026 the World Health Organization (WHO) published a progress assessment of its Global Health Sector Strategies on HIV, viral hepatitis and sexually transmitted infections for 2022 to 2030. The WHO says integration is one of the biggest opportunities to speed progress, and that countries with the strongest results deliver HIV, hepatitis and STI care through primary health care.

    It also points to innovation, including long-acting prevention such as twice-yearly lenacapavir, and to stronger community leadership and community-generated data.

    What it means

    The WHO issued consolidated HIV service delivery guidelines (22 July 2026) and consolidated guidance on routine HIV surveillance (27 July 2026) in the same period. It says eliminating mother-to-child transmission of HIV, syphilis and hepatitis B remains a priority, with more countries applying for WHO validation.

    Source WHO: Integration, innovation and community leadership key to ending HIV, hepatitis and STIs · World Health Organization · 27 July 2026
