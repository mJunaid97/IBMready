    Eye tests: what happens and who is entitled to a free NHS sight test in England

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    The NHS says everyone should have regular eye tests, at least every 2 years, and lists who can have a free NHS sight test in England.

    Open in the 3D explorerEye anatomy

    Key points

    • An optometrist checks vision and the health of the eyes, and can spot conditions such as glaucoma and cataracts.
    • The NHS says glaucoma develops slowly and is usually picked up at routine eye tests.
    • People aged 60 or over, people with diabetes or glaucoma, and under-16s are among those who qualify for a free NHS sight test in England.
    • The NHS lists sudden intense eye pain with a red eye, nausea and blurred vision as needing emergency care: call 999 or go to A&E.

    What happens at an eye test?

    An optometrist or ophthalmic practitioner examines the inside and outside of the eyes. The aim is to check vision and eye health and to spot conditions such as glaucoma, macular degeneration and cataracts. An ophthalmic practitioner can prescribe glasses or contact lenses, and a dispensing optician fits them. If necessary, the optician refers the person to a GP or a hospital eye clinic for further tests.

    When glaucoma is suspected, the NHS says the first checks are quick and painless and include vision tests and measurements of the pressure inside the eye. If glaucoma is suspected, referral to an eye specialist called an ophthalmologist follows for further tests.

    Why have tests when vision seems fine?

    The NHS says glaucoma develops slowly over many years and is usually picked up during routine eye tests. It says everyone should have regular eye tests, at least every 2 years. People at higher risk of glaucoma, for example those with a parent, brother or sister who has it, may be advised to have tests more often.

    Who can have a free NHS sight test in England?

    The NHS lists people aged 60 or over, under-16s, and 16 to 18 year olds in full-time education among those who can have a free sight test. People registered as partially sighted or blind, and people diagnosed with diabetes or glaucoma, also qualify. The list also covers some people at risk of glaucoma, including some with a close relative who has been diagnosed; the age and other conditions are set out on the NHS page. The list also covers some people receiving certain benefits; the NHS page gives the details. Everyone else pays for a private test; the full list is on the NHS page. In Scotland the rules differ, and NHS inform says all NHS eye examinations are free for people who usually live in the UK.

    How often does the NHS pay for a test?

    The standard entitlement is a test every 2 years. An earlier NHS test can be arranged if the optometrist considers it clinically necessary. A person who wants tests more often than the optometrist considers necessary pays for a private test. Optical vouchers, which help with the cost of glasses or contact lenses, have their own eligibility rules separate from free sight tests; the NHS page sets out who qualifies.

    Which eye symptoms need urgent help?

    NHS trust information advises seeing an optician or GP about vision worries that are not sudden. Glaucoma can sometimes come on suddenly, with intense eye pain, a red eye, rainbow-coloured circles around lights, blurred vision, nausea or vomiting, and a headache. The NHS says these symptoms need emergency care, and directs people to call 999 or go to A&E.
