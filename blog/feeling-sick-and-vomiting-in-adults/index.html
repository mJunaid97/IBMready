    Feeling sick and vomiting in adults

    Guide Published 3 November 2026

    NHS inform says vomiting in adults usually lasts only 1 or 2 days, but blood in vomit means calling 999 or going to A&E, and vomiting for more than 2 days is a reason to call NHS 111.

    Open in the 3D explorerStomach anatomy

    Key points

    • The NHS says feeling sick is common and usually goes away on its own.
    • NHS inform lists signs of severe dehydration, including confusion, a rapid heartbeat, sunken eyes and passing little or no urine, among the reasons to call NHS 111.
    • NHS inform says to call 999 or go to A&E for vomit that contains blood or looks like coffee granules, or for green or yellow-green vomit.
    • The NHS advises seeing a GP if the sickness has not improved after a few days or keeps coming back, and calling NHS 111 for advice if a GP cannot be seen.

    What causes nausea and vomiting?

    The NHS says feeling sick is common and usually goes away on its own. Diarrhoea or vomiting can point to norovirus or food poisoning, and a headache with a high temperature can suggest an infection such as flu. Other causes include morning sickness in pregnancy, motion sickness, anxiety, alcohol, medicines and recent surgery. NHS inform calls gastroenteritis one of the most common causes of vomiting in adults, and also lists migraine, labyrinthitis, appendicitis and medicines such as antibiotics and opioid painkillers. NHS inform says norovirus usually clears up by itself within a few days, and that there is no specific treatment, so the illness has to run its course. NHS inform says antibiotics will not help norovirus because it is caused by a virus, and that small sips of water help to prevent dehydration.

    What can help at home?

    The NHS describes getting fresh air, sipping cold drinks, trying ginger or peppermint tea, and eating smaller, more frequent meals. It also lists hot, fried or greasy food, eating too quickly and lying down soon after eating as things to avoid. NHS inform advises resting at home and taking small sips of water or squash often so that dehydration does not develop.

    What are the signs of dehydration?

    NHS inform lists signs of severe dehydration, including confusion, a rapid heartbeat, sunken eyes and passing little or no urine, among the reasons to call NHS 111. NHS inform advises people with diabetes who have been vomiting a lot to call NHS 111, especially if they take insulin, because vomiting can affect blood sugar levels.

    Which symptoms need urgent help?

    NHS inform says to call 999 or go to A&E for vomit that contains blood or looks like coffee granules, or for green or yellow-green vomit. NHS inform advises calling NHS 111 for a person who keeps being sick and cannot keep fluids down, has been vomiting for more than 2 days, or has lost a lot of weight since becoming ill; its gastroenteritis page lists fever and bloody diarrhoea as reasons to contact NHS 111 or the GP practice urgently. The NHS says to call 999 if sudden sickness comes with tight or heavy chest pain, pain spreading to the arms, back, neck or jaw, or shortness of breath, because these could be signs of a heart attack.

    When should a GP be involved?

    The NHS advises seeing a GP if the sickness has not improved after a few days or keeps coming back, and calling NHS 111 for advice if a GP cannot be seen. The GP can look for the cause and may prescribe anti-sickness medicine. The NHS says a pregnant person who is being sick often and cannot keep food down should tell their midwife or doctor, or contact the hospital, as soon as possible.
