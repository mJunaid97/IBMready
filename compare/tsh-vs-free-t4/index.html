    TSH vs free T4

    TSH comes from the pituitary and free T4 from the thyroid, and they move in opposite directions. Reading them together tells whether a thyroid problem is mild or severe and whether it starts in the gland or in the pituitary.

    AspectTSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)Free T4 (thyroxine)
    SourcePituitary glandThyroid gland (as thyroxine, T4)
    Direction in an underactive thyroidRises, often to many times the upper limitFalls
    Direction in an overactive thyroidSuppressed towards zeroRises
    SensitivityVery high: changes exponentially with small changes in T4Changes more slowly and stays within range in mild (subclinical) disease
    Speed after a dose changeTakes about 6 weeks to settleChanges within days
    When it misleadsPituitary disease, the first trimester, severe illness, biotin supplementsBinding-protein changes are avoided by measuring the free fraction; heparin and biotin can interfere

    Where TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) fits

    Screening for thyroid disease and adjusting levothyroxine, because it is the most sensitive single measure

    Where Free T4 (thyroxine) fits

    Judging severity, following treatment in the first weeks, and in pituitary disease where TSH cannot be trusted
