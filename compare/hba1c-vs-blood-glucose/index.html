    HbA1c vs blood glucose

    Blood glucose is a snapshot of one moment; HbA1c is a three-month average. Both diagnose diabetes, but they answer different questions and each fails in different circumstances.

    AspectHbA1cBlood glucose
    What it measuresThe fraction of haemoglobin with glucose attached, reflecting average glucose over 2-3 monthsGlucose concentration in blood at the time of sampling
    PreparationNone; no fastingFasting for 8-12 hours for a fasting test; timed after a glucose drink for a tolerance test
    Diagnostic threshold (UK)48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or above, repeated unless symptomaticFasting 7.0 mmol/L or above, or 11.1 mmol/L two hours after a 75 g glucose load
    Day-to-day variationVery littleLarge: meals, exercise, stress and illness move it within hours
    Not reliable inAnaemia, haemolysis, transfusion, pregnancy, haemoglobin variants, kidney failure, suspected type 1 diabetesDelayed samples without fluoride, meter inaccuracy, acute stress
    Monitoring roleEvery 3-6 months for treatment targetsDaily self-monitoring on insulin; continuous glucose monitoring

    Where HbA1c fits

    Diagnosing and monitoring type 2 diabetes without fasting, and setting treatment targets

    Where Blood glucose fits

    Suspected type 1 diabetes, hypoglycaemia, pregnancy, acute illness, and any situation where red-cell lifespan is abnormal

    Educational content. A comparison explains what each test, marker or medicine is for and how they differ. It is educational and does not say which one a person should have; that decision belongs to the clinician who knows the person and the question.
